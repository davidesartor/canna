What is inside LisaFlow?

The flow network of every band × size, measured from the code: tensor shapes from jax.eval_shape, parameters from the module's leaves, FLOPs from XLA's cost analysis of each part and from a scan-aware walk of the jaxpr. Pick a config with the bar or by clicking a chart row.

How is the network wired?

Two token streams through the same MMDiT blocks. The data stream (blue) sees only the window and its frequency, and the frame registers (brown, one per f0-ordered source) read it alongside; no data or register token attends to the sources. Every block leaves its data and register keys and values in a cache, and the registers' final state goes through the frame head to the frame (μ, log σ): one encode per observation. The source stream (orange, in z = (x − μ) / σ) and the attention sinks (green) run decode per draw and per ODE step, attending jointly to their own tokens and the cached keys. Shapes are for the selected config.

What shape is each tensor?

Every stage of one example's forward (batching is the caller's vmap). The cache is in the trainer's precision; FLOPs elsewhere on this page are counted in float32.

Where do the parameters live?

Share of the parameters by group, for every config (bar length is the share, the label the total). Backbone parameters are split by the stream they act on; qk_gain counts with the sources, and frame_slot and the frame head with the registers.

What is one block made of?

Parameters and float32 FLOPs per forward of one middle block and of the last block, by stream and part. FLOPs are XLA's count of each part lowered alone; the attention core (scores, softmax, weighted sums, rotary turns, residual adds) is the block's total minus its projections, modulation and MLP. The last block drops the data stream's attention output, its MLP, and the sinks' updates: nothing reads them. The registers keep updating there, since the frame head reads them.

What does the module tree hold?

Every parameter leaf of the selected config, as eqx holds it. Stacked blocks carry a leading axis of num_blocks − 1. M: Muon takes it; the rest is AdamW.

How does the compute of a forward scale?

Matmul FLOPs of one encode (per observation) and one decode (per draw and network call), from the jaxpr walk; the log axis spans all ten configs. Hover for XLA's count.

What does sampling and training cost?

Matmul FLOPs from the jaxpr walk of sample_posterior (one observation with 1 and with 2 draws, so the per-draw and per-observation parts separate) and of the training objective and its gradient for one observation of draws samples. The midpoint sampler makes 2 network calls per ODE step, all vmapped over the draws. The draws share t, so c_mlp and every adaLN modulation run once per call for all draws: that part lands in the per-observation cost beside the encode, and a draw costs fewer than one decode per call.

What this means

  • The encode outweighs a decode more on the full band. One full-B encode costs 3.9 decodes (3.7 at XS, 4.0 at XL); on the narrow band it is 1.4 at B. In training the encode is 20% of a full-B forward and 8% of a narrow-B one.
  • The cache makes sampling cheap per draw. A full-B draw costs 21.4 GFLOP over 64 network calls, against 4.8 GFLOP once per observation; past a few draws the encode no longer matters.
  • The data embedding is one linear map. y_embed maps the 144 STFT numbers of a token to D: 0.3% of the full-B encode, 1.3% at XS.
  • The frame is nearly free. The 4 registers add 4 tokens to a stream of 32, and the frame head (frame_modulation, frame_unembed) is 0.11% of the full-B encode (0.31% narrow-B), yet the register stream holds 26% of B's parameters: it has its own projections, MLP and modulation in every block.
  • Parameters split four ways, compute does not. At B the data, register, source and sink streams hold 23%, 26%, 25% and 23% of the parameters (25.9% for the registers includes the frame head), so the 4 sink tokens carry as many weights as the 32 data tokens. Modulation is 37.5% of every block's weights but 1.9% of a full-B data block's FLOPs.
  • Matmuls are the compute. XLA's count of a lone middle block is within 0.9% of the matmul walk in every config, and the attention core (scores, softmax, weighted sums, residuals) is 2% of a full-B data block: the SwiGLU MLP (58%) and projections dominate.
