What do the learned loss weights do?

The trainer learns one log-variance λ per (time bin, coordinate group) and minimises err·e−λ + λ, where err is the group's mean squared velocity error in that bin of log10(1 − s). At equilibrium λ = log err, so every bin and group carries the same relative weight. This page follows λ over training and reads off what the final weights emphasise.

Has λ caught up with the error?

λ − log err per bin and group at the last epoch. Zero means equilibrium. A positive gap means the bin is weighted less than its error warrants. AdamW moves λ by at most about its learning rate per step; the tiles above give that bound next to the largest move seen.

What do the final weights emphasise?

The share of the objective's error term that each time bin contributes: draws in the bin × err × weight, summed over groups. The learned weights (solid) are compared with the plain mean-squared flow loss (dashed). Under the plain loss, late bins dominate because the velocity error grows as 1/(1 − s).

What this means

  • The learned weights remove the error's scale, not its timing. At equilibrium each (bin, group) term carries the same relative weight, so a bin's share of the gradient follows the share of draws that land in it, not the size of its error. Which times train the flow is then fixed by late_time_mix and late_time_floor.
  • λ is slow. It sits on AdamW, so it moves by at most about the AdamW learning rate per step. A run must be well past distance/lr steps before its λ reflect the error instead of the init.
  • Some trained times go unused by the default sampler. Draws later than the sampler's last query only matter if sampling uses more steps or a schedule that reaches them.
