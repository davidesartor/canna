Is drawing the training batch a bottleneck?

Timed on the 10-22 network. The numbers were measured on 2026-10-24 before the catalogue prior, unordered sources, the x1 head and the per-source frame; the scripts follow HEAD and were not rerun. At HEAD a training batch makes its base draws with a standard normal and x1 by the chart, so it costs about one jaxgb pass per observation (the observation's own), plus 64 single-source candidate passes per observation to place its window (sample_window). The 18-pass accounting below is the 10-22 one.

Each training step draws a fresh batch from the simulator (jaxgb in float64) on the same device as the network. The draw is timed alone, per device and batch, and inside the trainer's step at batch 256.

How long does a draw take, against the batch?

The simulator alone at the band's size B, one jitted vmapped call per batch, best of 5. As measured on the 10-22 network, a training batch ran 18 jaxgb passes per observation (16 base draws, each rescaled to its SNR, plus the observation's 2); a bare observation ran 2. With a training batch of 4 sources, the diamond is the same draw inside the trainer card's step and the horizontal line is that step.

How much of the trainer's step is the draw?

The draw's share of each config's step at batch 256 observations (the 10-22 default), bf16 backbone, the 10-22 network. The draw depends only on the band; the step grows with the size. Click a bar to pick its band and card.

What this means

  • The draw matters only for small full-band networks. On the A100 80 GB the full-band draw takes 41 ms: 11% of full-B's step and 31% of full-XS's (41% on the L40S). In the narrow band it is under 5% from size S up.
  • The base draws carried the cost (10-22 network only). 16 of a training observation's 18 jaxgb passes rescaled the base draws to their SNR, so a training batch of 256 cost 6.8× its bare observations for full-B on an A100. At HEAD the base draws are standard normal and carry no jaxgb pass, so this ratio no longer holds.
  • Keep the draw on the GPU. 8 CPU cores take 0.28 s for a narrow-B batch, 2.1× the A100 80 GB's whole narrow-B step, and 6.5 s for full-B, 17× its step.
