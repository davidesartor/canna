What has been measured?

Seconds per step at the selected batch, from the best source: c the step check (the 10-23 network), s the sweep (2026-10-22 network); ÷n marks n micro-batches. OOM: out of memory at every split tried; —: not measured.

What this means

  • A B-size network trains in about a day of steps on an A100 80 GB with the bf16 backbone, in either band. narrow-B: 650k steps × 0.136 s = 1.02 d; full-B: 240k × 0.372 s = 1.03 d. The L40S takes 1.16 d and 1.61 d. XL takes 5.2 d (narrow) and 4.5 d (full) on the A100, 7.6 d and 8.0 d on the L40S, so XL runs belong on A100 80 GB cards.
  • The bf16 backbone is not optional. fp32 is 24× slower than the bf16 backbone for narrow-B and 23× for full-B on the A100 80 GB, and casting only attention to bf16 recovers almost none of it: the dense layers, not attention, carry the cost.
  • Memory decides only at XL. At batch 256 everything up to L fits on both cards in one pass. full-XL on the L40S needs 2 micro-batches; full-XL on the A100 80 GB and narrow-XL on the L40S fit alone but are borderline in the trainer's own loop (see the training-overhead report).
  • On the full band the batch draw sets the memory. The trainer draws the whole batch before splitting it, so gradient accumulation cannot shrink the draw's plan.
