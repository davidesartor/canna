One galactic binary of LisaGB and how each of its parameters moves the signal LisaFlow reads. Each parameter block has its own coordinates, and its sliders follow them: moving one slider holds the others of every block. The effect card draws the signal with ghosts one step down and up the picked slider; the address bar keeps the state. Several sources and the noise in one window: the window viewer. Built 2026-10-30. Its port was checked under node against jaxgb's get_tdi and canna's preprocess, snr, stft_tokens, source_bins and physical_to_flow.

What must the flow predict, and which phase do the data carry?

The 8 flow coordinates of the source (physical_to_flow). f₀ is log-scaled onto this window's prior range, and log₁₀ ḟ is standardised by the prior's mean and std at f₀. The sky is galactic (l, b), each over its prior std. The orientation is the helicity amplitudes z± = A(1 ∓ sin ι)²/4 · ei(φ₀ ± 2ψ) in units of amplitude_unit(f) = √(S̄(f)/T), about SNR 1, each radius warped |z| → asinh|z|. The phases are taken at mid-observation (φ₀ − πf₀T − πḟT²/4). The signal is linear in z₊ and z₋, so each dial is drawn as strong as its share of |z₊| + |z₋|.

Does the rotary position alone favour the tokens around the source?

The source token sits at f₀'s bin (source_bins), and each data token at its centre bin, patch·n + (patch − 1)/2. Both are counted from the window start, on the same axis. The line shows what the rotation alone does to a score at each data token: the mean over the 32 frequency pairs of cos 2πΔ/period, with Δ the token's bin minus f₀'s. The periods are log-spaced from 2 bins to 2 × the window.

Do the φ₀ and ι takeaways hold at this source?

Each row changes this source and measures the relative change of its whitened window, max|Δỹ|/max|ỹ|. Face-on (sin ι = +1) the data carry only φ₋ = φ₀ − 2ψ, face-off (sin ι = −1) only φ₊ = φ₀ + 2ψ: turning ψ by 0.3 and φ₀ by ±0.6 holds one of them. The build checks the same at one fixed source per band and stops unless the ≈ 0 rows are below 10⁻⁹ and the others above 0.1.
