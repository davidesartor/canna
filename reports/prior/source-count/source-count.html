How many sources does a window hold?

N sources in the band, each independently in a window of w bins centred at f with probability p(f, w) = ∫window prior(f₀) P(SNR ≥ t | f₀) df₀, so a window holds K ~ Bin(N, p(f, w)) sources at SNR ≥ t: the prior thinned to the visible ones, prior(f₀ | SNR ≥ t) with N·P(SNR ≥ t) sources. t = 0 counts every source. Window centres follow the prior's f₀ marginal, as training draws them; this mixes the binomials. Each curve is P(K > k), the share of windows holding more than k sources, on a log scale down to 10⁻⁶, for one N; the catalogue the prior was fitted to has 9M. Past a mean of 500 a window's binomial is taken as normal.

Where are the windows that hold the most?

Per window centre, K's median and its 80, 98, 99.8 and 99.98% central intervals over the windows there, both bounds in one shade, and its mean N·p(f, w), at the catalogue's N = 9M. Every source counts most near the prior's f₀ peak; visible ones, a decade above it, where the SNR at a given A has grown faster than the prior has thinned.

What this means

  • A window holds hundreds of thousands of sources. With N = 9,000,000 and w = 512 bins (4·128, full-B), a median 172,651 and up to 345,032 in 99% of windows.
  • Most windows hold no visible source. 0.70% of the prior's sources reach SNR 7, a mean of 6.88 per window, but windows are centred where the prior is, mostly below where SNR 7 is reachable: 90.8% of windows hold none.
  • The rest can hold hundreds. 6.3% of windows hold more than the 4 source tokens; 99% hold at most 263, 99.9% at most 329 and 99.99% at most 352. The busiest centres, near 1.68 mHz, hold up to 365 in 99% of their windows, and 1.3% of windows are centred within 0.1 dex of them.
  • The count scales with the window. At SNR ≥ 7, 99.99% of windows hold at most 30 at w = 32, 53 at w = 64, 97 at w = 128, 183 at w = 256, 352 at w = 512, 685 at w = 1024, 1340 at w = 2048, 2649 at w = 4096.
