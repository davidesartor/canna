The LisaGB prior: response span

Autosizing gave every full-B source R = response_points = 1024 bins, the next power of two above response_span of the fastest chirp the sampler then allowed (ḟ cut 5σ above its mean) at the top of f₀, 12 mHz: a hard bound, 536 bins. This page asks instead for a fuzzy one per source: the smallest R that keeps at least a share x of its power above the noise inside its R bins, with probability y under the prior, ḟ uncut. Measured on 65,536 sources, f₀ log-uniform over the band and weighted back to the prior (9,325 effective sources above the noise), each against a 4,096-bin jaxgb reference; A enters linearly, so it is integrated out: the share of A above the noise in closed form, 16 stratified draws inside it. SNR cut

How much of a source's above-noise power does each R keep?

Each line is one R: the share y of the counted sources that keep at least a share x of their above-noise power inside their R bins. Both axes count nines, so 99% and 99.9% sit a decade apart; the last x is 100%, every bin above the noise. Only the first R that keeps everything is drawn; wider ones lie on it.

How many bins does a source need at each f₀?

Where the counted sources lie in f₀ and in the bins their whole above-noise power spans, with contours holding 50% / 90% / 99% of them. A source fits R when it lies below the R line. Below, how those sources and the prior's spread over f₀.

What this means

  • Keeping 99% of what the data see needs 8× fewer bins than the hard bound. R = 128 keeps 99% of the above-noise power for 99% of the sources, against the hard bound's 1024; 99.9% for 99.9% takes R = 128. full-B uses R = 128.
  • Keeping all of it costs little more. Every above-noise bin fits in R = 128 for 99% of the sources and 128 for 99.9%; 99.99% of them span at most 166 bins. Leakage below the noise does not count, so x = 100% is finite.
  • R follows f₀. Keeping 99% for 99% of the sources at each f₀ takes R = 32 for 0.862–1.77 mHz (51% of them); 64 for 1.77–4.61 mHz (46% of them); 128 for 4.61–7.43 mHz (2% of them); 256 for 7.43–12 mHz (1.8e-01% of them).
  • narrow-B's R = 32. Below its 1.26 mHz top (17.2% of the sources above the noise), R = 32 loses 0% of the above-noise power at the median, 0% for 99% of sources and 0% for 99.9%; keeping all of it for 99.9% of them takes 32.
  • Which sources count. 1.18% of the prior's sources have a bin above the noise, from SNR 1.6 up (median 8); 41% of them stay below SNR 7, and 0.70% of the prior's reach it.
  • A higher SNR cut widens the tail. Brighter sources lift more sidelobes above the noise. Per k, the counted share of the prior's sources, then R(99%, 99%) / R(100%, 99.9%): off: 1.18%, 128 / 128; k = 3: 1.18%, 128 / 128; k = 5: 1.02%, 128 / 128; k = 7: 0.70%, 128 / 128; k = 10: 0.43%, 128 / 256; k = 15: 0.23%, 128 / 256; k = 20: 0.14%, 128 / 256.
How it is measured

A bin's signal power is 2|h|²/(Sn·T/2) per A, E, T channel, in units where it sums to SNR², and the noise there has expected power 2; the above-noise power is the signal power past that, summed over bins and channels. Sources with none, which the data cannot see, never count: off counts the 1.18% of the prior's sources that have some.

SNR ≥ k counts only the sources that also reach SNR k. A source's SNR is linear in A, and its A draws are stratified, so the cut filters them without bias. LisaGB.visible_snr = k (default 7) centres training windows by about the prior's f₀ times P(SNR ≥ k | f₀), as the page weights it, toward high f₀ where sources are wider; the sources in those windows stay the catalogue's, and the fainter ones are narrower, so at each f₀ they need at most the R shown. The dashed curve is the catalogue's f₀ (sample_f).

R(x, y) is the next power of two above the y-quantile of the narrowest centred band, in bins of 1/T, holding a share x of the above-noise power. The tooltip's quantiles are per f₀ bin (20 log bins), left out where fewer than 1/(1 − y) effective sources lie above them. Widths come in even bins; the density spreads each over its step before a one-cell smoothing.

The hard bound is response_span at the ḟ ceiling the sampler used to cut at (5σ above the mean log ḟ), which autosizing read R off at the band's top; neither the draws here nor the sampler cut ḟ.
