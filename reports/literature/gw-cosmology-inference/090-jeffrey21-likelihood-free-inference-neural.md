# Likelihood-free inference with neural compression of DES SV weak lensing map statistics

Jeffrey, Alsing, Lanusse, 2021, MNRAS 501, 954 · [arXiv:2009.08459](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.08459)

**Evidence: B.** Peer-reviewed (Monthly notices of the Royal Astronomical Society); 96
citations in 6.1 yr (Semantic Scholar, 2026-10-19).

## What they do

The first real-data (DES SV, 139 deg²) likelihood-free weak-lensing analysis, inferring Ω_m and σ8. Pipeline: Kaiser–Squires mass map → summaries → neural compression to 2 numbers → pyDELFI NLE on p(t|θ) → grid posterior. Summaries tested: power spectrum (18 bands), peak counts (11 bins), both jointly, and a ResNet-18 operating directly on the map. Introduces VMIM to cosmology: the compressor and a conditional MAF (4 stages, 2×128) are trained jointly on −log q(θ|F(d)), and the MAF is then thrown away.

## What they find

Simulations: 74 L-PICOLA octants (74 cosmologies), each cut into 18 non-overlapping DES SV patches (256², 4.5′). Shape noise is generated by randomly re-assigning ellipticities among real galaxies. The augmented compressor training set uses 2500 noise/patch realisations per octant: 130k training and 55k validation samples for the dense compressor. Only the density-estimation step needs independent sims. Peaks + power is modestly tighter than power alone. Both CNN compressors (MSE and VMIM) give slightly tighter S8 marginals than the hand-crafted summaries and agree with the original DES SV 2pt analysis (S8 = 0.81 ± 0.06). MSE versus VMIM barely differs on this noisy data. Training the ResNet on a Colab TPU ran at more than 5500 examples/s, 110k steps in under 4 h. L1 versus L2 compression: L2 gave slightly tighter constraints on 4 mock observations, with "extremely small" differences.

## Implementation details

(1) Data augmentation for the compressor: non-independent sims (many noise/patch draws per expensive N-body run) are fine for training a compressor but not for the density estimator. (2) Run density estimation in decorrelated coordinates (Ω_m, S8 = σ8(Ω_m/0.3)^0.5) and Jacobian-correct the prior back. (3) NDE ensemble (2 MDNs with 2 and 3 components, 2 MAFs with 4 and 5 MADEs, tanh, stacked by loss); disagreement across the ensemble is used as a sims-sufficiency check and an active-learning signal. (4) Freeze the network weights before looking at the data, as a form of blinding against retraining until the result looks good. (5) Validate the simulator's summaries against a higher-resolution N-body run (MICE) with a χ² test against the data covariance (χ_ν ≈ 0.2 with data noise, 3.4 with only sim noise). (6) A poor compression inflates errors but does not bias the result, provided it is applied identically to sims and data.

## Caveats

Only 74 cosmologies. Ω_b, n_s and h fixed. No intrinsic alignment or photo-z nuisances. No coverage test. The MSE-versus-VMIM comparison is qualitative on real noisy data.
