The flow's posteriors are judged against Markov chains that sample the window likelihood of
\cref{eq:loglik} directly; training never sees them. This appendix says what the reference is, how far it
can be trusted and what it costs, in support of the width comparisons of \cref{app:results-mcmc}. Unlike a
global fit \citep{littenberg2020global}, the number of sources and the noise are known, so no move changes
the model.

\subsection{Sampler}
\label{app:mcmc-sampler}

Each eval window (\cref{app:evaluation-injections}) is sampled jointly, one ensemble over all $8S$
parameters of its sources, with responses of \PriorsNarrowR{} bins in the narrow band and \PriorsFullR{} in
the full band, in double precision. Each source moves in
$(\ln f_0, \ln\fdot, \ln\Amp, \alpha, \delta, \psi, \cos\iota, \phi_0)$ under the training prior
(\cref{app:priors}), with one extra cut, $\log_{10}\fdot$ at most \McmcFdotSigmas{} standard
deviations above its mean given $f_0$ (prior mass \num{\McmcFdotCutMass}). The sampler is a
parallel-tempered ensemble sampler with differential-evolution \citep{terbraak2006markov} and
stretch moves \citep{goodman2010ensemble,foreman2013emcee}, \McmcTemps{} temperatures up to
\McmcTempMax{} \citep{vousden2016dynamic} (\cref{tab:mcmc-settings}). The cold draws are relabelled to the
injected sources (\cref{eq:evaluation-matching}) and folded onto the window's branch of the
$(\psi,\phi_0)$ symmetries (\cref{eq:psi-phi-symmetry}) before any width is taken.

\subsection{Initialisation from a Fisher forecast}
\label{app:fisher}

The chains start from draws of a Gaussian centred at the truth $\theta^\star$, with precision
\begin{equation}
  \Gamma = \frac1{N_{\rm F}}\sum_{r=1}^{N_{\rm F}}\nabla^2_\theta\bigl[-\log L(\theta; d_r)\bigr]_{\theta^\star}
  + \Lambda,
  \qquad N_{\rm F} = \EvaluationFisherDraws,
  \label{eq:evaluation-fisher}
\end{equation}
the Hessian of \cref{eq:loglik} averaged over noise realisations $d_r$, which is the Fisher matrix
\citep{cutler1994gravitational,vallisneri2008use}, plus $\Lambda$, a Gaussian stand-in for the prior that keeps
poorly measured directions at the prior's width. It is drawn with $\log\fdot$ in place of $\fdot$, and each
eigenvalue is floored at the prior's precision along its eigenvector. Being unimodal and Gaussian, it misses
the exact symmetries of \cref{eq:psi-phi-symmetry} and the curved amplitude--inclination degeneracy, and where
the data barely constrain a parameter it returns the prior's width. It is not a stand-in for the posterior:
from \ac{snr} \McmcFisherMidSnr{} up its median width per parameter is within
\McmcFisherHighMedianLo--\McmcFisherHighMedianHi{} times the chains', but below \ac{snr}
\McmcFisherFaintSnr{} the chains spread a faint source's $f_0$ over much of the window, as the prior does, and
the forecast does not.

\subsection{Convergence}
\label{app:mcmc-convergence}

Over the six references the largest split $\hat R$ \citep{gelman1992inference} is
\ResultsMcmcRhatMax{} (\ResultsMcmcRhatNarrowMax{} on the narrow band) and the smallest \ac{ess}
\ResultsMcmcEssMin{}; the full-band chains sit above the usual $\hat R<1.01$ for their worst
parameter. Longer chains of the narrow band with four sources, \McmcConvSeeds{} seeds on
windows \McmcConvRuns, show that the loud sources are settled once read without labels, while
two things drift below $\snr=7$: sources under $\snr=3$ widen towards the prior, and sources at
$\snr=3$--7 are slowly lost. The reference is therefore sound at $\snr\ge7$, somewhat narrow for
invisible sources, and an upper bound on how often a source at $\snr=3$--7 is localised. For the
problems with the most parameters the faint sources stay near their Fisher start, which
\cref{app:results-mcmc} takes into account when reading the width ratios. On the verification binary of
\citet{strub2022bayesian} the sampler reproduces the published \rev{\ac{snr} (\cref{app:noise-validation}) and posterior}.

\subsection{Cost}
\label{app:mcmc-cost}

On \McmcJointCores{} \acs{cpu} cores a joint chain of \num{400000} iterations takes
\qtyrange{\McmcCostNarrowFourLo}{\McmcCostNarrowFourHi}{\hour} for a narrow-band window with four
sources, \qtyrange{\McmcCostNarrowEightLo}{\McmcCostNarrowEightHi}{\hour} with eight, and about
\qtyrange{\McmcCostFullSixteenLo}{\McmcCostFullSixteenHi}{\hour} for a full-band window with sixteen
(doubled from \num{200000}-iteration chains), against a fraction of a second for the flow
(\cref{app:fm-sampling}).
