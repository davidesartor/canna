subsections explain the batch, the optimiser, the schedule, the training loop and the cost.

\subsection{Batch}
\label{app:training-batch}

A batch holds \TrainingBatch{} $(Z_0, t)$ samples: \TrainingObservations{} simulated observations,
each with its own centre frequency $\fw$ and sources (\cref{app:priors}), and \TrainingDraws{} draws
of the base point and time per observation. The data stream of an observation is encoded once and
shared by its draws (\cref{app:network}), so the draws amortise both the simulation and the
encoding. The times are stratified over the whole batch, one per stratum of \TrainingBatch{} equal
strata (\cref{app:fm-clock}).

Every batch is simulated on the device inside the step, so no observation is seen twice. The
simulation takes \qty{\TrainingComputeNarrowBDrawPercent}{\percent} of a narrow-B step and
\qty{\TrainingComputeFullBDrawPercent}{\percent} of a full-B step on an A100, whereas on
\TrainingDrawCpuCores{} CPU cores the full-band batch alone takes \qty{\TrainingDrawCpuFullSeconds}{\second},
\TrainingDrawCpuOverStep{} times the whole GPU step. Simulating on the host is therefore not an
option. The gradient can be accumulated over equal micro-batches of whole observations, which
leaves the mean gradient unchanged; by default the batch is not split.

\subsection{Optimiser}
\label{app:training-optimiser}

The gradient of the loss (\cref{eq:loss}) is clipped to a global norm of \TrainingClipNorm{}. The
parameters are then split between Muon \citep{jordan2024muon} and AdamW, with the settings with
which \citet{liu2025muon} scale Muon to large language models.

\paragraph{Muon.}
For a weight matrix $W \in \R^{m \times n}$ (output by input) with clipped gradient $G$, Muon keeps
a momentum $M \leftarrow \upsilon M + (1-\upsilon)\, G$, takes the Nesterov look-ahead $N =
\upsilon M + (1-\upsilon)\, G$, and replaces it by an approximation $O$ of the nearest
semi-orthogonal matrix $UV\T$ (from $N = U\Sigma V\T$), computed with \TrainingNewtonSchulzWord{}
quintic Newton--Schulz iterations \citep{jordan2024muon}. The step is
\begin{equation}
  W \leftarrow W - \gamma_t \left(\TrainingMuonRms{}\sqrt{\max(m, n)}\; O + \varkappa W\right).
  \label{eq:training-muon}
\end{equation}
Its orthogonal part has an RMS of $\TrainingMuonRms{}\gamma_t$ for every shape, the scale at which
\citet{liu2025muon} match AdamW's update size, so one learning rate serves every matrix. Weight
matrices stacked over the backbone's blocks are orthogonalised one block at a time. The decoupled
decay $\varkappa$ applies only to the matrices Muon updates, and through $\gamma_t$ it ends with the
cooldown; Muon's updates have a fixed size whatever the gradient's, so without decay the weights
grow and its lead over AdamW fades on long runs \citep{liu2025muon}.

\paragraph{AdamW.}
Every other parameter is updated by AdamW, without decay: decaying the AdamW parameters of an
earlier version of the network shrank the head's output rows for one coordinate to a small fraction
of the others. The first moment is float32, because a bfloat16 one loses the small increments of a
smooth gradient.

\paragraph{Assignment.}
Muon takes the weight matrix of every linear layer in \TrainingMuonFieldCount{} modules of the
network (\cref{app:network}): the backbone, the two conditioning \acp{mlp}, the two Fourier-feature
\acp{mlp}, the output modulations of the flow and of the frame, and the hidden layer of the
endpoint head. AdamW takes the rest: the linear embeddings of the sources and of the data tokens,
the register and sink embeddings, the frame's output layer, the query gains, every bias and the
endpoint head's output layer. Muon holds \qty{\TrainingMuonShareXS}{\percent} of the parameters of
full-XS, \qty{\TrainingMuonShareB}{\percent} of full-B and \qty{\TrainingMuonShareXL}{\percent} of
full-XL.

\paragraph{Learning rates and stability.}
The peak rates were not tuned by a sweep on this network, and neither was the batch. Muon's matches
AdamW's because its update RMS does. At these rates five of the six runs of \cref{app:results}
trained to their end with every loss finite. Full-band $S=4$ did not: from a few epochs before,
isolated single steps sent the frame's loss and the gradient norm up by many decades while the
epoch medians stayed flat, and at epoch \ResultsFullFourDivergedEpoch, just before its cooldown, a
loss turned non-finite (\cref{app:results-runs}). Its continuation from an earlier snapshot with the
Student-t frame loss of \cref{app:fm-frame}, at the same rates and schedule, trained to its end with
every loss finite, and its largest single-step gradient norm was \num{\ResultsTFourMaxGrad},
against up to \num{\ResultsTFourGaussMaxGrad} in the Gaussian run's last epochs. Whether lower
rates avoid such spikes, or higher ones train faster, is open.

Muon \citep{jordan2024muon}, its decay and update scale \citep{liu2025muon} and the
warmup--stable--decay schedule \citep{hu2024minicpm,hagele2024scaling} are established on language
models; none was tested on flow matching, and none was ablated here.

\subsection{Schedule}
\label{app:training-schedules}

Both learning rates rise linearly from zero over the first \qty{\TrainingWarmupPercent}{\percent} of
the run, stay constant, and fall to zero over the last \qty{\TrainingCooldownPercent}{\percent} as
$\gamma_t = \gamma\,\bigl(1 - \sqrt{(t - T_c)/(T - T_c)}\bigr)$, with $T$ the run's total steps and
$T_c$ the cooldown's start. This is the warmup--stable--decay schedule \citep{hu2024minicpm} with
the $1-\sqrt{\cdot}$ cooldown that \citet{hagele2024scaling} find best; with a cooldown of a fifth
of the run, they report it matching a cosine schedule. Only the cooldown depends on $T$, so raising
$T$ before the cooldown starts extends the run as if it had been planned that long.

The step counts are a wall-time budget, not a convergence criterion. Each run of \cref{app:results}
was sized from the epoch time of a short smoke test of its band and source count to
\ResultsEpochs{} epochs, about \ResultsNarrowHours\,h on the narrow band and \ResultsFullHours\,h on
the full band of A100 time. A later cooldown generally ends lower, and some configurations are not
converged at the planned epoch (\cref{tab:results-forks}).

\subsection{Training Loop}
\label{app:training-loop}

\paragraph{Epochs.}
An epoch is \TrainingEpochSteps{} steps (\TrainingEpochsNarrow{} epochs on the narrow band,
\TrainingEpochsFull{} on the full band) compiled as one program: the simulator, the loss, the
gradient and the optimiser update of all its steps run in one loop on the device.

\paragraph{Eval-catalogue loss.}
After every epoch, the \EvaluationInjections{} eval injections with their noise
(\cref{app:evaluation-injections}) give the frame's and the flow's negative log-likelihoods, each
at the midpoints of \FlowMatchingLossBins{} equal steps in $s$. At the midpoints of
\FlowMatchingLossBins{} bins of $\log_{10}(1-s)$ down to $10^{\FlowMatchingLossDecades}$ they also
give, per bin and coordinate group, the predicted $\log_{10}\tau$ and the squared standardised
error $((Z_1-\hat Z_1)/\tau)^2$ (\cref{app:fm-loss}), and per bin of the source's \ac{snr} the
frame's $\log_{10}\sigma/\sigma_\pi$ and $\kappa^2$ (\cref{app:fm-frame}). The catalogue and its
draws of $Z_0$ come from a random stream that training never uses and are fixed for the run, so the
curves move only with the weights. Training stops before the checkpoint if any loss of the epoch is
not finite, so the last checkpoint is always finite.

\paragraph{Checkpoints.}
Every epoch saves, in the background while the next epoch trains, the weights, the optimisers'
moments, the random key and the loss histories, keeping only the latest. A second checkpoint keeps
the epoch with the lowest sum of the eval-catalogue frame and flow negative log-likelihoods, which
is the checkpoint used for evaluation (\cref{app:evaluation-weights}). Because the key is
checkpointed, a resumed run draws the same batches as an uninterrupted one.

\paragraph{Snapshots and cooldown forks.}
Every \ResultsSnapshotEvery{} epochs a run also keeps a snapshot of its whole state, which the
rolling checkpoint overwrites. A fork restarts from the snapshot at epoch $k$ with the total cut to
$k/(1-c)$ epochs, $c$ the cooldown fraction, so it cools down at once, over $ck/(1-c)$ epochs, as a
run planned that long from the start would have; the schedule before the cooldown depends only on
the warmup, which every fork shares with its parent. Forks from epochs \ResultsForkEpochs{} of each
run give, at the cost of the cooldowns alone, the loss and the posteriors of four shorter runs
(\cref{tab:results-forks}).

\subsection{Precision}
\label{app:training-precision}

Parameters and AdamW's moments are float32. The backbone runs in bfloat16, forward and backward,
from bfloat16 copies of its float32 weights, while the embeddings, the conditioning \acp{mlp} and
the heads stay in float32 (\cref{app:network-sizes}); Muon's momentum and orthogonalisation run in
bfloat16. The bfloat16 backbone makes a step \numrange{\TrainingBackboneSpeedupAMin}{\TrainingBackboneSpeedupAMax}
times faster than float32 on an A100, more for the larger sizes, whereas bfloat16 attention alone
gains at most \qty{\TrainingAttentionGainPercent}{\percent}: the dense layers carry the cost.

A float32 backbone is available, but it needs the GPU's matrix-multiplication autotuning switched
off and full float32 matrix products (no TF32). On an earlier version of the network, float32
programs compiled with autotuning gave silently wrong, over-confident posteriors on two GPU models,
while the same program without autotuning reproduced the CPU. This was not re-tested on the current
network; the bfloat16 backbone was not affected.

\subsection{Compute}
\label{app:training-compute}

\Cref{fig:training-compute} gives the measured cost of every band and size at the default batch on
an A100. The narrow band's step is the network's. The full band also simulates its observations at
many more frequency points (\cref{app:forward-model}), a fixed cost per batch that weighs most on
the small sizes and sets a memory floor, since the whole batch is simulated before it is split into
micro-batches. A size-B run takes about a day of steps on one A100 in either band:
\qty{\TrainingComputeNarrowBHours}{\hour} on the narrow band and \qty{\TrainingComputeFullBHours}{\hour} on the full band.
Every size fits on the A100 without splitting the batch, whereas full-XL does not fit the \qty{80}{\giga\byte} card
once the trainer's eval pass and checkpointing run beside it.
