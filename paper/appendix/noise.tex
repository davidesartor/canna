% =======================================================================================
\section{Noise, Likelihood and Signal-to-Noise Ratio}
\label{app:noise}
% =======================================================================================

This appendix gives the noise model, the likelihood it implies, the whitening that turns a
window into the network's input and the two \acp{snr} we quote, and ends with the checks of the
simulator against published references. The waveform and the \ac{tdi} response are in
\cref{app:forward-model}; the amplitude prior, set through the \ac{snr}, is in \cref{app:priors}.

\subsection{Instrument Noise}
\label{app:noise-psd}

The noise is the instrument noise of the \ac{lisa} science requirements \citep{babak2021lisa}: an
\ac{oms} displacement noise and a test-mass acceleration noise on each link (their eqs.~9 and 11),
\begin{align}
  P_{\rm oms}(f) &= \bigl(\qty{\NoiseOms}{\pico\metre}\bigr)^2
  \left[1+\Bigl(\frac{\qty{2}{\milli\hertz}}{f}\Bigr)^4\right]\unit{\per\hertz}, \nonumber\\
  P_{\rm acc}(f) &= \bigl(\qty{\NoiseAcc}{\femto\metre\per\second\squared}\bigr)^2
  \left[1+\Bigl(\frac{\qty{0.4}{\milli\hertz}}{f}\Bigr)^2\right]
  \left[1+\Bigl(\frac{f}{\qty{8}{\milli\hertz}}\Bigr)^4\right]\unit{\per\hertz}.
  \label{eq:noise-single-link}
\end{align}
The response is a fractional frequency shift, so the noise is expressed in the same unit: a
displacement enters with $(2\pi f/c)^2$ in power and an acceleration with a further
$(2\pi f)^{-4}$ (their eqs.~10 and 13),
\begin{equation}
  S_{\rm oms}(f) = \Bigl(\frac{2\pi f}{c}\Bigr)^2P_{\rm oms}(f),\qquad
  S_{\rm acc}(f) = \frac{P_{\rm acc}(f)}{(2\pi f)^2\,c^2}.
\end{equation}
For equal arms of light time $L = \qty{\NoiseArmSeconds}{\second}$
(\qty{\NoiseArmGm}{\giga\metre}), the same noise levels on every link and uncorrelated noises,
the \acs{tdi}~1.5 Michelson combination $X$ has the \ac{psd} of \citet[eq.~19]{babak2021lisa},
and $X$ and $Y$ have a cross spectral density $S_{XY}$. \Citet[eq.~129]{babak2021lisa} give
$S_{XY}$ for \acs{tdi}~2.0; for equal arms every \acs{tdi}-2.0 channel is its \acs{tdi}-1.5
counterpart filtered by the same factor, of power $4\sin^2 2x$, and dividing by it gives
\begin{equation}
  S_X = 16\sin^2x\,\bigl[S_{\rm oms} + (3+\cos2x)\,S_{\rm acc}\bigr],\qquad
  S_{XY} = -8\sin^2x\,\cos x\,\bigl[S_{\rm oms} + 4S_{\rm acc}\bigr],\qquad
  x = 2\pi fL .
\end{equation}
The channels $A$, $E$, $T$ diagonalise the $XYZ$ noise covariance, with $S_A = S_E = S_X - S_{XY}$
and $S_T = S_X + 2S_{XY}$ (their eqs.~127--128):
\begin{align}
  S_A(f) = S_E(f) &= 8\sin^2x\,\bigl[(2+\cos x)\,S_{\rm oms}
  + 4\,(1 + \cos x + \cos^2 x)\,S_{\rm acc}\bigr], \nonumber\\
  S_T(f) &= 16\sin^2x\,(1-\cos x)\,\bigl[S_{\rm oms} + 2\,(1-\cos x)\,S_{\rm acc}\bigr].
  \label{eq:noise-psd}
\end{align}
These one-sided \acp{psd}, dimensionless per hertz, are what the simulator draws noise from, what
the likelihood weights by and what the network's input is whitened with (\cref{fig:noise-psd}a).
The band ends well below the transfer frequency $1/(2\pi L) = \qty{\NoiseTransfer}{\milli\hertz}$
($x = \NoiseXTop{}$ at \qty{\NoiseBandHigh}{\milli\hertz}), so no zero of $\sin x$ falls inside
it and every $S_c$ is strictly positive. $T$ is far quieter than $A$ and $E$ at low frequency,
$S_T/S_A = \num{\NoiseTOverALow}$ at \qty{\NoiseBandLow}{\milli\hertz} and \num{\NoiseTOverAHigh}
at \qty{\NoiseBandHigh}{\milli\hertz}, but its signal response is suppressed with it, so it
carries almost no \ac{snr} in our band (\cref{app:noise-validation}); the likelihood and the
network input keep all three channels.

The unit matters more than it looks. Writing the noise as a fractional arm length instead,
dividing by $L$ rather than by $c/2\pi f$, changes every $S_c$ by $4x^2$: a tilt across the band
rather than a constant, which would re-weight low- against high-frequency sources without any
visible failure. \Cref{app:noise-validation} checks the unit directly.

The noise is instrumental only: no Galactic confusion foreground (the $S_c$ term of
\citealt[eq.~1]{robson2019construction}; \citealt{karnesis2021characterization}), no gaps or
glitches, and the \acs{psd} is stationary, known and the same for every window.

\subsection{Data Model and Likelihood}
\label{app:noise-likelihood}

The observation lasts $\Tobs = \qty{\NoiseTobsYears}{\year}$ and sources have $f_0$ in
\qtyrange{\NoiseBandLow}{\NoiseNarrowHigh}{\milli\hertz} (narrow band) or
\qtyrange{\NoiseBandLow}{\NoiseBandHigh}{\milli\hertz} (full band). A window is $K$ consecutive
Fourier bins $f_k = (k_0 + k)/\Tobs$, $k = 0,\dots,K-1$, with $K = \NoiseKNarrow{}$ in the
narrow band and \NoiseKFull{} in the full band (\cref{app:forward-model-windows}). The data are
the sum of the \NoiseSourcesWord{} source responses and Gaussian noise,
\begin{equation}
  \tilde d_{k,c} = \sum_{s=1}^{S}\tilde h_c(f_k;\theta_s) + \tilde n_{k,c},\qquad
  \tilde n_{k,c} \sim \CN\!\bigl(0,\ P_c(f_k)\bigr),\qquad
  P_c(f) = \tfrac12\,S_c(f)\,\Tobs,
  \label{eq:noise-data}
\end{equation}
independent across bins $k$ and channels $c\in\{A,E,T\}$; the real and imaginary parts each
have variance $P_c/2$. $P_c$ is evaluated at each bin's own frequency, so the noise level
changes across a window as it does across the band.

\paragraph{Normalisation.}
$\tilde h_c$ is the continuous Fourier transform $\int h_c(t)\,e^{-2\pi\ii ft}\,\dd t$, as JaxGB
returns it \citep{jaxgb}. For stationary noise
$\E[\tilde n(f)\,\tilde n^*(f')] = \tfrac12 S(f)\,\delta(f-f')$, and over a finite observation
$\delta(0)$ becomes $\Tobs$, which gives the variance $P_c$ above. The sampling interval appears
nowhere: a discrete transform would carry it in signal and noise alike, and it would cancel from
every \ac{snr} and likelihood ratio. An \ac{snr} that changes with the sampling interval is
therefore a unit error, which is how we test it.

With the noise-weighted inner product, the discrete form of $4\,\mathrm{Re}\int a\,b^*/S\,\dd f$
with $\dd f = 1/\Tobs$ \citep{cutler1994gravitational},
\begin{equation}
  \inner{a}{b} = 4\,\mathrm{Re}\sum_{c}\sum_{k}\frac{a_{k,c}\,b^*_{k,c}}{S_c(f_k)\,\Tobs}
  = 2\,\mathrm{Re}\sum_{c}\sum_{k}\frac{a_{k,c}\,b^*_{k,c}}{P_c(f_k)},
  \label{eq:inner}
\end{equation}
the log-likelihood of a window is the Whittle likelihood of \cref{eq:noise-data},
\begin{equation}
  \log p(d\mid\theta_{1:S}) = -\tfrac12\inner{d-h}{d-h} + \text{const}
  = -\sum_{k,c}\frac{\bigl|\tilde d_{k,c}-\tilde h_{k,c}(\theta_{1:S})\bigr|^2}{P_c(f_k)}
  + \text{const},\qquad
  \tilde h_{k,c}(\theta_{1:S}) = \sum_{s}\tilde h_c(f_k;\theta_s).
  \label{eq:loglik}
\end{equation}
The data are simulated from exactly this model, so the likelihood is exact for them: the noise
the data carry is the noise the posterior assumes. It is the likelihood of the window alone and
knows nothing of sources outside it (\cref{app:priors}).

Training never evaluates \cref{eq:loglik}: the flow learns from simulated pairs, and its
posteriors come from the flow alone. The likelihood defines the posterior the flow must
reproduce, and only the references we compare against use it: the Fisher matrix
$F_{ij} = \inner{\partial_i h}{\partial_j h}$, its expected negative Hessian
\citep{vallisneri2008use}, in \cref{app:evaluation}, and the \ac{mcmc} baseline in
\cref{app:mcmc}.

\paragraph{Whitening.}
The network sees the window divided by the noise standard deviation of each real and imaginary
part,
\begin{equation}
  y_{k} = \Bigl[\mathrm{Re}\,\frac{\tilde d_{k,c}}{\sqrt{P_c(f_k)/2}},\
  \mathrm{Im}\,\frac{\tilde d_{k,c}}{\sqrt{P_c(f_k)/2}}\Bigr]_{c=A,E,T} \in \R^{6},\qquad
  y \in \R^{K\times 6},
  \label{eq:noise-whiten}
\end{equation}
that is, \num{\NoiseInputsNarrow} numbers in the narrow band and \num{\NoiseInputsFull} in the full band.
With $\hat h(\theta_{1:S})$ the clean signal whitened the same way, \cref{eq:noise-data} becomes
$y = \hat h + \varepsilon$ with $\varepsilon\sim\Normal(0, I)$, and \cref{eq:loglik} becomes
$-\tfrac12\lVert y-\hat h\rVert^2$. We whiten for three reasons. The map is fixed and
invertible, so nothing is lost. The noise has unit variance in every input wherever the window
sits, although $S_c$ changes by orders of magnitude across the band (\cref{fig:noise-psd}a), so
one network serves the whole band. And a source's whitened energy is its \ac{snr},
$\lVert\hat h(\theta_s)\rVert^2 = \snr_s^2$, so the input scale is set by the \acs{snr} prior and
not by the frequency (\cref{fig:forward-model-window}). How $y$ is cut into tokens is in
\cref{app:network}.

\subsection{Signal-to-Noise Ratio}
\label{app:noise-snr}

The optimal matched-filter \ac{snr} of a signal $h$ is $\snr = \sqrt{\inner{h}{h}}$; for
noiseless data $d = h$, $\snr^2$ is twice the log-likelihood gain of the true sources over no
source. We quote two: that of one source's response alone and that of all a window's sources
together,
\begin{equation}
  \snr_s^2 = \inner{h(\theta_s)}{h(\theta_s)},\qquad
  \snr_w^2 = \Bigl\langle\textstyle\sum_s h(\theta_s)\,\Big|\,\sum_s h(\theta_s)\Bigr\rangle
  = \sum_s\snr_s^2 + 2\sum_{s<s'}\inner{h(\theta_s)}{h(\theta_{s'})}.
  \label{eq:noise-snr}
\end{equation}
The cross terms vanish only when the responses do not overlap, so $\snr_w$ is not the
quadrature sum of the $\snr_s$ when sources share bins. Both sum over the three channels. The
per-source $\snr_s$ says how far a source stands above the noise, and under the catalogue prior
most stand far below it (\cref{app:priors}); the window's $\snr_w$ selects the evaluation injections (\cref{app:evaluation}).

\paragraph{Sky-averaged sensitivity.}
The chart measures amplitudes in a unit that depends only on the window,
$u(\fw) = \sqrt{S_n(\fw)/\Tobs}$ (\cref{app:chart}), with $S_n$ the sky-averaged sensitivity of
\citet[eq.~1]{robson2019construction} without the confusion term,
\begin{equation}
  S_n(f) = \frac{10}{3\,(cL)^2}\left[P_{\rm oms} + \frac{4P_{\rm acc}}{(2\pi f)^4}\right]
  \left[1+0.6\Bigl(\frac{f}{f_\star}\Bigr)^2\right],\qquad f_\star = \frac{1}{2\pi L}.
  \label{eq:noise-sky}
\end{equation}
Their eq.~13 carries $2\bigl(1+\cos^2(f/f_\star)\bigr)$ in place of the 4; over
\qtyrange{\NoiseBandLow}{\NoiseBandHigh}{\milli\hertz} the two differ by at most
\qty{\NoiseRobsonPower}{\percent} in $S_n$ (\qty{\NoiseRobsonAmplitude}{\percent} in amplitude),
because the acceleration term is negligible where they differ. $S_n$ enters nothing but this
unit: the likelihood, the \ac{snr} and the prior all use the full response and
\cref{eq:noise-psd}. For a monochromatic source averaged over sky, polarisation, inclination and
phase, $\E[\snr^2] = C\,\Amp^2\Tobs/S_n(f)$. Rather than take $C$ from a paper with other
normalisation conventions, we measure it with our own $\snr$ on \num{\NoiseCSources} sources
(\cref{fig:noise-psd}b): $C = \num{\NoiseC \pm \NoiseCError}$ for an isotropic sky
(\num{\NoiseCGalactic} for the Galactic sky prior), constant to \qty{\NoiseCFlat}{\percent} below
\qty{\NoiseCFlatBelow}{\milli\hertz} and \qty{\NoiseCRise}{\percent} higher at
\qty{\NoiseCTop}{\milli\hertz}. The unit $u$ is thus the amplitude of a source at a sky-averaged
\ac{snr} of $\sqrt C\approx\NoiseSqrtC{}$; the chart needs a scale that tracks the noise across
the band, not an exact \acs{snr}.

\subsection{Validation of the Forward Model}
\label{app:noise-validation}

We checked the simulator and the likelihood against independent expressions, against their own
statistics and against a published analysis.

\paragraph{Noise.}
The \acp{psd} of \cref{eq:noise-psd} equal $S_X - S_{XY}$ and $S_X + 2S_{XY}$ built from eqs.~19
and 129 of \citet{babak2021lisa} to a relative difference of at most \num{\NoisePsdGap} over the
full band. The generator draws noise with these statistics (\cref{tab:noise-generator}): over
\num{\NoiseGeneratorWindows} pure-noise windows of \num{\NoiseGeneratorBins} bins, every moment
matches to within the Monte-Carlo floor, and $\inner{n}{n}$ over $A$ and $E$ follows
$\chi^2_{\NoiseGeneratorDof{}}$ (Kolmogorov--Smirnov $p = \NoiseGeneratorKs{}$).

\paragraph{Units.}
The sky-averaged sensitivity of the simulator,
$S_{\rm sky} = \bigl(\sum_{c\in\{A,E\}} R_c/S_c\bigr)^{-1}$ with $R_c$ the response power of
unit-amplitude sources averaged over \num{\NoiseSkyDraws} isotropic sky positions, is flat
against \cref{eq:noise-sky} with Robson's $\cos^2$ factor (\cref{fig:forward-model-checks}b):
log-log slope $\NoiseSkySlope{}$ over \qtyrange{\NoiseSkyFitLow}{\NoiseSkyFitTop}{\milli\hertz}
and ratio \numrange{\NoiseSkyRatioLow}{\NoiseSkyRatioHigh} below
\qty{\NoiseSkyRatioBelow}{\milli\hertz}. The expected ratio is $1/2$, since $A$ and $E$ together
are two Michelson-equivalent channels and the analytic curve describes one
\citep[eq.~43]{babak2021lisa}. In fractional length the same ratio falls with slope
$\NoiseSkySlopeLength{}$.

\paragraph{\acs{tdi} convention.}
Our \acs{tdi}-1.5 $X$ (\cref{eq:forward-model-tdi-x}) agrees with the standard equal-arm $X$ of
\citet[eq.~14]{babak2021lisa}, built from the same link responses for \num{\NoiseTdiSources}
prior sources, to a relative difference of at most \num{\NoiseTdiLow} up to
\qty{\NoiseTdiLowTop}{\milli\hertz} and \num{\NoiseTdiHigh} (median; at most
\num{\NoiseTdiHighMax}) over \qtyrange{\NoiseTdiHighLo}{\NoiseTdiHighHi}{\milli\hertz}. The
difference grows as $f_0^{\NoiseTdiSlope{}}$, the higher-order term of
\cref{app:forward-model-tdi}, and the signal powers agree to
\qty{\NoiseTdiPowerMax}{\percent} over the band (\cref{fig:forward-model-checks}a).

\paragraph{A published source.}
For the faint verification binary of \citet[Table~VII]{strub2022bayesian}
($f_0 = \qty{\NoiseStrubFzeroMhz}{\milli\hertz}$, $\log_{10}\fdot = \NoiseStrubLogFdot{}$,
$\Amp = \num{\NoiseStrubAmp}$, ecliptic sky $(\NoiseStrubLon{}, \NoiseStrubLat{})$ rotated to the
equatorial frame of the orbits, $\iota = \NoiseStrubTruthIota{}$, $\psi = \NoiseStrubPsi{}$,
$\phi_0 = \NoiseStrubPhi{}$, $\Tobs = \qty{\NoiseTobsYears}{\year}$) our $\snr$ is
\NoiseStrubSnr{}, against their \NoiseStrubSnrTheirs{}, \qty{\NoiseStrubSnrGap}{\percent} apart
(\cref{tab:noise-strub}). It is converged in the window: from $R = \NoiseStrubRLow{}$ to
\NoiseStrubRHigh{} response points it moves by at most \num{\NoiseStrubWindowSpread}, and
dropping $T$ moves it by \num{\NoiseStrubNoT}. A sky left in ecliptic coordinates gives
\NoiseStrubSnrUnrotated{}, the size of a frame error.

The reference \ac{mcmc} of \cref{app:mcmc}, run on \cref{eq:loglik} under Strub et al.'s priors
(isotropic sky, log-uniform $\fdot$ and $\Amp$), reproduces the features they report
(\cref{fig:strub-source}): an $f_0$ width comparable to theirs
($\sigma_{f_0} = \qty{\NoiseStrubSigmaFzero}{\nano\hertz}$), an $f_0$--$\log\fdot$ correlation
(\num{\NoiseStrubCorrFzeroFdot}), a prior-limited $\fdot$ (posterior width \NoiseStrubFdotPrior{} of
the prior's) and the $\cos\iota$--$\log\Amp$ anticorrelation (\num{\NoiseStrubCorrCosIotaAmp}). Our
$\cos\iota$ interval is wider than theirs. Restricting the prior to a box about the maximum
(\NoiseStrubBoxSigmas{} Fisher standard deviations in sky and inclination,
\NoiseStrubBoxFzeroSigmas{} in $f_0$, $\psi$ and $\phi_0$ pinned) cuts the face-on tail and
narrows it most of the way to theirs, which suggests their interval reflects a narrower
effective prior rather than a different likelihood; the box's $f_0$ interval is set by the box
itself. Over \NoiseStrubRealisations{} noise realisations the gain from the observed to the
maximum-likelihood \acs{snr}, a test of the likelihood's curvature, is
\num{\NoiseStrubGain \pm \NoiseStrubGainError}, with a scatter of \NoiseStrubGainScatter{} between
realisations, consistent with their single realisation's \NoiseStrubGainTheirs{}.

\paragraph{Self-consistency.}
On the noiseless injection of the same source the chain converges ($\hat R = \NoiseStrubRhat{}$)
and its likelihood peaks at the truth: the refined maximum-likelihood \acs{snr} is
\NoiseStrubRhoMle{} against the optimal \NoiseStrubRhoOpt{}. Of the \NoiseStrubMarginals{}
marginals, \NoiseStrubCentred{} centre on the truth, the largest pull (offset from the truth in
posterior standard deviations) being \NoiseStrubPullMax{}; $\log_{10}\Amp$ and $\cos\iota$ sit at
\num{\NoiseStrubPullAmp} and \num{\NoiseStrubPullCosIota}, moved along their degeneracy by prior volume,
not by the likelihood (\cref{fig:strub-source}a).
