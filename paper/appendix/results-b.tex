\subsection{Setup}

B covers the whole band, 0.1--12\,mHz, in windows of 4096 bins ($R=1024$, an interior of 3072
bins) with 2048 data tokens, 64 times as many as XS. Everything else is the XS recipe
(\cref{app:xs}): four sources per window, the same priors, the 512-wide network (75.6M
parameters, unchanged), the warped clock with $p=3$, auxiliary heads for the first 10\% of the
steps, and a linear cooldown over the last 20\%. A step costs 1.54\,s on one A100 (41 times
XS) with a peak of 18.6\,GiB, so B can afford a fifth of the steps of the XS runs:

We train the 512-wide network for 200k steps, the last 40k cooling down. On XS, width 768
bought detection at equal steps (\cref{app:xs-ladder}), but B is far short of the XS step
counts, and each doubling of the steps was worth about 20\% in width on XS; at this budget,
steps are the better use of compute. The run took 86 hours in four chained 24-hour jobs, each
of which restarted the optimiser's moments without a visible cost (\cref{app:training}).
\Cref{fig:training}c shows the curve: the flow loss fell from 0.753 in the first epoch to
0.498 at the end of the constant learning rate, and to 0.473 after the cooldown. The cooldown
is worth $-0.025$, twice what the constant-rate trend would have given over the same steps.
As on XS, the auxiliary losses drift once their weight reaches zero (at 20k steps).

The recipe trains on B, but the network does not localise $f_0$ (\cref{tab:b-band},
\cref{fig:b-results}a). One source in 240 is narrower than a bin. The widths are 2.8--4.5 bins
(interquartile range) at every SNR from 5 to 1700: flat in $\snr$, where the ideal width falls
as $1/\snr$, so a loud source's posterior is about 2000 times its ideal width. The posteriors
are honest about it. In every band in68 is 0.84--0.90 and in95 0.97--1.00, so they
over-cover, and the offsets are a fraction of a width. Away from $f_0$ the sky is resolved: on
the loudest eval source (SNR 1668) the right ascension $\alpha$ is known to 0.009\,rad. The
polarisation angle and the initial phase are not: with $f_0$ uncertain by a few bins the phase
is lost, and $\psi$ with it.

\paragraph{The XS floor, in units of the coordinate.}
\Cref{tab:b-coordinate} puts the loud-source widths of B and XS side by side, in bins and in
units of the flow coordinate $x_f$, which maps the window interior onto $[-1,1]$
(\cref{app:geometry}). One unit of $x_f$ spans 24 bins on XS and about 1536 on B. In those
units the two problems meet (\cref{fig:b-results}b). The cooled XS networks place loud
sources to $1.5$--$1.6\times10^{-3}$ and B to $2.1\times10^{-3}$, within 30\%, with a window 64
times wider and a sixth of the steps. The loud-source floor of \cref{app:xs} is therefore a
fixed precision in the coordinate, about half a bfloat16 ulp of a number of order one
($2^{-8}\approx3.9\times10^{-3}$): below a bin on XS, three bins on B. On XS only the
learning-rate cooldown has moved it; the width of the network and the integrator did not
(\cref{tab:xs-ladder,tab:numerics}).

\paragraph{What does not set it.}
Three effects that scale with the window are ruled out.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{The bfloat16 cell of the input coordinate} (\cref{app:geometry-remarks}). On B
        it spans 6 bins for $|x_f|>0.5$ and shrinks towards the window centre. Yet sources in
        0.1--0.4-bin cells are as wide as those in 6-bin cells (\cref{fig:b-results}c;
        Spearman $+0.21$ over 229 sources).
  \item \textbf{The positions of the data tokens.} The token index is built in the compute
        dtype, and bfloat16 holds integers exactly only up to 256. So on B the 2048 columns of
        tokens get only 641 distinct positions, in groups of 2, 4 and 8 above columns 256, 512
        and 1024. The positional sinusoids have no period shorter than the window, so even
        distinct neighbours differ by only 0.003\,rad. Yet the widths are 3.1, 3.1 and 3.5
        bins where the groups span 4, 8 and 16 bins. Whatever its effect, this is a defect
        for long windows, and the remedy below removes it.
  \item \textbf{Rounding at evaluation.} fp32 at evaluation leaves the XS floor unchanged to
        1\% (\cref{tab:numerics}). That caps the share of evaluation rounding at
        $7.5\times10^{-4}$ units of $x_f$ in quadrature, at most 7\% of B's width.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{By frequency.}
\Cref{tab:b-frequency} splits the band into thirds. Above 0.49\,mHz the width in units of $x_f$
is the same. Below, it is about 40\% larger, and the posteriors are shifted: offsets of 2.3
bins, and in68 of 0.71. These low-frequency windows span almost a factor of two in frequency,
so the map from $x_f$ to bins is most curved there. Their offsets are shared by the sources of
a window: across all 60 windows, the standard deviation of a window's mean offset is 3.2 bins,
against 1.6 within a window, as if the network misplaced the whole window. With $f_0$
unresolved, the chirp mass is not measured anywhere in the band.

\paragraph{A remedy under test.}
The network sees a source's frequency only as the raw scalar $x_f$, through a small MLP, and
the positions of the data tokens only through sinusoids no finer than the window
(\cref{app:architecture}). Both resolve a fixed fraction of the window. We give each source and
each column of tokens its position in the window, in bins, through one Fourier basis shared by
the two streams. The features are $\sin$ and $\cos$ of $2\pi b/P$, for 32 periods $P$ from 2
bins to twice the window. They are computed in fp32 before the bfloat16 cast and added
through linear maps, so a query can score a key by a weighted sum of
$\cos\big(2\pi(b_{\rm source}-b_{\rm token})/P\big)$.

The remedy is tested on XS first, where the floor is the same in units of $x_f$: the recipe of
the cooled 512 model, from scratch, with these features. A loud floor at or below 0.012 bins
($5\times10^{-4}$ units of $x_f$, a third of the current one) would mean the representation was
the limit, and B is then retrained with them. \tbd
