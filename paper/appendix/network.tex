% =======================================================================================
\section{Network}
\label{app:network}
% =======================================================================================

The network maps a source state $X_t$, the flow time $t$, the input $y$ and the window centre
$\fw$ to the mean and log standard deviation of the endpoint $X_1$ per coordinate of each source (\cref{app:fm}). It is a transformer with two token
streams, one for the sources and one for the data: the \ac{mmdit} of \citet{esser2024scaling},
with the adaptive-normalisation conditioning of \citet{peebles2023scalable}. Unlike \ac{mmdit},
the streams are one-way. The data tokens attend only to each other and are conditioned on $\fw$
alone, so their keys and values in every block depend on the observation only and are computed
once for it. The source tokens attend to each other, to \NetworkSinksWord{} sink tokens and to
the cached data, conditioned on the flow time and $\fw$. \Acp{rope} put both streams on one
frequency axis. \Cref{fig:network-architecture} shows the network and \cref{tab:network-sizes}
its sizes. Transformers also condition posterior estimators for ground-based detectors
\citep{kofler2026flexible}; here the sources are tokens too, and attend to the data and to each
other.

\subsection{Input}
\label{app:network-input}

The network sees a window through three operations: whitening, an \ac{stft} into tokens, and a
compression of the dynamic range.

\paragraph{Whitening.}
The input $y$ is the window whitened by \cref{eq:noise-whiten}: the real and imaginary parts of
$A$, $E$ and $T$ in each of the $K$ bins, $K\times6$ numbers, from the window start $k_0$
(\cref{app:forward-model}). Below, $y_{k,c}$ is the complex whitened value of bin $k =
0,\dots,K-1$ and channel $c\in\{A, E, T\}$. Under noise alone its real and imaginary parts have
unit variance, and a source's whitened signal $\hat h$ has $\sum_{k,c}|\hat h_{k,c}|^2 =
\snr^2$ (\cref{eq:inner}).

\paragraph{Short-time spectra.}
The window is cut into bands of $Q=\NetworkPatch{}$ consecutive bins, one token each:
\NetworkTokensNarrow{} tokens on the narrow band ($K=\NetworkBinsNarrow{}$) and
\NetworkTokensFull{} on the full band ($K=\NetworkBinsFull{}$). An orthonormal inverse discrete
Fourier transform over each band turns its spectrum into $Q$ time samples of the band's signal,
per channel,
\begin{equation}
  \check y_{n,j,c} = \frac{1}{\sqrt Q}\sum_{l=0}^{Q-1} y_{nQ+l,\,c}\,e^{2\pi\ii jl/Q},
  \qquad j = 0,\dots,Q-1,
  \label{eq:network-stft}
\end{equation}
an \ac{stft} with the tokens as its bands. It is critically sampled and untapered, so it is
invertible, keeps the noise white, and neither leaks nor duplicates power between tokens, as an
overlapping tapered transform would. Within a token, a source's sub-bin frequency becomes a
phase slope over the samples, its initial phase the intercept, and the annual Doppler and
amplitude modulation that carry the sky a slow evolution in time.

\paragraph{Compression.}
The transform is unitary, so a source's samples carry the same $\snr^2$ as its bins and a single
sample of its signal has $|\check h| \le \snr$, which reaches \NetworkSnrTop{} at the prior's \qty{\NetworkSnrQuantile}{\percent}
quantile in the full band (\cref{app:priors}), against unit-variance noise. Each complex sample enters as its compressed
amplitude $\operatorname{asinh}|\check y|$, linear in the noise and logarithmic for loud signals,
and its gated phase $\check y/\sqrt{1+|\check y|^2}$, which is $\check y$ in the noise and a unit
phasor for a loud signal. A token is thus $3Q\times3 = \NetworkTokenScalars{}$ numbers:
\NetworkAmplitudeScalars{} amplitudes and \NetworkPhaseScalars{} phase parts.

\paragraph{Absolute frequency.}
Whitening and windowing remove the absolute frequency, which still matters: the Doppler width
scales with $f$ and the prior's typical drift $\fdot$ with $f_0^{\ChartFdotSlope}$ (\cref{app:chart}). Each data token is therefore given the
absolute frequencies of its lowest and highest bin, $(k_0 + nQ)/\Tobs$ and $(k_0 + nQ + Q - 1)/\Tobs$,
and the data stream the window centre $\fw$ (\cref{app:network-embeddings}).

\subsection{Embeddings}
\label{app:network-embeddings}

All feed-forward maps are SwiGLU \citep{shazeer2020glu}, $\mathrm{FF}(h) =
W_2\bigl(a\odot\mathrm{SiLU}(g)\bigr)$ with $[a;g] = W_1h$. Scalars enter in one of three ways
(\cref{tab:network-embeddings}). The \NetworkTokenScalars{} numbers of a data token, already
compressed, are mapped linearly to $\R^D$. A piecewise-linear embedding \citep{gorishniy2022embeddings}
cuts the range of a scalar into equal bins, codes the scalar by how far it has crossed each one,
with the end codes unclipped outside the range, and maps the codes linearly to $\R^D$; it
resolves a fraction of a bin wherever the scalar lies. A frequency or a time enters as sinusoids,
$E(\tau) = \bigl[\cos(2\pi\tau/P_k);\ \sin(2\pi\tau/P_k)\bigr]_k$ over a set of periods $P_k$,
followed by an $\mathrm{FF}$. The absolute frequencies of the data tokens and the sources share
one set of periods in hertz, octaves $P_k = 2^kP_0$ from $P_0 = \NetworkFreqFinest{}$ of a bin
($1/\Tobs$) up to past twice the top bin of the band; their turns $\tau/P_k$ are reduced modulo
one in float64 before the cast, so even the finest period keeps its phase. Every embedding runs in float32, whatever the backbone's precision
(\cref{app:network-sizes}).

\paragraph{Sources.}
Each source enters as \NetworkSourceScalars{} scalars: its \NetworkCoordsWord{} flow
coordinates (\cref{app:chart}) and the higher harmonics of its two helicity amplitudes,
$r\cos k\phi$ and $r\sin k\phi$ for $k=2,\dots,\NetworkHarmonics{}$, with $(r,\phi)$ the polar
form of each helicity pair (\NetworkHarmonicScalars{} scalars). The harmonics give the network
functions of the phases that vanish with the amplitude. The token adds a sinusoidal embedding of
its absolute frequency $f_0$ and no label of its position (\cref{app:network-slots}); $f_0$
comes from $x_f$ through the inverse chart in float64, before any cast.

\paragraph{Conditioning.}
The data stream is conditioned on the window alone, $\nu_f = \mathrm{FF}_f\bigl(E(\ln\fw - \ln
f_{\min})\bigr)$, and the source stream on
\begin{equation}
  \nu = \mathrm{FF}_\nu\Bigl(\bigl[E(t);\ E\bigl(\max(\log_{10}(1-t), -\NetworkTimeDecades{}) +
  \NetworkTimeDecades{}\bigr);\ E(\ln\fw - \ln f_{\min})\bigr]\Bigr),
  \label{eq:network-c}
\end{equation}
where $f_{\min}$ is the bottom of the $f_0$ prior and each scalar's periods are geometric down
from twice its span. The network sees the flow time $t$, not the path position. The second input
resolves the last decades of the path, where the fine structure of a narrow posterior forms
(\cref{app:fm-sensitivity}).

\subsection{One-Way Blocks}
\label{app:network-blocks}

Each of the $N$ blocks updates the data tokens $h^y$, the source tokens $h^x$ and the sink
tokens $h^s$. For each stream $\ell$, a zero-initialised linear map of $\mathrm{SiLU}(\nu_f)$
($\ell = y$) or $\mathrm{SiLU}(\nu)$ ($\ell = x, s$) produces a shift, a scale and a gate,
$(\beta^\ell,\gamma^\ell,\varpi^\ell)$, so that every residual branch starts as the identity and
a fresh network ignores its conditioning \citep{peebles2023scalable}:
\begin{align}
  u^\ell &= \mathrm{LN}(h^\ell)\odot(1+\gamma^\ell) + \beta^\ell,\qquad
  [q^\ell; k^\ell; v^\ell] = \bigl[W^\ell_{q}u^\ell; W^\ell_{k}u^\ell; W^\ell_{v}u^\ell\bigr], \nonumber\\
  o^y &= \mathrm{Attn}\bigl(q^y;\ k^y, v^y\bigr),\qquad
  [o^x; o^s] = \mathrm{Attn}\bigl([q^x; q^s];\ [k^x; k^s; k^y], [v^x; v^s; v^y]\bigr), \nonumber\\
  h^\ell &\leftarrow h^\ell + \varpi^\ell\odot W^\ell_{o}\,o^\ell, \nonumber\\
  h^\ell &\leftarrow h^\ell + \varpi'^\ell\odot\mathrm{FF}^\ell\bigl(\mathrm{LN}(h^\ell)\odot(1+\gamma'^\ell) + \beta'^\ell\bigr),
  \label{eq:network-block}
\end{align}
with a second shift, scale and gate $(\beta'^\ell,\gamma'^\ell,\varpi'^\ell)$ for the
feed-forward branch, $\mathrm{LN}$ a layer normalisation without learned affine parameters, and
feed-forward hidden width $\NetworkExpand{}D$. Each stream has its own maps, and there are no
biases inside the blocks. The query, key and value maps are separate matrices, so the optimiser
orthogonalises each on its own (\cref{app:training}). Activations are rematerialised in the
backward pass \citep{chen2016training}.

\paragraph{The cache.}
The data stream never reads the sources, the sinks or $t$, so its keys and values in every
block, $(k^y, v^y)$, depend on $(y,\fw)$ alone. One encoding of an observation serves all
\NetworkDraws{} base draws that share it in training, and in sampling all \NetworkSampleEvals{} evaluations along every draw's path
(\cref{app:fm-sampling}). In B, encoding a full-band window takes \NetworkEncodeGflopFull{} GFLOP
of matrix products, \NetworkEncodeOverDecodeFull{} times one evaluation on the cache
(\NetworkDecodeGflopFull{} GFLOP), and the cache holds \qty{\NetworkCacheMibFull}{\mebi\byte} in
bfloat16; a narrow window takes \NetworkEncodeGflopNarrow{} and \NetworkDecodeGflopNarrow{} GFLOP,
with \qty{\NetworkCacheMibNarrow}{\mebi\byte} of cache. The last block's data and sink outputs
reach nothing, so it has no output projections, feed-forward modulations or feed-forward maps for them.

\paragraph{Attention.}
Attention has $H$ heads of dimension \NetworkHeadDim{}. Queries and keys are RMS-normalised per
head, and the queries carry a learned per-head gain, initialised to one
\citep{henry2020query,dehghani2023scaling}. The source and sink queries take one softmax over
their own keys and the cached keys, taken in two parts that are never concatenated in memory,
which would copy the shared cache once per draw.

\paragraph{Positions.}
Queries, keys and values are rotated \citep{su2024roformer,miyato2024gta}: each of the
\NetworkRopePairs{} pairs of a head's dimensions turns by $2\pi (f-f_c)/P_j$, with periods $P_j =
2K^{j/(\NetworkRopePairs{}-1)}/T$ geometric from 2 bins to twice the window. A score then depends
on how far apart its two tokens are in frequency, and the weighted sum of rotated values is turned back
by the query's own angle, so a value also arrives relative to its query. A data token's position
$f$ is its centre frequency and a source's its $f_0$, both taken from the window's centre
frequency $f_c$, so that the unrotated sinks see the same layout in every window; the angles are
reduced in float64 before the cast. With positions in frequency a source finds the data tokens near its own
$f_0$ by relative distance, instead of learning where each absolute position sits.

\paragraph{Sinks.}
The \NetworkSinksWord{} sink tokens, each $\nu$ plus a learned embedding, join the source stream
with their own maps and no position: their keys and values are not rotated, and their outputs
are not turned back. They give the sources' attention somewhere to go other than the data, and
since they start from $\nu$ they can carry per-draw state
\citep{xiao2024efficient,darcet2024vision}. They are neither cached nor read out.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Head}
\label{app:network-head}

The source tokens pass through a final layer normalisation with a shift and scale from $\nu$,
and an $\mathrm{FF}:\R^D\to\R^{\NetworkHeadOutputs{}}$ with hidden width $D$, shared over
sources, which gives each of the \NetworkCoords{} coordinates the mean $\hat X_1$ and log standard
deviation $\log\sigma$ of the endpoint (\cref{app:fm-loss}). There is no other output.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Permutation Equivariance}
\label{app:network-slots}

Every map applied to a source token is shared across sources, and no token carries a label of
its position, so attention over the source tokens is equivariant to their permutations, as in set
transformers \citep{zaheer2017deep,lee2019set}: permuting the input sources permutes the output
the same way. The other inputs keep this. The rotary positions are physical, the bin of
each source's own $f_0$, so a source attends to the data near its frequency and to the other
sources by their distance in bins, whatever their order; and the attention sinks are shared by
all sources. From an exchangeable
base the flow's draws are therefore exchangeable too (\cref{app:chart-slots}), and the evaluation
pairs drawn sources with injections before scoring (\cref{app:evaluation}).

\subsection{Sizes and Precision}
\label{app:network-sizes}

\Cref{tab:network-sizes} gives the five sizes; all have heads of dimension \NetworkHeadDim{}. The
network is the same on both bands apart from the number of data tokens, which adds no parameters,
and the number of periods of the two absolute-frequency embeddings, which makes the narrow-band totals
\numrange{\NetworkNarrowSavedMin}{\NetworkNarrowSavedMax} parameters lower. Each block has $48D^2
+ H$ parameters: $12D^2$ in the modulations and $8D^2$ in the attention maps of the data and
source streams, $12D^2$ in their feed-forward maps, and $16D^2$ for the sinks. The last block
lacks $20D^2$ of them, the data and sink streams' output projections ($2D^2$), feed-forward
modulations ($6D^2$) and feed-forward maps ($12D^2$), so the backbone has $N(48D^2 + H) - 20D^2$,
which matches the count of the built network.

\paragraph{Precision.}
Parameters are kept in float32. One setting chooses what runs in bfloat16
\citep{micikevicius2018mixed,kalamkar2019study}: the whole backbone, forward and backward (the
default), only the attention scores and weighted sums, or nothing. In every mode the embeddings,
the conditioning maps and the head run in float32, and every softmax is taken in float32. The
embeddings stay in float32 because bfloat16 keeps \NetworkBfBits{} significant bits: rounding a
whitened value above \NetworkBfUnity{} can move it by a unit of noise, and the loudest
samples reach \NetworkSnrTop{}. The source stream's attention logits are computed in float32 from
bfloat16 inputs; it has only \NetworkSourcesWord{} source and \NetworkSinksWord{} sink queries
per draw. In bfloat16 on Ampere or newer GPUs the data stream's self-attention runs as a fused
flash-attention kernel \citep{dao2022flashattention}, so its logits are never materialised.
