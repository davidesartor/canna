% =======================================================================================
\section{Network}
\label{app:network}
% =======================================================================================

This appendix specifies the network of \cref{sec:method-network} well enough to rebuild it: the
forward pass in the order of the data flow, then the sizes. The network is a one-way multi-stream
transformer, the \ac{mmdit} of \citet{esser2024scaling} with the adaptive-normalisation
conditioning of \citet{peebles2023scalable}. An encoder runs the data tokens and one frame register
per slot, conditioned on the window alone, and returns the frame of \cref{app:fm}, a mean and a log
standard deviation per coordinate of each source slot. A decoder runs the source tokens and
\NetworkSinksWord{} sink tokens, conditioned on the flow time and the window, and returns the mean
and log standard deviation of the endpoint $Z_1$ in that frame. \Cref{fig:network-architecture}
shows the layout, \cref{tab:network-embeddings} what each token is built from and
\cref{tab:network-sizes} the sizes.

\paragraph{Notation.}
All feed-forward maps are SwiGLU \citep{shazeer2020glu}, $\mathrm{FF}(h) =
W_2\bigl(a\odot\mathrm{SiLU}(g)\bigr)$ with $[a;g] = W_1h$. A scalar $\xi$ enters as sinusoids
$E(\xi) = \bigl[\cos(2\pi\xi/P_k);\ \sin(2\pi\xi/P_k)\bigr]_k$ over a set of periods $P_k$,
followed by an $\mathrm{FF}$. The absolute frequencies of the data tokens, the sources and the
window share one set of periods in hertz, octaves $P_k = 2^kP_0$ from $P_0 =
\NetworkFreqFinest{}$ of a bin ($1/\Tobs$) up to past twice the top bin of the band. The fine
periods give the sub-bin resolution that posteriors this narrow require. The turns $\xi/P_k$ are
reduced modulo one in float64 before the cast, so that even the finest period keeps its phase.
Every embedding runs in float32, whatever the precision of the backbone
(\cref{app:network-sizes}).

\subsection{Data Tokens}
\label{app:network-input}

\paragraph{Whitened input.}
The input $y$ is the window whitened as in \cref{sec:gw-windows}: the real and imaginary parts of
$A$, $E$ and $T$ in each of the $K$ bins from the window start $k_0$ (\cref{app:forward-model}).
Below, $y_{k,c}$ is the complex whitened value of bin $k = 0,\dots,K-1$ and channel $c\in\{A, E,
T\}$. Under noise alone its real and imaginary parts have unit variance, and a source's whitened
signal $\hat h$ has $\sum_{k,c}|\hat h_{k,c}|^2 = \snr^2$ (\cref{eq:inner}).

\paragraph{Short-time spectra.}
The window is cut into bands of $Q=\NetworkPatch{}$ consecutive bins, one token each:
\NetworkTokensNarrow{} tokens on the narrow band ($K=\NetworkBinsNarrow{}$) and
\NetworkTokensFull{} on the full band ($K=\NetworkBinsFull{}$). An orthonormal inverse discrete
Fourier transform over each band turns its spectrum into $Q$ time samples of the band's signal, per
channel,
\begin{equation}
  \check y_{n,j,c} = \frac{1}{\sqrt Q}\sum_{l=0}^{Q-1} y_{nQ+l,\,c}\,e^{2\pi\ii jl/Q},
  \qquad j = 0,\dots,Q-1,
  \label{eq:network-stft}
\end{equation}
an \ac{stft} with the tokens as its bands. It is critically sampled and untapered, so it is
invertible, keeps the noise white, and neither leaks nor duplicates power between tokens, as an
overlapping tapered transform would. Within a token, a source's sub-bin frequency becomes a phase
slope over the samples, its initial phase the intercept, and the annual Doppler and the amplitude
modulation that carry its sky position a slow evolution in time.

\paragraph{Compression.}
The transform is unitary, so a source's samples carry the same $\snr^2$ as its bins, and a single
sample of its signal has $|\check h| \le \snr$, which reaches \NetworkSnrTop{} at the prior's
\qty{\NetworkSnrQuantile}{\percent} quantile in the full band (\cref{app:priors}), against
unit-variance noise. Each complex sample therefore enters as its compressed amplitude
$\operatorname{asinh}|\check y|$, linear in the noise and logarithmic for loud signals, and its
gated phase $\check y/\sqrt{1+|\check y|^2}$, which is $\check y$ in the noise and a unit phasor
for a loud signal. A token is $3Q\times3 = \NetworkTokenScalars{}$ numbers:
\NetworkAmplitudeScalars{} amplitudes and \NetworkPhaseScalars{} phase parts, mapped linearly to
$\R^D$.

\paragraph{Absolute frequency.}
Whitening and windowing remove the absolute frequency, which still matters: the Doppler width
scales with $f$ and the prior's typical drift $\fdot$ with $f_0^{\ChartFdotSlope}$
(\cref{app:chart}). Each data token therefore receives the absolute frequencies of its lowest and
highest bin, $(k_0 + nQ)/\Tobs$ and $(k_0 + nQ + Q - 1)/\Tobs$, through $\mathrm{FF}_y$.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Frame Registers and Frame Head}
\label{app:network-frame}

The $S$ frame registers are tokens that run beside the data tokens in every block, one per source
slot, each a learned slot embedding plus $\nu_f$. They attend to the data and to each other, and
the data attend to them; they are registers in the sense of \citet{darcet2024vision}, tokens that
hold global state, here one per source. The slots are the sources in order of $f_0$
(\cref{app:chart-slots}), so register $k$ learns to frame the $k$-th lowest source.

After the last block, a final layer normalisation with a shift, scale and gate from $\nu_f$ and a
linear map $\R^D\to\R^{\NetworkHeadOutputs{}}$ without bias give each slot the shift and log scale
$(\delta_\mu,\delta_s)$ of its frame, per coordinate (\cref{eq:fm-frame}). The modulation and the
gate are zero-initialised, so a fresh network's frame is the prior's moments. The frame costs almost
nothing: the registers add $S$ tokens to the data tokens, and the head is a small part of the
encoder.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Source Tokens and Sinks}
\label{app:network-sources}

Each source token carries \NetworkSourceScalars{} scalars, mapped linearly to $\R^D$:
\begin{itemize}
  \item its \NetworkCoordsWord{} coordinates in the frame, $Z$, and in the chart, $X = \mu +
  \sigma Z$ (\cref{app:chart});
  \item the higher harmonics of its two helicity amplitudes in $X$, $r\cos k\phi$ and $r\sin
  k\phi$ for $k=2,\dots,\NetworkHarmonics{}$, with $(r,\phi)$ the polar form of each helicity pair
  (\NetworkHarmonicScalars{} scalars), which give the network functions of the phases that vanish
  with the amplitude;
  \item its slot's frame, $\mu$ and $\log\sigma$, through a stop-gradient, so that the flow cannot
  train the frame (\cref{app:fm-frame}).
\end{itemize}
To these the token adds a sinusoidal embedding of its absolute frequency $f_0$, obtained from $x_f$
through the inverse chart in float64 before any cast. A source token carries no label of its slot:
it reads its frame through $\mu$ and $\log\sigma$ and its slot's keys and values in the cache, and
every map applied to a source token is shared across sources, as in set transformers
\citep{zaheer2017deep,lee2019set}.

The \NetworkSinksWord{} sink tokens, each a learned embedding plus $\nu$, join the source stream
with their own maps. They give the sources' attention somewhere to go other than the data, and since
they start from $\nu$ they can carry per-draw state \citep{xiao2024efficient,darcet2024vision}.
They are neither cached nor read out.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{One-Way Blocks}
\label{app:network-blocks}

Each of the $N$ blocks updates four streams: the data tokens $h^y$ and the frame registers $h^r$,
conditioned on $\nu_f$, and the source tokens $h^x$ and the sink tokens $h^s$, conditioned on $\nu$
(\cref{app:network-conditioning}). For each stream $\ell$, a zero-initialised linear map of
$\mathrm{SiLU}(\nu_f)$ or $\mathrm{SiLU}(\nu)$ gives a shift, a scale and a gate
$(\beta^\ell,\gamma^\ell,\varpi^\ell)$, so that every residual branch starts as the identity
\citep{peebles2023scalable}:
\begin{align}
  u^\ell &= \mathrm{LN}(h^\ell)\odot(1+\gamma^\ell) + \beta^\ell,\qquad
  [q^\ell; k^\ell; v^\ell] = \bigl[W^\ell_{q}u^\ell; W^\ell_{k}u^\ell; W^\ell_{v}u^\ell\bigr], \nonumber\\
  [o^y; o^r] &= \mathrm{Attn}\bigl([q^y; q^r];\ [k^y; k^r], [v^y; v^r]\bigr),\nonumber\\
  [o^x; o^s] &= \mathrm{Attn}\bigl([q^x; q^s];\ [k^x; k^s; k^y; k^r], [v^x; v^s; v^y; v^r]\bigr), \nonumber\\
  h^\ell &\leftarrow h^\ell + \varpi^\ell\odot W^\ell_{o}\,o^\ell, \nonumber\\
  h^\ell &\leftarrow h^\ell + \varpi'^\ell\odot\mathrm{FF}^\ell\bigl(\mathrm{LN}(h^\ell)\odot(1+\gamma'^\ell) + \beta'^\ell\bigr),
  \label{eq:network-block}
\end{align}
with a second shift, scale and gate for the feed-forward branch, $\mathrm{LN}$ a layer
normalisation without learned affine parameters, and feed-forward hidden width $\NetworkExpand{}D$.
Each stream has its own maps and there are no biases inside the blocks. The query, key and value
maps are separate matrices, so that the optimiser orthogonalises each on its own
(\cref{app:training-optimiser}). Activations are rematerialised in the backward pass
\citep{chen2016training}.

\paragraph{Cache.}
The data and the registers never read the sources, the sinks or $t$, so their keys and values in
every block and the registers' outputs depend on $(y,\fw)$ alone. One encoding of an observation
therefore gives its frame and serves all \NetworkDraws{} base draws that share it in training, and
in sampling all \rev{$2K_t=\FlowMatchingEvaluations{}$} evaluations along every draw's path (\cref{app:fm-sampling}).
In size B, encoding a full-band window takes \NetworkEncodeGflopFull{} GFLOP of matrix products,
\NetworkEncodeOverDecodeFull{} times one evaluation on the cache (\NetworkDecodeGflopFull{} GFLOP),
and the cache holds \qty{\NetworkCacheMibFull}{\mebi\byte} in bfloat16; a narrow window takes
\NetworkEncodeGflopNarrow{} and \NetworkDecodeGflopNarrow{} GFLOP, with
\qty{\NetworkCacheMibNarrow}{\mebi\byte} of cache. The last block's data and sink outputs reach
nothing, so it omits their output projections, feed-forward modulations and feed-forward maps; its
register outputs feed the frame head.

\paragraph{Attention.}
Attention has $H$ heads of dimension \NetworkHeadDim{}. Queries and keys are normalised by their
\ac{rms} per head, and the queries carry a learned per-head gain initialised to one
\citep{henry2020query,dehghani2023scaling}. The source and sink queries take one softmax over their
own keys and the cached keys, computed in parts that are never concatenated in memory, since
concatenation would copy the shared cache once per draw.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Conditioning}
\label{app:network-conditioning}

The data stream, the registers and the frame head are conditioned on the window alone,
\begin{equation}
  \nu_f = \mathrm{FF}_f\bigl(\bigl[E(f_{\rm lo});\ E(f_{\rm hi})\bigr]\bigr),
\end{equation}
with $f_{\rm lo}$ and $f_{\rm hi}$ in hertz the lowest and highest $f_0$ a source of the window can
have, the prior's range cut to the window less its guard bands (\cref{app:forward-model}). The
source stream, the sinks and the endpoint head are conditioned on
\begin{equation}
  \nu = \mathrm{FF}_\nu\Bigl(\bigl[E(t);\ E\bigl(\max(\log_{10}(1-t), -\NetworkTimeDecades{}) +
  \NetworkTimeDecades{}\bigr)\bigr]\Bigr) + \nu_f,
  \label{eq:network-c}
\end{equation}
where $t$ is the path position $s$ of \cref{app:fm} and each time scalar's periods are geometric
down from twice its span. The second input resolves the last decades of the path, where the fine
structure of a posterior forms (\cref{app:fm-sensitivity}).

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Positions}
\label{app:network-positions}

The keys, queries and values of the data and source tokens are rotated by frequency
\citep{su2024roformer,miyato2024gta}: each of the \NetworkRopePairs{} pairs of a head's dimensions
turns by $2\pi f/P_j$, with periods $P_j = 2K^{j/(\NetworkRopePairs{}-1)}/T$ geometric from 2 bins
to twice the window. A score then depends on how far apart its two tokens are in frequency, and the
weighted sum of rotated values is turned back by the query's own angle, so that a value also
arrives relative to its query. A data token's position $f$ is its centre frequency and a source's
its $f_0$, absolute in hertz, with the turns $f/P_j$ reduced modulo one in float64 before the cast.
A source thus finds the data tokens near its own $f_0$ by relative distance, instead of learning
where each absolute position sits, and attends to the other sources by their distance in bins.
Many of the periods are shorter than a token, so the rotation also resolves frequency inside a
patch. The registers and the sinks carry no position: their keys and values are not rotated, and
their outputs are not turned back.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Endpoint Head}
\label{app:network-head}

The source tokens pass through a final layer normalisation with a shift and scale from $\nu$ and an
$\mathrm{FF}:\R^D\to\R^{\NetworkHeadOutputs{}}$ with hidden width $D$, shared over sources. It gives
each of the \NetworkCoords{} coordinates the mean $\hat Z_1$ and the log standard deviation
$\log\tau$ of the endpoint in the frame (\cref{app:fm-loss}). With the frame head, these are the
network's only outputs.

\subsection{Sizes and Precision}
\label{app:network-sizes}

\Cref{tab:network-sizes} gives the five sizes, all with heads of dimension \NetworkHeadDim{}. The
two bands differ only in the number of data tokens, which adds no parameters, and in the number of
periods of the two absolute-frequency embeddings, which makes the narrow-band totals
\numrange{\NetworkNarrowSavedMin}{\NetworkNarrowSavedMax} parameters lower. Each block has $64D^2 +
H$ parameters: each of the four streams has $6D^2$ in its modulations, $4D^2$ in its attention maps
and $6D^2$ in its feed-forward maps, and the queries carry $H$ gains. The last block lacks the
$20D^2$ of the data and sink streams' unused output projections, modulations and feed-forward maps,
so the backbone has $N(64D^2 + H) - 20D^2$ parameters.

\paragraph{Precision.}
Parameters are kept in float32. One setting chooses what runs in bfloat16
\citep{micikevicius2018mixed,kalamkar2019study}: the whole backbone, forward and backward (the
default), only the attention scores and weighted sums, or nothing. In every mode the embeddings, the
conditioning maps and the two heads run in float32, and every softmax is taken in float32. The
embeddings stay in float32 because bfloat16 keeps \NetworkBfBits{} significant bits: rounding a
whitened value above \NetworkBfUnity{} can move it by a unit of noise, and the loudest samples reach
\NetworkSnrTop{}. In bfloat16 on Ampere or newer GPUs the data stream's self-attention runs as a
fused flash-attention kernel \citep{dao2022flashattention}.

Evaluating the trained models with a float32 backbone \rev{changes them little against their gap to the
chains. In the separate re-evaluation of \cref{app:results-mcmc}, whose bfloat16 values differ from those of
\cref{tab:results-widths},} the median $f_0$ width
relative to the joint chains' at $\snr=15$--40 goes from \ResultsNarrowFourFpBfMid{} to
\ResultsNarrowFourFpThirtyTwoMid{} (narrow band, $S=4$) and from \ResultsFullEightFpBfMid{} to
\ResultsFullEightFpThirtyTwoMid{} (full band, $S=8$), and on loud full-band sources below
\qty{10}{\milli\hertz} from \ResultsFullEightFpBfLoud{} to \ResultsFullEightFpThirtyTwoLoud. The
bfloat16 backbone is therefore not what keeps the loud sources wide\rev{, though at $\snr=15$--40 on the full band it
accounts for about a sixth of the width}.
