% =======================================================================================
\section{Flow Matching: Objective and Sampler}
\label{app:fm}
% =======================================================================================

The flow is trained on straight paths from prior draws to the true sources. The network predicts
a Gaussian over the endpoint of the path, its mean and width per coordinate, and is trained on the
endpoint's negative log-likelihood; the sampler follows the velocity to the predicted mean.
\Cref{tab:fm-recipe} lists the choices. A Gaussian model of one coordinate
(\cref{app:fm-sensitivity}) shows why the likelihood, unlike the velocity loss, rewards narrow
posteriors, and what the sampler can and cannot resolve at the end of the path. The network is described in
\cref{app:network}, the optimiser in \cref{app:training}.

  \label{tab:fm-recipe}
  \small
  \begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
    \toprule
    component & setting & reason \\
    \midrule
    base & the prior & undetectable sources need no transport \\
    coupling & frequency order of the target & unordered sources, no crossing in $f_0$ \\

    output & endpoint mean $\hat X_1$ and $\log\sigma$ & bounded output, a width per source \\
    loss & Gaussian likelihood of $X_1$ & rewards narrowing relative to each width \\

\subsection{Straight Paths from the Prior}
\label{app:fm-paths}

A window with centre frequency $\fw$ holds $S$ sources $\theta_{1:S}$, in no particular order,
and its data $d$ enter the network as the input $y = y(d,\fw)$ (\cref{app:network}). Write
$X = \varphi(\theta_{1:S};\fw)\in\R^{8S}$ for the sources in the flow coordinates of
\cref{eq:chart}, source by source. The target is the posterior $p(X\mid y,\fw)\propto p(d\mid
X)\,\pi(X\mid\fw)$, with the likelihood of \cref{eq:loglik} and the prior of \cref{app:priors}.
The flow transports draws $X(0)$ from the prior along
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\dd X}{\dd s} = \vnet\bigl(X, s; y, \fw\bigr),\qquad s\in[0,1],
  \label{eq:fm-ode}
\end{equation}
so that $X(1)$ is a posterior draw. The position $s$ along the path is the network's time input,
written $t$ in \cref{app:network}.

Training uses conditional flow matching \citep{lipman2023flow,liu2023flow,albergo2023building}.
One training observation draws $\fw$ and $\theta_{1:S}$ from the prior, simulates the data
(\cref{app:forward-model,app:noise}) and sets $X_1 = \varphi(\theta_{1:S};\fw)$. It then draws
\FlowMatchingDraws{} independent base points $X_0 = \varphi(\theta'_{1:S};\fw)$,
$\theta'_{1:S}\sim\pi(\cdot\mid\fw)$, each paired with $X_1$ in order of $f_0$ (\cref{eq:fm-coupling}) and each with its own time; a
batch holds \FlowMatchingWindows{} observations, \FlowMatchingBatch{} points in all. The path is
the straight line, source by source,
\begin{equation}
  X_s = X_0 + s\,(X_1 - X_0),\qquad
  u = \frac{\dd X_s}{\dd s} = X_1 - X_0 .
  \label{eq:fm-path}
\end{equation}
Regressing a field on $u$ has the marginal field $v^\star(X,s;y,\fw) = \E[u\mid X_s = X,
y,\fw]$ as its minimiser, which carries $p_0$ to $p_1 = p(\cdot\mid y,\fw)$
\citep{lipman2023flow}. Because $X_0$ is independent of the data up to its labelling, $p_0$ is the
prior itself (\cref{app:fm-coupling}). All
eight coordinates are flat (\cref{app:chart}), so the line needs no geodesics.

The prior is the natural base for two reasons. A source the data say nothing about, such as a
source far below the noise, has a posterior close to its prior and needs almost no transport. And
every base draw is a valid set of sources in the window, so the path never leaves the region
where the network is trained. \Ac{fmpe} with a Gaussian base is studied by \citet{dax2023flow},
and has been applied to \ac{lisa} massive black-hole binaries \citep{liang2024rapid} and to a
single \ac{gb}-like source in gapped data \citep{mao2025robust}.

\subsection{Pairing Unordered Sources}
\label{app:fm-coupling}

The sources are exchangeable (\cref{app:chart-slots}), so the order in which $X_1$ lists them
carries no information, and a base draw paired with it label by label would send each base source
to an arbitrary target source, often across the window. Each base draw is therefore
paired with its target in order of frequency,
\begin{equation}
  X_0 \leftarrow X_0^{\hat\sigma},\qquad
  \hat\sigma = \tau_0\circ\tau_1^{-1},
  \label{eq:fm-coupling}
\end{equation}
with $X_0^{\sigma}$ the sources of $X_0$ in the order $\sigma(1),\dots,\sigma(S)$ and $\tau_0$,
$\tau_1$ the permutations that sort $X_0$ and $X_1$ by $f_0$: the base source of the $k$-th lowest
frequency heads for the target source of the $k$-th lowest. This is a sort, $O(S\log S)$, and the
optimal coupling of $f_0$ alone, the monotone rearrangement, so no two paths cross in $f_0$, the
coordinate the posterior pins down most tightly. It keeps the marginals: the relabelling permutes
the sources of $X_0$ without changing them, the network is equivariant and the prior exchangeable,
so the field it learns is that of the coupling symmetrised under a common relabelling of both ends,
whose ends are the prior and the posterior over unordered sources \citep{klein2023equivariant}.
The other coordinates are paired as they fall. Per source, the mean squared path in the
narrow-B window (flow coordinates) is \FlowMatchingPathNarrow{} in $f_0$ order, of which
\FlowMatchingPathNarrowFZero{} in $f_0$ against \FlowMatchingPathNarrowRandomFZero{} under labels
blind to $f_0$; the loudest-first and random labellings are \FlowMatchingPathNarrowSnrLonger{}\%
and \FlowMatchingPathNarrowRandomLonger{}\% longer in total. The nearest labelling below is
\FlowMatchingPathNarrowNearestShorter{}\% shorter (\FlowMatchingPathFullNearestShorter{}\% in
full-B), with a longer $f_0$ part (\FlowMatchingPathNarrowNearestFZero{}) and shorter
$\fdot$ and sky parts.

The nearest labelling over all coordinates,
\begin{equation}
  \hat\sigma = \operatorname*{arg\,min}_{\sigma\in\mathfrak S_S}
  \sum_{s=1}^{S}\bigl\|X_{0,\sigma(s)} - X_{1,s}\bigr\|^2,
  \label{eq:fm-coupling-ot}
\end{equation}
is the optimal-transport coupling over the permutation group of equivariant flow matching
\citep{klein2023equivariant}, applied to each pair rather than across the batch
\citep{tong2024improving}. It shortens the paths in every coordinate, at the cost of an assignment
per draw: $S!$ sums exhaustively, $O(S^3)$ by the Hungarian algorithm \citep{kuhn1955hungarian},
or Sinkhorn iterations \citep{cuturi2013sinkhorn} for an entropy-regularised soft assignment,
cheaper still on a GPU and rounded to a permutation per draw, as minibatch couplings are
\citep{tong2024improving}.

\subsection{The Endpoint Distribution and the Loss}
\label{app:fm-loss}

\paragraph{Output.}
For each source and coordinate the network outputs the mean $\hat X_1$ and the log standard
deviation $\log\sigma$ of a Gaussian over the endpoint, given the point on the path, and the
velocity is the one that reaches the mean in the remaining path,
\begin{equation}
  \vnet = \frac{\hat X_1 - X_s}{1-s}.
  \label{eq:fm-velocity}
\end{equation}
The output is a position in flow coordinates, whose scale is set by the chart and does not change
along the path, while the velocity grows as $1/(1-s)$ near the end, where a narrow posterior is
resolved (\cref{app:fm-sensitivity}); endpoint prediction converted to a velocity is also used on
structured data \citep{stark2024harmonic}. The width $\sigma$ serves the loss only; the sampler
follows the mean.

\paragraph{Loss.}
The objective is the Gaussian negative log-likelihood of the endpoint, up to a constant and a
factor of two,
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal L = \E\;\frac{1}{8S}\sum_{k=1}^{S}\sum_{i=1}^{8}
  \Bigl[\Bigl(\frac{X_{1,ki}-\hat X_{1,ki}}{\sigma_{ki}}\Bigr)^2 + 2\log\sigma_{ki}\Bigr],
  \label{eq:loss}
\end{equation}
with the expectation over the batch's observations, draws and times. Its minimiser is the
conditional mean and variance of each coordinate, $\hat X_1 = \E[X_1\mid X_s,y,\fw]$ and $\sigma^2
= \Var(X_1\mid X_s,y,\fw)$, so the velocity of \cref{eq:fm-velocity} is the marginal field
$v^\star$. On the straight path $X_1 - X_s = (1-s)\,u$, so $\hat X_1 - X_1 = (1-s)(\vnet - u)$ and
$\Var(X_1\mid X_s) = (1-s)^2\Var(u\mid X_s)$: at the optimal $\sigma$ each term of \cref{eq:loss}
is, up to a constant,
\begin{equation}
  \frac{(\vnet - u)^2}{\Var(u\mid X_s,y,\fw)},
  \label{eq:fm-relative}
\end{equation}
a velocity loss divided by its own irreducible part, for every point, coordinate and source. The
factors of $1-s$ cancel, and a loud source whose posterior is narrow weighs as much as a weak one
whose posterior is its prior, with no weights over time or coordinates. The learned $\sigma$ acts as
the learned task uncertainties of \citet{kendall2018multi}, one per point rather than one per task.

\paragraph{Diagnostics.}
The loss is logged with the mean squared error of $\hat X_1$. On the evaluation catalog the same
quantities are binned (\cref{app:training}): \FlowMatchingLossBins{} equal bins of
$\log_{10}(1-s)$ on $[\FlowMatchingLossDecades{},0]$, $\FlowMatchingLossBinWidth{}$ of a decade each,
and $G=\FlowMatchingGroups{}$ coordinate groups, $x_f$, $x_{\fdot}$, the sky $(x_l,x_b)$, $z_+$ and
$z_-$, each pooled over the sources. Each bin and group reports the squared error of $\hat X_1$,
the mean of $\sigma^2$ and the mean squared standardised error $\bigl((X_1-\hat X_1)/\sigma\bigr)^2$,
which is one where the widths are calibrated.

\subsection{Stratified Time}
\label{app:fm-clock}

Each point's time is $s\sim\Unif(0,1)$, stratified over the $B=\FlowMatchingBatch{}$ points of a
batch: with $\pi$ a random permutation, point $i$ gets $s_i = (\pi_i + U_i)/B$,
$U_i\sim\Unif(0,1)$. Every stratum of $s$ is then covered in every batch, which lowers the
variance of the batch loss at no cost, as the low-discrepancy sampler of variational diffusion
models does \citep{kingma2021variational}. The network sees $s$ and $\log_{10}(1-s)$, the latter
clipped to $[\FlowMatchingTimeDecades{},0]$ (\cref{app:network}), so it can tell apart times that
differ only in how close they are to the end.

Non-uniform time sampling is standard for diffusion models
\citep{karras2022elucidating,kingma2023understanding,esser2024scaling}, and \ac{fmpe} up-weights late
times with a power law in $t$ \citep[Sec.~3.3]{dax2023flow}. Here time stays uniform: under
\cref{eq:loss} the reward for narrowing a posterior does not fade with its width and is collected
along the whole path (\cref{app:fm-sensitivity}), so the end of the path needs no extra share of
the batch.

\subsection{What the Loss Sees of a Narrow Posterior}
\label{app:fm-sensitivity}

The posteriors of loud sources are orders of magnitude narrower than the prior in $x_f$ and
$x_{\fdot}$. A Gaussian model of one coordinate shows how much a loss rewards getting such a width
right: the velocity loss of standard flow matching hardly does, the endpoint likelihood of
\cref{eq:loss} does at every width.

\paragraph{Model.}
Take one coordinate with base $x_0\sim\Normal(0,s_0^2)$, posterior $x_1\sim\Normal(0,\sigma^2)$,
the straight path $x_s = (1-s)x_0 + s x_1$ and the target $u = x_1 - x_0$. With $\epsilon=1-s$ the
remaining path, the marginal is $x_s\sim\Normal(0,m^2)$ with $m^2 = \epsilon^2s_0^2 + s^2\sigma^2$,
and the optimal field is linear,
\begin{equation}
  v^\star(x,s) = g(s)\,x,\qquad
  g(s) = \frac{s\,\sigma^2 - \epsilon\,s_0^2}{m^2},\qquad
  V \equiv \Var(u\mid x_s) = \frac{s_0^2\sigma^2}{m^2}.
\end{equation}
A model that takes the posterior to be the wider $\Normal(0,\sigma_m^2)$ has the field $g_m(s)\,x$
and the conditional variance $V_m$, with $\sigma\to\sigma_m$ in $g$, $m$ and $V$. Under uniform
time the velocity loss pays, by the orthogonality of the regression residual, an excess over its
irreducible part $V$ of
\begin{equation}
  \Delta L_{\rm vel}(\sigma_m;\sigma) = \int_0^1 m^2\,\bigl[g_m - g\bigr]^2\,\dd\epsilon ,
  \label{eq:fm-excess}
\end{equation}
and \cref{eq:loss}, whose minimiser is $(g, V)$ by \cref{eq:fm-relative}, pays
\begin{equation}
  \Delta L_{\rm nll}(\sigma_m;\sigma) = \int_0^1 \Bigl[\frac{m^2(g_m-g)^2}{V_m}
  + \frac{V}{V_m} - 1 - \ln\frac{V}{V_m}\Bigr]\dd\epsilon .
  \label{eq:fm-excess-nll}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:fm-sensitivity}
For $\sigma\ll\sigma_m\ll s_0$,
\begin{equation}
  \Delta L_{\rm vel} \simeq \frac{\pi}{4}\,s_0\,\sigma_m ,
  \label{eq:fm-sensitivity-uniform}
\end{equation}
to leading order independent of the true width $\sigma$, and for $\sigma,\sigma_m\ll s_0$
\begin{equation}
  \Delta L_{\rm nll} \simeq \ln\frac{\sigma_m^2}{\sigma^2} + \frac{\sigma^2}{\sigma_m^2} - 1
  = 2\,\mathrm{KL}\bigl(\Normal(0,\sigma^2)\,\big\|\,\Normal(0,\sigma_m^2)\bigr),
  \label{eq:fm-sensitivity-nll}
\end{equation}
which depends on the widths only through $\sigma_m/\sigma$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Set $s_0=1$. Where $\epsilon\gg\sigma$, $m^2\simeq\epsilon^2$ and $g\simeq-1/\epsilon$, so up to a
relative $O(\epsilon)$
\begin{equation*}
  m^2(g_m-g)^2 \simeq \frac{\sigma_m^4}{(\epsilon^2+\sigma_m^2)^2},
\end{equation*}
and the region $\epsilon\lesssim\sigma$ contributes a relative $O(\sigma/\sigma_m)$. With
$\epsilon=\sigma_m z$, \cref{eq:fm-excess} is $\sigma_m\int_0^\infty(1+z^2)^{-2}\dd z =
\frac{\pi}{4}\sigma_m$. In \cref{eq:fm-excess-nll}, $V_m\simeq\sigma_m^2/(\epsilon^2+\sigma_m^2)$, so the
first term is $\simeq\sigma_m^2/(\epsilon^2+\sigma_m^2)$ and integrates to $\frac{\pi}{2}\sigma_m$.
Where $\epsilon\gg\sigma_m$, $V/V_m\simeq\sigma^2/\sigma_m^2$ up to a relative
$O(\sigma_m^2/\epsilon^2)$, and the rest of the integrand is the constant
\cref{eq:fm-sensitivity-nll}; the region $\epsilon\lesssim\sigma_m$ changes the integral by
$O(\sigma_m\ln(\sigma_m/\sigma))$. The factors of $s_0$ follow from dimensions: $\Delta L_{\rm vel}$
scales as $s_0^2$ and depends on $\sigma_m/s_0$ alone, $\Delta L_{\rm nll}$ is dimensionless.
\end{proof}

Quadrature of \cref{eq:fm-excess,eq:fm-excess-nll} confirms both: at $\sigma_m = 10^{-3}s_0$ and a
true posterior $10^4$ times narrower, the velocity loss's excess is \FlowMatchingCheckUniform{} of
\cref{eq:fm-sensitivity-uniform} and the likelihood's \FlowMatchingCheckNll{} of
\cref{eq:fm-sensitivity-nll} (\cref{fig:fm-sensitivity}a).

\paragraph{The velocity loss rewards absolute narrowness, weakly.}
Under the velocity loss the excess is linear in the model's width: halving a width of
$10^{-3}s_0$ is worth a hundredth of halving one of $10^{-1}s_0$. Nothing in the objective grows as
a narrow posterior approaches its true width, and the reward for the last decades of narrowing is
far below the scatter of a logged loss. Reweighting time towards the end of the path raises it, but
only down to the latest time the weighting reaches.

\paragraph{The likelihood rewards relative narrowness, everywhere.}
\Cref{eq:fm-sensitivity-nll} pays $2$ for every e-fold by which the model is too wide, whatever the
true width, until $\sigma_m$ nears $\sigma$; a model too narrow pays more still, through
$\sigma^2/\sigma_m^2$. The reward is collected along the whole path rather than at its end: wherever
$\epsilon\gg\sigma_m/s_0$, $V$ is the posterior variance itself, and the loss compares the model's
width with it directly. Because $\sigma$ is predicted per point, the comparison is made for each
source separately, not against a mean pooled over the batch.

\paragraph{Where the bands sit.}
The $x_f$ coordinate is uniform on $[-1,1]$ under the prior, so $s_0 = 1/\sqrt3$, and one unit of
$x_f$ spans \FlowMatchingNarrowBinsPerUnit{} frequency bins in the narrow band and
\FlowMatchingFullBinsPerUnit{} in the full band (\cref{app:chart}). For a sinusoid of unknown
amplitude and phase the Fisher bound on the frequency is $\sqrt3/(\pi\snr)$ bins. Over the visible
sources, from \ac{snr} \FlowMatchingSnrLow{} to the prior's \FlowMatchingSnrQuantile{}\% quantile
(\FlowMatchingNarrowSnrHigh{} in the narrow band, \FlowMatchingFullSnrHigh{} in the full band),
this gives an ideal $\sigma/s_0$ from
\num{\FlowMatchingNarrowLoud} to \num{\FlowMatchingNarrowWeak} in the narrow band and from
\num{\FlowMatchingFullLoud} to \num{\FlowMatchingFullWeak} in the full band (bars in
\cref{fig:fm-sensitivity}). The floor of the time input, $10^{\FlowMatchingTimeDecades{}}$, lies
below both ranges, so every ideal width is resolved before the last time the network can tell
apart.

The model is idealised: one coordinate, Gaussian base and posterior, and the simplest model error.
Its point is the scaling. A flow-matching loss on the velocity with uniform time is nearly blind to
the width of posteriors much narrower than the prior, which is the regime of loud \ac{gw} sources.
Other amortised estimators for \acp{gb} report a related symptom, frequency posteriors wider than
\ac{mcmc} at high frequency, over wide bands or with many components
\citep{delmond2026neural,houba2025slotflow}, and attribute it to the conditioning network; the
loss is a second candidate.

\subsection{Sampling}
\label{app:fm-sampling}

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics{figures/sampling-cost.pdf}
  \caption{Wall time to draw posterior samples of one window, against model size: the flow on
  one A100 (lines full band, rings narrow band; best of three windows), and the joint \ac{mcmc}
  reference over all sources of a window on two CPU cores (median line and range over the chains,
  one per injection). The arrow marks the gap at the B size.}
  \label{fig:fm-cost}
\end{figure}

To sample a window, $N$ base sets are drawn from the prior, carried over $K_t =
\FlowMatchingOdeSteps{}$ uniform midpoint steps of \cref{eq:fm-ode} with the velocity of
\cref{eq:fm-velocity}, and mapped back with $\varphi^{-1}$ (\cref{alg:fm-sample}). A step from $s$
to $s+\Delta s$ evaluates the velocity at its start, takes a half step to the midpoint and takes the
full step with the velocity there, so a draw costs $2K_t=\FlowMatchingEvaluations{}$ network
evaluations. The data stream of the network depends only on $(y,\fw)$, so it is encoded once per
window and serves every draw and every step (\cref{app:network}).

\paragraph{Cost.}
On one A100, \FlowMatchingCostDraws{} draws of one window take \qty{\FlowMatchingCostNarrow}{\second} with the
narrow-band B model and \qty{\FlowMatchingCostFull}{\second} with the full-band one, and
\FlowMatchingCostDrawsMore{} draws take \qty{\FlowMatchingCostFullMore}{\second}: the cost is linear in the
draws and almost independent of the band, since the network evaluations dominate and the data are
encoded once (\cref{fig:fm-cost}). Across sizes, \FlowMatchingCostDraws{} draws take
\qty{\FlowMatchingCostSmallest}{\second} (XS) to \qty{\FlowMatchingCostLargest}{\second} (full-band XL). The \ac{mcmc}
reference (\cref{app:mcmc}) runs one chain per injection over all the sources of its window, and a
chain takes \qtyrange{\FlowMatchingMcmcWindowLow}{\FlowMatchingMcmcWindowHigh}{\hour} on two CPU
cores. The flow is about $10^{\FlowMatchingMcmcGapDecades{}}$ times faster per window. The hardware
differs, so the ratio is a wall-time comparison, not one of compute; and the flow's cost is paid
once more in training (\cref{app:training}).

\paragraph{Resolution at the end of the path.}
The time grid is uniform, so the last step starts at $1-s = 1/K_t$ and evaluates its midpoint at
$1-s = \FlowMatchingLastMid{}$, where the velocity carries the draw to $2\hat X_1' - \hat X_1$, with
$\hat X_1$ and $\hat X_1'$ the means at the start and the midpoint. Where a posterior is much
narrower than $s_0/2K_t$, the exact means $\E[X_1\mid X_s]$ at both points hardly vary across
draws: they are close to the posterior mean, and the last step puts every draw near it. Run on the
exact Gaussian mean, the sampler returns \FlowMatchingWidthWide{} of the true width at $\sigma =
0.1\,s_0$, but \FlowMatchingWidthNarrowWeak{} of it at the narrow band's ideal width for \ac{snr}
\FlowMatchingSnrLow{} (\FlowMatchingWidthNarrowWeakMore{} with \FlowMatchingOdeStepsMore{} steps,
\FlowMatchingWidthNarrowWeakMost{} with \FlowMatchingOdeStepsMost{}) and
\num{\FlowMatchingWidthFullWeak} at the full band's (\FlowMatchingWidthFullWeakMost{} with
\FlowMatchingOdeStepsMost{} steps; \cref{fig:fm-sensitivity}b). A trained network is not the exact
mean, and errors that widen its posteriors partly offset this, so the effect on trained models
has to be measured.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
  \caption{One training step, written for one observation; the batch holds
  \FlowMatchingWindows{} observations of \FlowMatchingDraws{} points each.}
  \label{alg:fm-train}
  \begin{algorithmic}
    \STATE {\bfseries Input:} network weights $\eta$
    \STATE draw $\fw$ and $\theta_{1:S}\sim\pi(\cdot\mid\fw)$ (\cref{app:priors})
    \STATE simulate the data $d$ and form $y = y(d,\fw)$ (\cref{app:forward-model,app:noise})
    \STATE $X_1\leftarrow\varphi(\theta_{1:S};\fw)$;\ \ encode the data stream of $(y,\fw)$ once
    \FOR{each of the \FlowMatchingDraws{} points}
      \STATE $X_0\leftarrow\varphi(\theta'_{1:S};\fw)$, $\theta'_{1:S}\sim\pi(\cdot\mid\fw)$;\ \ $s$ from its stratum
      \STATE pair the sources of $X_0$ with those of $X_1$ in order of $f_0$ \hfill (\cref{eq:fm-coupling})
      \STATE $X_s\leftarrow X_0 + s(X_1 - X_0)$ \hfill (\cref{eq:fm-path})
      \STATE $(\hat X_1,\log\sigma)\leftarrow\mathrm{net}_\eta(X_s, s; y, \fw)$
    \ENDFOR
    \STATE take an optimiser step on \cref{eq:loss} in $\eta$ (\cref{app:training})
  \end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{algorithm}[t]
  \caption{Sampling the posterior of one window.}
  \label{alg:fm-sample}
  \begin{algorithmic}
    \STATE {\bfseries Input:} the input $y$ of a window $\fw$, number of draws $N$, steps $K_t=\FlowMatchingOdeSteps{}$
    \STATE encode the data stream of $(y,\fw)$ once;\ \ $\Delta s\leftarrow1/K_t$
    \STATE $\vnet(X,s) = \bigl(\hat X_{1,\eta}(X, s; y, \fw) - X\bigr)/(1-s)$ \hfill (\cref{eq:fm-velocity})
    \FOR{$n=1$ {\bfseries to} $N$ (in parallel)}
      \STATE $X\leftarrow\varphi(\theta_{1:S};\fw)$, $\theta_{1:S}\sim\pi(\cdot\mid\fw)$
      \FOR{$j=0$ {\bfseries to} $K_t-1$}
        \STATE $s\leftarrow j\Delta s$;\ \ $X'\leftarrow X + \tfrac{\Delta s}{2}\,\vnet(X, s)$
        \STATE $X\leftarrow X + \Delta s\,\vnet\bigl(X', s+\tfrac{\Delta s}{2}\bigr)$ \hfill (\cref{eq:fm-ode})
      \ENDFOR
      \STATE {\bfseries output} $\varphi^{-1}(X;\fw)$
    \ENDFOR
  \end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}
