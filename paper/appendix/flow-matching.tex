% =======================================================================================
\section{Flow Matching: Objective and Sampler}
\label{app:fm}
% =======================================================================================

The flow is trained on straight paths from prior draws to the true sources. The network predicts
the endpoint of the path and is trained on the implied velocity. A clock sends a share of the
training draws to the very end of the path, and learned weights balance the loss across time and
coordinates. \Cref{tab:fm-recipe} lists the choices. A Gaussian model of one coordinate
(\cref{app:fm-sensitivity}) shows why the end of the path matters for narrow posteriors, and what
the clock and the sampler can and cannot resolve there. The network is described in
\cref{app:network}, the optimiser in \cref{app:training}.

\subsection{Straight Paths from the Prior}
\label{app:fm-paths}

A window with centre frequency $\fw$ holds $S$ sources $\theta_{1:S}$, sorted loudest first,
and its data $d$ enter the network as the input $y = y(d,\fw)$ (\cref{app:network}). Write
$X = \varphi(\theta_{1:S};\fw)\in\R^{8S}$ for the sources in the flow coordinates of
\cref{eq:chart}, slot by slot. The target is the posterior $p(X\mid y,\fw)\propto p(d\mid
X)\,\pi(X\mid\fw)$, with the likelihood of \cref{eq:loglik} and the prior of \cref{app:priors}.
The flow transports draws $X(0)$ from the prior along
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\dd X}{\dd t} = \dot s(t)\,\vnet\bigl(X, t; y, \fw\bigr),\qquad t\in[0,1],
  \label{eq:fm-ode}
\end{equation}
so that $X(1)$ is a posterior draw. The clock $s(t)$ is described in \cref{app:fm-clock}; it
is the identity in the trained recipe ($p=\FlowMatchingTimePower{}$).

Training uses conditional flow matching \citep{lipman2023flow,liu2023flow,albergo2023building}.
One training observation draws $\fw$ and $\theta_{1:S}$ from the prior, simulates the data
(\cref{app:forward-model,app:noise}) and sets $X_1 = \varphi(\theta_{1:S};\fw)$. It then draws
\FlowMatchingDraws{} independent base points $X_0 = \varphi(\theta'_{1:S};\fw)$,
$\theta'_{1:S}\sim\pi(\cdot\mid\fw)$, each sorted loudest first and each with its own time; a
batch holds \FlowMatchingWindows{} observations, \FlowMatchingBatch{} points in all. The path is
the straight line, slot by slot,
\begin{equation}
  X_s = X_0 + s\,(X_1 - X_0),\qquad
  u = \frac{\dd X_s}{\dd s} = X_1 - X_0 .
  \label{eq:fm-path}
\end{equation}
Regressing a field on $u$ has the marginal field $v^\star(X,s;y,\fw) = \E[u\mid X_s = X,
y,\fw]$ as its minimiser, which carries $p_0$ to $p_1 = p(\cdot\mid y,\fw)$
\citep{lipman2023flow}. Because $X_0$ is independent of the data, $p_0$ is the prior itself. All
eight coordinates are flat (\cref{app:chart}), so the line needs no geodesics.

The prior is the natural base for two reasons. A source the data say nothing about, such as a
slot far below the noise, has a posterior close to its prior and needs almost no transport. And
every base draw is a valid set of sources in the window, so the path never leaves the region
where the network is trained. \Ac{fmpe} with a Gaussian base is studied by \citet{dax2023flow},
and has been applied to \ac{lisa} massive black-hole binaries \citep{liang2024rapid} and to a
single \ac{gb}-like source in gapped data \citep{mao2025robust}.

\subsection{Endpoint Prediction and the Loss}
\label{app:fm-loss}

\paragraph{Endpoint prediction.}
The network outputs an estimate $\hat X_1$ of the endpoint, and the velocity is the one that
reaches it in the remaining path,
\begin{equation}
  \vnet = \frac{\hat X_1 - X_s}{1-s},
  \qquad
  e = \frac{\hat X_1 - X_1}{\max\bigl(1-s,\,10^{\FlowMatchingLossDecades{}}\bigr)},
  \label{eq:fm-error}
\end{equation}
where $e = \vnet - u$ is the velocity error the loss is computed on. The output is a position in
flow coordinates, whose scale is set by the chart and does not change along the path, while the
velocity grows as $1/(1-s)$ near the end, where a narrow posterior is resolved
(\cref{app:fm-sensitivity}). The loss stays a velocity loss, so the minimiser is still $v^\star$
and the end of the path keeps its weight; endpoint prediction converted to a velocity is also used
on structured data \citep{stark2024harmonic}. The floor $10^{\FlowMatchingLossDecades{}}$ is the
lower edge of the last bin of the learned weights, below which no weight could absorb the
division. Only the uniform part of the clock reaches below it, a share of
\num{\FlowMatchingShareBelowLossFloor} of the draws.

\paragraph{Learned loss weights.}
The size of $e$ changes by orders of magnitude along the path and between coordinates: a fresh
network's endpoint error is of order one everywhere, so its $e^2$ grows as $(1-s)^{-2}$, and a
trained network contracts frequency and chirp mass far more than the phases. Unweighted, the
largest of these errors would set the gradient. Following \citet{kendall2018multi} and
\citet{karras2024analyzing}, the objective learns one log-variance $\lambda_{b,g}$ per time bin
$b$ and coordinate group $g$,
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal L = \E\;\frac{1}{G}\sum_{g=1}^{G}
  \Bigl[\bar e_g\,e^{-\lambda_{b(s),g}} + \lambda_{b(s),g}\Bigr],
  \qquad
  \bar e_g = \frac{1}{S\,\lvert g\rvert}\sum_{k=1}^{S}\sum_{i\in g} e_{k,i}^2 ,
  \label{eq:loss}
\end{equation}
with the expectation over the batch's observations and draws. The $G=\FlowMatchingGroups{}$
groups are $x_f$, $x_{\Mc}$, the sky $(x_l,x_b)$, $z_+$ and $z_-$, each pooled over the slots,
and $b(s)$ is one of \FlowMatchingLossBins{} equal bins of $\log_{10}(1-s)$ on
$[\FlowMatchingLossDecades{},0]$, $\FlowMatchingLossBinWidth{}$ of a decade each, with times
outside clipped to the end bins. For fixed weights the minimiser is unchanged. At the optimum in
$\lambda$, $e^{\lambda_{b,g}}$ is the bin's mean of $\bar e_g$, so each bin and group contributes
the logarithm of its mean error and its gradient is its error relative to that mean.

The weights start at the error of a fresh network, $\lambda_{b,g} = -2\ln(1-s_b)$ at the bin
centre $s_b$, so $e^{-\lambda}$ undoes the $(1-s)^{-2}$ of \cref{eq:fm-error} from the first
step. Started at zero, the late bins would need tens of units of travel in $\lambda$ to match
their error, and would dominate the gradient meanwhile. The weights are optimised with the network
in both bands (\cref{app:training}).

\subsection{The Clock}
\label{app:fm-clock}

\paragraph{Stratified time.}
Each point's time is $t\sim\Unif(0,1)$, stratified over the $B=\FlowMatchingBatch{}$ points of a
batch: with $\pi$ a random permutation, point $i$ gets $t_i = (\pi_i + U_i)/B$,
$U_i\sim\Unif(0,1)$. Every stratum of $t$ is then covered in every batch, which lowers the
variance of the batch loss at no cost, as the low-discrepancy sampler of variational diffusion
models does \citep{kingma2021variational}. The point sits at
\begin{equation}
  s(t) = 1 - (1-t)^p
  \label{eq:fm-warp}
\end{equation}
along the path, with the target $u=\dd X_s/\dd s$ and the factor $\dot s$ of \cref{eq:fm-ode}
applied by the sampler; the trained recipe keeps $p=\FlowMatchingTimePower{}$. The network sees
$t$ and $\log_{10}(1-t)$, the latter clipped to $[\FlowMatchingTimeDecades{},0]$
(\cref{app:network}), so it can tell apart times that differ only in how close they are to the
end.

\paragraph{The late-time mix.}
With probability $\FlowMatchingLateMix{}$, independently for each point, the time is replaced so
that $1-s = 10^{\FlowMatchingLateFloor{}\,u}$ for the point's stratified uniform $u$: the
remaining path is log-uniform on $[10^{\FlowMatchingLateFloor{}},1]$. Each decade of $1-s$ above
the floor receives $\FlowMatchingPerDecade{}$ of all points, so \qty{\FlowMatchingShareHundredth}{\percent} of
them have $1-s<10^{-2}$ and \qty{\FlowMatchingShareThousandth}{\percent} have $1-s<10^{-3}$, against \qtylist{1;0.1}{\percent} under the uniform clock. Below the floor only the uniform half reaches, a share of
\num{\FlowMatchingShareBelowFloor} (\cref{fig:fm-sensitivity}a). Only the weighting over $s$
changes, so the minimiser does not. Non-uniform time sampling is standard for diffusion models
\citep{karras2022elucidating,kingma2023understanding,esser2024scaling}, which concentrate it in the
middle of the path, and \ac{fmpe} up-weights late times with a power law in $t$
\citep[Sec.~3.3]{dax2023flow}. Here the weight goes to the last decades of the path, which a power
law with bounded density does not reach.

\paragraph{Why a mixture.}
A power clock alone also moves points to the end, but takes them from the start of the path, where
the flow settles the coarse structure of the window: how many slots hold a detectable source and
roughly where. With $p=3$ only \qty{\FlowMatchingFirstHalfWarp}{\percent} of the points fall in the first half of
the path ($s<\tfrac12$), against \qty{50}{\percent} for the uniform clock; the mixture keeps
\qty{\FlowMatchingFirstHalfMix}{\percent}. The price is depth: $p=3$ puts \qty{\FlowMatchingBelowFloorWarp}{\percent} of the
points below $1-s=10^{\FlowMatchingLateFloor{}}$, where the mixture puts almost none
(\cref{fig:fm-sensitivity}a).

\subsection{Self-Conditioning}
\label{app:fm-selfcond}

The network can also take its own earlier estimate of the endpoint as an input
\citep{chen2023analog,stark2024harmonic}. At each training step one coin, with probability
$\FlowMatchingSelfCondition{}$, decides for the whole batch whether a first pass without an estimate
runs and its $\hat X_1$, with gradients stopped, is fed to the trained second pass; otherwise the
single trained pass sees no estimate. Both passes share one encoding of the data. The sampler always
feeds the latest estimate (\cref{alg:fm-sample}), which enters through its own embedding
(\cref{app:network}). The network can then refine a coarse estimate instead of recomputing it from
the noisy $X_s$, at the cost of one extra evaluation on half the training steps and one per draw at
sampling.

\subsection{What the Loss Sees of a Narrow Posterior}
\label{app:fm-sensitivity}

The posteriors of loud sources are orders of magnitude narrower than the prior in $x_f$ and
$x_{\Mc}$. A Gaussian model of one coordinate shows how much the flow-matching loss rewards getting
such a width right, and where along the path the reward sits.

\paragraph{Model.}
Take one coordinate with base $x_0\sim\Normal(0,s_0^2)$, posterior $x_1\sim\Normal(0,\sigma^2)$,
the straight path $x_s = (1-s)x_0 + s x_1$ and the target $u = x_1 - x_0$. With $\epsilon=1-s$ the
remaining path, the marginal is $x_s\sim\Normal(0,m^2)$ with $m^2 = \epsilon^2s_0^2 + s^2\sigma^2$,
and the optimal field is linear,
\begin{equation}
  v^\star(x,s) = g(s)\,x,\qquad
  g(s) = \frac{s\,\sigma^2 - \epsilon\,s_0^2}{m^2},\qquad
  \Var(u\mid x_s) = \frac{s_0^2\sigma^2}{m^2}.
\end{equation}
A model whose field is that of a wider posterior $\Normal(0,\sigma_m^2)$, the field $g_m(s)\,x$ with
$\sigma\to\sigma_m$ in $g$ and $m$, pays an excess over the irreducible loss $\Var(u\mid x_s)$. By
the orthogonality of the regression residual, under a clock that puts the remaining path at density
$q(\epsilon)$,
\begin{equation}
  \Delta L(\sigma_m;\sigma) = \int_0^1 m^2\,\bigl[g_m - g\bigr]^2\,q(\epsilon)\,\dd\epsilon .
  \label{eq:fm-excess}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:fm-sensitivity}
For $\sigma\ll\sigma_m\ll s_0$, the uniform clock ($q=1$) gives
\begin{equation}
  \Delta L_{\rm unif} \simeq \frac{\pi}{4}\,s_0\,\sigma_m ,
  \label{eq:fm-sensitivity-uniform}
\end{equation}
and, if also $\epsilon_f\ll\sigma_m/s_0$, the late-time mix (share $\mu$ log-uniform on
$[\epsilon_f,1]$, the rest uniform) gives
\begin{equation}
  \Delta L_{\rm mix} \simeq (1-\mu)\,\frac{\pi}{4}\,s_0\,\sigma_m
  + \mu\,s_0^2\,\frac{\ln\bigl(\sigma_m/s_0\epsilon_f\bigr) - \tfrac12}{\ln(1/\epsilon_f)} .
  \label{eq:fm-sensitivity-mix}
\end{equation}
To leading order neither depends on the true width $\sigma$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Set $s_0=1$. Where $\epsilon\gg\sigma$, $m^2\simeq\epsilon^2$ and $g\simeq-1/\epsilon$, so up to a
relative $O(\epsilon)$
\begin{equation*}
  m^2(g_m-g)^2 \simeq \frac{\sigma_m^4}{(\epsilon^2+\sigma_m^2)^2},
\end{equation*}
and the region $\epsilon\lesssim\sigma$ contributes a relative $O(\sigma/\sigma_m)$. With
$\epsilon=\sigma_m z$, the uniform part is $\sigma_m\int_0^\infty(1+z^2)^{-2}\dd z =
\frac{\pi}{4}\sigma_m$. The log-uniform part has density $1/(\epsilon\ln(1/\epsilon_f))$ on
$[\epsilon_f,1]$ and gives $\ln(1/\epsilon_f)^{-1}\int_{\epsilon_f/\sigma_m}^{1/\sigma_m}
z^{-1}(1+z^2)^{-2}\,\dd z$, and $\int_a^\infty z^{-1}(1+z^2)^{-2}\dd z = \ln(1/a) - \frac12 +
O(a^2)$. The factors of $s_0$ follow from dimensions: $\Delta L$ scales as $s_0^2$ and depends on
$\sigma_m/s_0$ alone.
\end{proof}

Quadrature of \cref{eq:fm-excess} confirms both: at $\sigma_m = 10^{-3}s_0$ the uniform clock's
excess is \FlowMatchingCheckUniform{} of \cref{eq:fm-sensitivity-uniform}, and at
$\sigma_m=10^{-2}s_0$ the mix's is \FlowMatchingCheckMix{} of \cref{eq:fm-sensitivity-mix}, both for
a true posterior $10^4$ times narrower than the model (\cref{fig:fm-sensitivity}b).

\paragraph{The uniform clock rewards absolute narrowness, weakly.}
Under the uniform clock the excess is linear in the model's width: halving a width of $10^{-3}s_0$
is worth a hundredth of halving one of $10^{-1}s_0$. Nothing in the objective grows as a narrow
posterior approaches its true width, and the reward for the last decades of narrowing is far below
the scatter of a logged loss.

\paragraph{The mix rewards relative narrowness, down to its floor.}
The log-uniform part of \cref{eq:fm-sensitivity-mix} pays the same for every e-fold of narrowing,
$\mu s_0^2/\ln(1/\epsilon_f)$, as long as the model is wider than $s_0\epsilon_f$. At $\sigma_m =
10^{-3}s_0$ one e-fold of narrowing lowers the loss $\FlowMatchingMixGain{}$ times more under the mix
than under the uniform clock. The reward follows the model's width, not the true one: a posterior of
width $\sigma$ is only resolved at $1-s\lesssim\sigma/s_0$, and below the floor $\epsilon_f =
10^{\FlowMatchingLateFloor{}}$ only the uniform part remains, with its linear reward.

\paragraph{Where the bands sit.}
The $x_f$ coordinate is uniform on $[-1,1]$ under the prior, so $s_0 = 1/\sqrt3$, and one unit of
$x_f$ spans \FlowMatchingNarrowBinsPerUnit{} frequency bins in the narrow band and
\FlowMatchingFullBinsPerUnit{} in the full band (\cref{app:chart}). For a sinusoid of unknown
amplitude and phase the Fisher bound on the frequency is $\sqrt3/(\pi\snr)$ bins, which over the
\ac{snr} range $[\FlowMatchingSnrLow{},\FlowMatchingSnrHigh{}]$ gives an ideal $\sigma/s_0$ from
\num{\FlowMatchingNarrowLoud} to \num{\FlowMatchingNarrowWeak} in the narrow band and from
\num{\FlowMatchingFullLoud} to \num{\FlowMatchingFullWeak} in the full band (bars in
\cref{fig:fm-sensitivity}). The mix's floor $10^{\FlowMatchingLateFloor{}}$ thus lies inside the
narrow band's range and above the whole of the full band's, and the floors of the time input and of
\cref{eq:fm-error}, $10^{\FlowMatchingLossDecades{}}$, lie inside the full band's range. In the full
band the mix stops rewarding narrowing before any source reaches its ideal $f_0$ width, and the
frequency posteriors of its loudest sources are resolved only past the last time the network can
tell apart.

\paragraph{What the learned weights change.}
At the optimum in $\lambda$, \cref{eq:loss} divides each bin's error by the mean error of that bin
and group. The mean is pooled over every observation, slot and \ac{snr} in the batch, and at the end
of the path it is dominated by the irreducible part $\Var(u\mid x_s)$ of the sources whose
posteriors are still wide there. The weights therefore keep the late bins from being drowned by the
early ones, but they do not make the objective relative for each source: one loud source's excess
is still divided by a pooled mean, not by its own error.

The model is idealised: one coordinate, Gaussian base and posterior, and the simplest model error.
Its point is the scaling. A flow-matching loss with uniform time is nearly blind to the width of
posteriors much narrower than the prior, which is the regime of loud \ac{gw} sources. Other amortised
estimators for \acp{gb} report a related symptom, frequency posteriors wider than \ac{mcmc} at high
frequency, over wide bands or with many components \citep{delmond2026neural,houba2025slotflow}, and
attribute it to the conditioning network; the loss is a second candidate.

\subsection{Sampling}
\label{app:fm-sampling}

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics{figures/sampling-cost.pdf}
  \caption{Wall time to draw posterior samples of one window, against model size: the flow on
  one A100 (lines full band, rings narrow band; best of three windows), and the \ac{mcmc}
  reference on two CPU cores (median line and range over chains), for one source with the others
  fixed and for all sources jointly. The arrow marks the gap at the B size.}
  \label{fig:fm-cost}
\end{figure}

To sample a window, $N$ base sets are drawn from the prior, transported over $K_t =
\FlowMatchingOdeSteps{}$ uniform steps in $t$ and mapped back with $\varphi^{-1}$
(\cref{alg:fm-sample}). With $r(t) = (1-t)^p$ the remaining path, a step from $t$ to $t'$ that holds
the endpoint estimate $\hat X_1$ fixed moves to
\begin{equation}
  X \leftarrow X + \Bigl(1 - \frac{r(t')}{r(t)}\Bigr)\bigl(\hat X_1 - X\bigr),
  \label{eq:fm-x-step}
\end{equation}
the exact solution of \cref{eq:fm-ode,eq:fm-error} for a constant $\hat X_1$. Each step evaluates
$\hat X_1$ at its start, takes a half step to the midpoint, evaluates $\hat X_1$ there and takes the
full step with that estimate; the last step lands on its estimate. A draw costs
$2K_t+1=\FlowMatchingEvaluations{}$ network evaluations, the extra one being the first
self-conditioning estimate at $t=0$. The data stream of the network depends only on $(y,\fw)$, so
it is encoded once per window and serves every draw and every step (\cref{app:network}).

\paragraph{Cost.}
On one A100, \FlowMatchingCostDraws{} draws of one window take \qty{\FlowMatchingCostNarrow}{\second} with the
narrow-band B model and \qty{\FlowMatchingCostFull}{\second} with the full-band one, and
\FlowMatchingCostDrawsMore{} draws take \qty{\FlowMatchingCostFullMore}{\second}: the cost is linear in the
draws and almost independent of the band, since the network evaluations dominate and the data are
encoded once (\cref{fig:fm-cost}). Across sizes, \FlowMatchingCostDraws{} draws take
\qty{\FlowMatchingCostSmallest}{\second} (XS) to \qty{\FlowMatchingCostLargest}{\second} (full-band XL). The \ac{mcmc}
reference (\cref{app:mcmc}) takes \qtyrange{\FlowMatchingMcmcSourceLow}{\FlowMatchingMcmcSourceHigh}{\minute}
per source and chain when it samples one source with the others fixed, and
\qtyrange{\FlowMatchingMcmcWindowLow}{\FlowMatchingMcmcWindowHigh}{\hour} per window and chain when it samples
all sources jointly, on two CPU cores. The flow is about $10^{\FlowMatchingMcmcGapDecades{}}$ times
faster per window. The hardware differs, so the ratio is a wall-time comparison, not one of
compute; and the flow's cost is paid once more in training (\cref{app:training}).

\paragraph{Resolution at the end of the path.}
The time grid is uniform in $t$, so the last step starts at $1-s = 1/K_t$ and lands on the estimate
made at its midpoint, $1-s = \FlowMatchingLastMid{}$. Where a posterior is much narrower than
$s_0/2K_t$, the exact endpoint $\E[X_1\mid X_s]$ at that midpoint hardly varies across draws: it is
close to the posterior mean, and the last step puts every draw near it. Run on the exact Gaussian
endpoint, the sampler returns \FlowMatchingWidthWide{} of the true width at $\sigma = 0.1\,s_0$, but
\FlowMatchingWidthNarrowWeak{} of it at the narrow band's ideal width for \ac{snr}
\FlowMatchingSnrLow{} (\FlowMatchingWidthNarrowWeakMore{} with \FlowMatchingOdeStepsMore{} steps,
\FlowMatchingWidthNarrowWeakMost{} with \FlowMatchingOdeStepsMost{}) and
\num{\FlowMatchingWidthFullWeak} at the full band's (\FlowMatchingWidthFullWeakMost{} with
\FlowMatchingOdeStepsMost{} steps; \cref{fig:fm-sensitivity}c). A trained network is not the exact
endpoint, and errors that widen its posteriors partly offset this, so the effect on trained models
has to be measured.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
  \caption{One training step, written for one observation; the batch holds
  \FlowMatchingWindows{} observations of \FlowMatchingDraws{} points each.}
  \label{alg:fm-train}
  \begin{algorithmic}
    \STATE {\bfseries Input:} network weights $\eta$ and loss log-variances $\lambda$
    \STATE draw $\fw$ and $\theta_{1:S}\sim\pi(\cdot\mid\fw)$, loudest first (\cref{app:priors})
    \STATE simulate the data $d$ and form $y = y(d,\fw)$ (\cref{app:forward-model,app:noise})
    \STATE $X_1\leftarrow\varphi(\theta_{1:S};\fw)$;\ \ encode the data stream of $(y,\fw)$ once
    \FOR{each of the \FlowMatchingDraws{} points}
      \STATE $X_0\leftarrow\varphi(\theta'_{1:S};\fw)$, $\theta'_{1:S}\sim\pi(\cdot\mid\fw)$;\ \ $u$ from its stratum
      \STATE with probability $\FlowMatchingLateMix{}$: $1-s\leftarrow10^{\FlowMatchingLateFloor{}u}$;\ \ else $s\leftarrow1-(1-u)^p$ \hfill (\cref{eq:fm-warp})
      \STATE $X_s\leftarrow X_0 + s(X_1 - X_0)$ \hfill (\cref{eq:fm-path})
    \ENDFOR
    \STATE once per step, with probability $\FlowMatchingSelfCondition{}$: $\hat X\leftarrow\operatorname{sg}\bigl[\hat X_{1,\eta}(X_s, t; y, \fw)\bigr]$, else none
    \STATE $\hat X_1\leftarrow\hat X_{1,\eta}(X_s, t; y, \fw, \hat X)$;\ \ $e\leftarrow(\hat X_1 - X_1)/\max(1-s, 10^{\FlowMatchingLossDecades{}})$ \hfill (\cref{eq:fm-error})
    \STATE take an optimiser step on \cref{eq:loss} in $(\eta,\lambda)$ (\cref{app:training})
  \end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{algorithm}[t]
  \caption{Sampling the posterior of one window.}
  \label{alg:fm-sample}
  \begin{algorithmic}
    \STATE {\bfseries Input:} the input $y$ of a window $\fw$, number of draws $N$, steps $K_t=\FlowMatchingOdeSteps{}$
    \STATE encode the data stream of $(y,\fw)$ once;\ \ $\Delta t\leftarrow1/K_t$;\ \ $r(t) = (1-t)^p$
    \FOR{$n=1$ {\bfseries to} $N$ (in parallel)}
      \STATE $X\leftarrow\varphi(\theta_{1:S};\fw)$, $\theta_{1:S}\sim\pi(\cdot\mid\fw)$
      \STATE $\hat X\leftarrow\hat X_{1,\eta}(X, 0; y, \fw)$
      \FOR{$j=0$ {\bfseries to} $K_t-1$}
        \STATE $t\leftarrow j\Delta t$;\ \ $t'\leftarrow t+\tfrac{\Delta t}{2}$
        \STATE $\hat X\leftarrow\hat X_{1,\eta}(X, t; y, \fw, \hat X)$;\ \
               $X'\leftarrow X + \bigl(1-\tfrac{r(t')}{r(t)}\bigr)(\hat X - X)$
        \STATE $\hat X\leftarrow\hat X_{1,\eta}(X', t'; y, \fw, \hat X)$;\ \
               $X\leftarrow X + \bigl(1-\tfrac{r(t+\Delta t)}{r(t)}\bigr)(\hat X - X)$ \hfill (\cref{eq:fm-x-step})
      \ENDFOR
      \STATE {\bfseries output} $\varphi^{-1}(X;\fw)$
    \ENDFOR
  \end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}
