% =======================================================================================
\section{Flow Matching: Objective and Sampler}
\label{app:fm}
% =======================================================================================

The posterior is learned in two parts. From the data alone, the network first returns a Gaussian
frame per source, a mean $\mu$ and a standard deviation $\sigma$ for each coordinate, which places
and scales the posterior. A flow then carries a standard normal to the posterior in that frame,
$Z = (X-\mu)/\sigma$, on straight paths: it predicts a Gaussian over the endpoint $Z_1$, its mean
and width per coordinate, and is trained on the endpoint's negative log-likelihood, the frame on
that of $X_1$. The sampler follows the velocity to the predicted mean and maps the draw back with
$X = \mu + \sigma Z$. \Cref{tab:fm-recipe} lists the choices. A Gaussian model of one coordinate
(\cref{app:fm-sensitivity}) shows why the likelihood, unlike the velocity loss, rewards narrow
posteriors, and why a flow run on the prior's scale cannot resolve them at the end of the path,
while one run in the frame can. The network is described in \cref{app:network}, the optimiser in
\cref{app:training}.

\subsection{Straight Paths in the Frame}
\label{app:fm-paths}

A window with centre frequency $\fw$ holds $S$ sources $\theta_{1:S}$, and its data $d$ enter the
network as the input $y = y(d,\fw)$ (\cref{app:network}). Write $X = \varphi(\theta_{1:S};\fw)\in
\R^{8S}$ for the sources in the flow coordinates of \cref{eq:chart}, source by source, listed in
order of $f_0$ (\cref{app:chart-slots}). The target is the posterior $p(X\mid y,\fw)\propto p(d\mid
X)\,\pi(X\mid\fw)$ over ordered sources, with the likelihood of \cref{eq:loglik} and the prior of
\cref{app:priors}. Given the frame $(\mu,\sigma)$ of the window (\cref{app:fm-frame}), a function
of $(y,\fw)$ alone, the flow transports standard normal draws $Z(0)$ along
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\dd Z}{\dd s} = \vnet\bigl(Z, s; y, \fw\bigr),\qquad s\in[0,1],
  \label{eq:fm-ode}
\end{equation}
so that $X = \mu + \sigma Z(1)$ is a posterior draw, elementwise. The position $s$ along the path is
the network's time input, written $t$ in \cref{app:network}.

Training uses conditional flow matching \citep{lipman2023flow,liu2023flow,albergo2023building}.
One training observation draws $\fw$ and $\theta_{1:S}$ from the prior, simulates the data
(\cref{app:forward-model,app:noise}) and sets $X_1 = \varphi(\theta_{1:S};\fw)$, its sources in
order of $f_0$, and $Z_1 = (X_1-\mu)/\sigma$ in the frame the network reads off the data. It then
draws \FlowMatchingDraws{} independent base points $Z_0\sim\Normal(0,I)$, each with its own time; a
batch holds \FlowMatchingWindows{} observations, \FlowMatchingBatch{} points in all. The path is the
straight line
\begin{equation}
  Z_s = Z_0 + s\,(Z_1 - Z_0),\qquad
  u = \frac{\dd Z_s}{\dd s} = Z_1 - Z_0 .
  \label{eq:fm-path}
\end{equation}
Regressing a field on $u$ has the marginal field $v^\star(Z,s;y,\fw) = \E[u\mid Z_s = Z, y,\fw]$ as
its minimiser, which carries $\Normal(0,I)$ to the posterior of $Z_1$ \citep{lipman2023flow}. The
frame is an affine map per coordinate, so straight lines in $Z$ are straight lines in $X$, and all
eight coordinates are flat (\cref{app:chart}): the path needs no geodesics. \Ac{fmpe} with a
Gaussian base is studied by \citet{dax2023flow}, and has been applied to \ac{lisa} massive
black-hole binaries \citep{liang2024rapid} and to a single \ac{gb}-like source in gapped data
\citep{mao2025robust}; here the Gaussian is placed and scaled by the data.

\subsection{The Frame}
\label{app:fm-frame}

The frame is the network's first output (\cref{app:network-frame}): for each of the $S$ slots and
each coordinate, a shift and a log scale $(\delta_\mu,\delta_s)$ around the prior's moments,
\begin{equation}
  \mu = \mu_\pi + \sigma_\pi\,\delta_\mu,\qquad \log\sigma = \log\sigma_\pi + \delta_s,
  \label{eq:fm-frame}
\end{equation}
with $(\mu_\pi,\sigma_\pi)$ the mean and standard deviation of the slot's coordinate under the prior,
sources in order of $f_0$. For $x_f$ these are the moments of the $k$-th of $S$ uniform order
statistics on $[-1,1]$, mean $2k/(S+1)-1$ and standard deviation
$2\sqrt{k(S+1-k)/((S+1)^2(S+2))}$; $x_{\fdot}$ and the sky are standardised by the chart and have
$(0,1)$; for $z_\pm$ the mean is zero and the variance $\E\lvert z_\pm\rvert^2/2$, by quadrature over
the amplitude prior at the window's centre and the inclination. The frame head starts at zero, so an
untrained network's frame is the prior's.

\paragraph{Loss.}
The frame is trained on the Gaussian negative log-likelihood of $X_1$, up to a constant and a
factor of two, with a weight and a ridge,
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal L_{\rm frame} = \E\;\frac{1}{8S}\sum_{k=1}^{S}\sum_{i=1}^{8}
  \operatorname{sg}\bigl(e^{\delta_{s,ki}}\bigr)\Bigl[\kappa_{ki}^2 + 2\delta_{s,ki}
  + \lambda\bigl(\delta_{\mu,ki}^2 + \delta_{s,ki}^2\bigr)\Bigr],\qquad
  \kappa = \frac{X_1-\mu}{\sigma},
  \label{eq:fm-frame-loss}
\end{equation}
with $\operatorname{sg}$ the stop-gradient and $\lambda = \FlowMatchingFrameDecay{}$. The frame
depends on $(y,\fw)$ alone, so the weight is the same for every $X_1$ the posterior holds and does
not move the minimiser of each observation's term: up to the ridge, $\mu = \E[X_1\mid y,\fw]$ and
$\sigma^2 = \Var(X_1\mid y,\fw)$, the posterior's marginal moments. What the weight changes is the
balance across observations. The gradient of $\kappa^2$ in $\delta_\mu$ is $-2\kappa\,e^{-\delta_s}$,
which grows as the frame narrows: a loud source, whose frame is many decades narrower than the
prior, would dominate the batch. With the weight it is $-2\kappa$, the same for every source in
standardised units. The ridge keeps $\delta$ finite where the batch holds no information, and is
small against the likelihood wherever it does.

\paragraph{Why a frame.}
The posterior widths span many decades, from the prior for a quiet source to a small fraction of a
bin in $f_0$ for a loud one (\cref{app:fm-sensitivity}). A flow from a base on the prior's scale
has to resolve the narrow end in the last steps of its path, where a uniform sampler grid collapses
it (\cref{app:fm-sampling}). In the frame, the posterior of $Z_1$ has mean near zero and variance near
one for every source, loud or quiet, so the flow transports order-one widths along its whole path
and learns what a Gaussian per coordinate misses: correlations, tails and multimodality, such as
the sky's antipodal modes and the edge where two close sources swap their order. The flow is
trained on $Z_1 = \operatorname{sg}(\kappa)$ and reads the frame through a stop-gradient
(\cref{app:network}), so it cannot move the frame to make its own task easier; the frame stays the
Gaussian moment match of \cref{eq:fm-frame-loss}.

\subsection{The Endpoint Distribution and the Loss}
\label{app:fm-loss}

\paragraph{Output.}
For each source and coordinate the flow outputs the mean $\hat Z_1$ and the log standard deviation
$\log\tau$ of a Gaussian over the endpoint, given the point on the path, and the velocity is the one
that reaches the mean in the remaining path,
\begin{equation}
  \vnet = \frac{\hat Z_1 - Z_s}{1-s}.
  \label{eq:fm-velocity}
\end{equation}
The output is a position in the frame, whose scale does not change along the path, while the
velocity grows as $1/(1-s)$ near the end (\cref{app:fm-sensitivity}); endpoint prediction converted
to a velocity is also used on structured data \citep{stark2024harmonic}. The width $\tau$ serves the
loss only; the sampler follows the mean.

\paragraph{Loss.}
The flow's objective is the Gaussian negative log-likelihood of the endpoint, up to a constant and
a factor of two, and the network minimises the sum
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal L = \mathcal L_{\rm frame} + \E\;\frac{1}{8S}\sum_{k=1}^{S}\sum_{i=1}^{8}
  \Bigl[\Bigl(\frac{Z_{1,ki}-\hat Z_{1,ki}}{\tau_{ki}}\Bigr)^2 + 2\log\tau_{ki}\Bigr],
  \label{eq:loss}
\end{equation}
with the expectation over the batch's observations, draws and times. The two terms share the
network but not their gradients: the frame sees only the first, the flow only the second. The
second term's minimiser is the conditional mean and variance of each coordinate, $\hat Z_1 =
\E[Z_1\mid Z_s,y,\fw]$ and $\tau^2 = \Var(Z_1\mid Z_s,y,\fw)$, so the velocity of
\cref{eq:fm-velocity} is the marginal field $v^\star$. On the straight path $Z_1 - Z_s = (1-s)\,u$,
so $\hat Z_1 - Z_1 = (1-s)(\vnet - u)$ and $\Var(Z_1\mid Z_s) = (1-s)^2\Var(u\mid Z_s)$: at the
optimal $\tau$ each term is, up to a constant,
\begin{equation}
  \frac{(\vnet - u)^2}{\Var(u\mid Z_s,y,\fw)},
  \label{eq:fm-relative}
\end{equation}
a velocity loss divided by its own irreducible part, for every point, coordinate and source. The
factors of $1-s$ cancel, and a source the data pin down weighs as much as one whose posterior is
its prior, with no weights over time or coordinates. The learned $\tau$ acts as the learned task
uncertainties of \citet{kendall2018multi}, one per point rather than one per task.

\paragraph{Diagnostics.}
The loss is logged with its two parts, the frame's likelihood $\kappa^2 + 2\delta_s$ unweighted and
the flow's. On the evaluation catalog the same quantities are binned (\cref{app:training}) over
$G=\FlowMatchingGroups{}$ coordinate groups, $x_f$, $x_{\fdot}$, the sky $(x_l,x_b)$, $z_+$ and
$z_-$, each pooled over the sources. The frame is binned by each source's own \ac{snr},
\FlowMatchingSnrBins{} equal bins of $\log_{10}\snr$ on $[-1,3]$: per bin and group the mean of
$\log_{10}(\sigma/\sigma_\pi)$, how far the frame has narrowed, and of $\kappa^2$, which is one where
the frame is calibrated; and the frame's $f_0$ width in bins over the Fisher width
$\sqrt3/(\pi\snr)$ of \cref{app:fm-sensitivity}. The flow is binned by time,
\FlowMatchingLossBins{} equal bins of $\log_{10}(1-s)$ on $[\FlowMatchingLossDecades{},0]$,
$\FlowMatchingLossBinWidth{}$ of a decade each: per bin and group the mean of $\log_{10}\tau$ and of
$\bigl((Z_1-\hat Z_1)/\tau\bigr)^2$.

\subsection{Stratified Time}
\label{app:fm-clock}

Each point's time is $s\sim\Unif(0,1)$, stratified over the $B=\FlowMatchingBatch{}$ points of a
batch: with $\pi$ a random permutation, point $i$ gets $s_i = (\pi_i + U_i)/B$,
$U_i\sim\Unif(0,1)$. Every stratum of $s$ is then covered in every batch, which lowers the
variance of the batch loss at no cost, as the low-discrepancy sampler of variational diffusion
models does \citep{kingma2021variational}. The network sees $s$ and $\log_{10}(1-s)$, the latter
clipped to $[\FlowMatchingTimeDecades{},0]$ (\cref{app:network}), so it can tell apart times that
differ only in how close they are to the end.

Non-uniform time sampling is standard for diffusion models
\citep{karras2022elucidating,kingma2023understanding,esser2024scaling}, and \ac{fmpe} up-weights late
times with a power law in $t$ \citep[Sec.~3.3]{dax2023flow}. Here time stays uniform: under
\cref{eq:loss} the reward for narrowing a posterior does not fade with its width and is collected
along the whole path (\cref{app:fm-sensitivity}), so the end of the path needs no extra share of
the batch.

\subsection{What the Loss Sees of a Narrow Posterior}
\label{app:fm-sensitivity}

The posteriors of loud sources are orders of magnitude narrower than the prior in $x_f$ and
$x_{\fdot}$. A Gaussian model of one coordinate shows how much a loss rewards getting such a width
right, and how well a sampler resolves it: the velocity loss of standard flow matching hardly
rewards it, the endpoint likelihood of \cref{eq:loss} does at every width, and a uniform sampler
grid resolves it only once the frame has divided it out.

\paragraph{Model.}
Take one coordinate with base $x_0\sim\Normal(0,s_0^2)$, posterior $x_1\sim\Normal(0,w^2)$, the
straight path $x_s = (1-s)x_0 + s x_1$ and the target $u = x_1 - x_0$. With $\epsilon=1-s$ the
remaining path, the marginal is $x_s\sim\Normal(0,m^2)$ with $m^2 = \epsilon^2s_0^2 + s^2w^2$, and
the optimal field is linear,
\begin{equation}
  v^\star(x,s) = g(s)\,x,\qquad
  g(s) = \frac{s\,w^2 - \epsilon\,s_0^2}{m^2},\qquad
  V \equiv \Var(u\mid x_s) = \frac{s_0^2w^2}{m^2}.
\end{equation}
Without a frame, $s_0$ is the prior's width; in the frame, $s_0=1$ and $w$ is the posterior's width
over the frame's. A model that takes the posterior to be the wider $\Normal(0,w_m^2)$ has the field
$g_m(s)\,x$ and the conditional variance $V_m$, with $w\to w_m$ in $g$, $m$ and $V$. Under uniform
time the velocity loss pays, by the orthogonality of the regression residual, an excess over its
irreducible part $V$ of
\begin{equation}
  \Delta L_{\rm vel}(w_m;w) = \int_0^1 m^2\,\bigl[g_m - g\bigr]^2\,\dd\epsilon ,
  \label{eq:fm-excess}
\end{equation}
and the flow's term of \cref{eq:loss}, whose minimiser is $(g, V)$ by \cref{eq:fm-relative}, pays
\begin{equation}
  \Delta L_{\rm nll}(w_m;w) = \int_0^1 \Bigl[\frac{m^2(g_m-g)^2}{V_m}
  + \frac{V}{V_m} - 1 - \ln\frac{V}{V_m}\Bigr]\dd\epsilon .
  \label{eq:fm-excess-nll}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:fm-sensitivity}
For $w\ll w_m\ll s_0$,
\begin{equation}
  \Delta L_{\rm vel} \simeq \frac{\pi}{4}\,s_0\,w_m ,
  \label{eq:fm-sensitivity-uniform}
\end{equation}
to leading order independent of the true width $w$, and for $w,w_m\ll s_0$
\begin{equation}
  \Delta L_{\rm nll} \simeq \ln\frac{w_m^2}{w^2} + \frac{w^2}{w_m^2} - 1
  = 2\,\mathrm{KL}\bigl(\Normal(0,w^2)\,\big\|\,\Normal(0,w_m^2)\bigr),
  \label{eq:fm-sensitivity-nll}
\end{equation}
which depends on the widths only through $w_m/w$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Set $s_0=1$. Where $\epsilon\gg w$, $m^2\simeq\epsilon^2$ and $g\simeq-1/\epsilon$, so up to a
relative $O(\epsilon)$
\begin{equation*}
  m^2(g_m-g)^2 \simeq \frac{w_m^4}{(\epsilon^2+w_m^2)^2},
\end{equation*}
and the region $\epsilon\lesssim w$ contributes a relative $O(w/w_m)$. With $\epsilon=w_m z$,
\cref{eq:fm-excess} is $w_m\int_0^\infty(1+z^2)^{-2}\dd z = \frac{\pi}{4}w_m$. In
\cref{eq:fm-excess-nll}, $V_m\simeq w_m^2/(\epsilon^2+w_m^2)$, so the first term is $\simeq
w_m^2/(\epsilon^2+w_m^2)$ and integrates to $\frac{\pi}{2}w_m$. Where $\epsilon\gg w_m$,
$V/V_m\simeq w^2/w_m^2$ up to a relative $O(w_m^2/\epsilon^2)$, and the rest of the integrand is the
constant \cref{eq:fm-sensitivity-nll}; the region $\epsilon\lesssim w_m$ changes the integral by
$O(w_m\ln(w_m/w))$. The factors of $s_0$ follow from dimensions: $\Delta L_{\rm vel}$ scales as
$s_0^2$ and depends on $w_m/s_0$ alone, $\Delta L_{\rm nll}$ is dimensionless.
\end{proof}

Quadrature of \cref{eq:fm-excess,eq:fm-excess-nll} confirms both: at $w_m = 10^{-3}s_0$ and a true
posterior $10^4$ times narrower, the velocity loss's excess is \FlowMatchingCheckUniform{} of
\cref{eq:fm-sensitivity-uniform} and the likelihood's \FlowMatchingCheckNll{} of
\cref{eq:fm-sensitivity-nll} (\cref{fig:fm-sensitivity}a).

\paragraph{The velocity loss rewards absolute narrowness, weakly.}
Under the velocity loss the excess is linear in the model's width: halving a width of
$10^{-3}s_0$ is worth a hundredth of halving one of $10^{-1}s_0$. Nothing in the objective grows as
a narrow posterior approaches its true width, and the reward for the last decades of narrowing is
far below the scatter of a logged loss. Reweighting time towards the end of the path raises it, but
only down to the latest time the weighting reaches.

\paragraph{The likelihood rewards relative narrowness, everywhere.}
\Cref{eq:fm-sensitivity-nll} pays $2$ for every e-fold by which the model is too wide, whatever the
true width, until $w_m$ nears $w$; a model too narrow pays more still, through $w^2/w_m^2$. The
reward is collected along the whole path rather than at its end: wherever $\epsilon\gg w_m/s_0$,
$V$ is the posterior variance itself, and the loss compares the model's width with it directly.
Because $\tau$ is predicted per point, the comparison is made for each source separately, not
against a mean pooled over the batch. The frame's loss, \cref{eq:fm-frame-loss}, is the same
Gaussian likelihood at $s=0$ and pays the same $2\,\mathrm{KL}$ per coordinate for a frame of the
wrong width.

\paragraph{Where the bands sit.}
The $x_f$ coordinate is uniform on $[-1,1]$ under the prior, so without a frame $s_0 = 1/\sqrt3$, and
one unit of $x_f$ spans \FlowMatchingNarrowBinsPerUnit{} frequency bins in the narrow band and
\FlowMatchingFullBinsPerUnit{} in the full band (\cref{app:chart}). For a sinusoid of unknown
amplitude and phase the Fisher bound on the frequency is $\sqrt3/(\pi\snr)$ bins. Over the visible
sources, from \ac{snr} \FlowMatchingSnrLow{} to the prior's \FlowMatchingSnrQuantile{}\% quantile
(\FlowMatchingNarrowSnrHigh{} in the narrow band, \FlowMatchingFullSnrHigh{} in the full band),
this gives an ideal $w/s_0$ from \num{\FlowMatchingNarrowLoud} to \num{\FlowMatchingNarrowWeak} in
the narrow band and from \num{\FlowMatchingFullLoud} to \num{\FlowMatchingFullWeak} in the full band
(bars in \cref{fig:fm-sensitivity}). A frame that matches the posterior's width brings every one of
these to $w/s_0\approx1$, and the flow's term then sees order-one widths whatever the source's
\ac{snr}; the decades the frame does not remove are left to the likelihood's relative reward.

The model is idealised: one coordinate, Gaussian base and posterior, and the simplest model error.
Its point is the scaling. A flow-matching loss on the velocity with uniform time is nearly blind to
the width of posteriors much narrower than the prior, which is the regime of loud \ac{gw} sources.
Other amortised estimators for \acp{gb} report a related symptom, frequency posteriors wider than
\ac{mcmc} at high frequency, over wide bands or with many components
\citep{delmond2026neural,houba2025slotflow}, and attribute it to the conditioning network; the
loss is a second candidate.

\subsection{Sampling}
\label{app:fm-sampling}

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics{figures/sampling-cost.pdf}
  \caption{Wall time to draw posterior samples of one window, against model size: the flow on
  one A100 (lines full band, rings narrow band; best of three windows), and the joint \ac{mcmc}
  reference over all sources of a window on two CPU cores (median line and range over the chains,
  one per injection). The arrow marks the gap at the B size.}
  \label{fig:fm-cost}
\end{figure}

To sample a window, the data are encoded once and the frame $(\mu,\sigma)$ read off; $N$ base draws
$Z\sim\Normal(0,I)$ are carried over $K_t = \FlowMatchingOdeSteps{}$ uniform midpoint steps of
\cref{eq:fm-ode} with the velocity of \cref{eq:fm-velocity}, and mapped back with $X = \mu + \sigma
Z$ and $\varphi^{-1}$ (\cref{alg:fm-sample}). A step from $s$ to $s+\Delta s$ evaluates the velocity
at its start, takes a half step to the midpoint and takes the full step with the velocity there, so
a draw costs $2K_t=\FlowMatchingEvaluations{}$ network evaluations. The data stream of the network
depends only on $(y,\fw)$, so it serves every draw and every step (\cref{app:network}). The draws
keep the order of the slots but are not forced into it: two close sources may swap, as they do in
the posterior's tail across the boundary of the ordered region (\cref{app:chart-slots}).

\paragraph{Cost.}
On one A100, \FlowMatchingCostDraws{} draws of one window take \qty{\FlowMatchingCostNarrow}{\second} with the
narrow-band B model and \qty{\FlowMatchingCostFull}{\second} with the full-band one, and
\FlowMatchingCostDrawsMore{} draws take \qty{\FlowMatchingCostFullMore}{\second}: the cost is linear in the
draws and almost independent of the band, since the network evaluations dominate and the data are
encoded once (\cref{fig:fm-cost}). Across sizes, \FlowMatchingCostDraws{} draws take
\qty{\FlowMatchingCostSmallest}{\second} (XS) to \qty{\FlowMatchingCostLargest}{\second} (full-band XL). The \ac{mcmc}
reference (\cref{app:mcmc}) runs one chain per injection over all the sources of its window, and a
chain takes \qtyrange{\FlowMatchingMcmcWindowLow}{\FlowMatchingMcmcWindowHigh}{\hour} on two CPU
cores. The flow is about $10^{\FlowMatchingMcmcGapDecades{}}$ times faster per window. The hardware
differs, so the ratio is a wall-time comparison, not one of compute; and the flow's cost is paid
once more in training (\cref{app:training}).

\paragraph{Resolution at the end of the path.}
The time grid is uniform, so the last step starts at $1-s = 1/K_t$ and evaluates its midpoint at
$1-s = \FlowMatchingLastMid{}$, where the velocity carries the draw to $2\hat Z_1' - \hat Z_1$, with
$\hat Z_1$ and $\hat Z_1'$ the means at the start and the midpoint. Where a posterior is much
narrower than $s_0/2K_t$, the exact means at both points hardly vary across draws: they are close to
the posterior mean, and the last step puts every draw near it. Run from the prior's scale on the
exact Gaussian mean, the sampler returns \FlowMatchingWidthWide{} of the true width at $w = 0.1\,s_0$,
but \FlowMatchingWidthNarrowWeak{} of it at the narrow band's ideal width for \ac{snr}
\FlowMatchingSnrLow{} (\FlowMatchingWidthNarrowWeakMore{} with \FlowMatchingOdeStepsMore{} steps,
\FlowMatchingWidthNarrowWeakMost{} with \FlowMatchingOdeStepsMost{}) and
\num{\FlowMatchingWidthFullWeak} at the full band's (\FlowMatchingWidthFullWeakMost{} with
\FlowMatchingOdeStepsMost{} steps; \cref{fig:fm-sensitivity}b). In a frame of the posterior's width,
$w = s_0$, it returns \FlowMatchingWidthFrame{} of it with \FlowMatchingOdeSteps{} steps: the frame
removes the collapse, as long as it is not decades wider than the posterior. A trained frame and
flow are not exact, so the widths of trained models have to be measured.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
  \caption{One training step, written for one observation; the batch holds
  \FlowMatchingWindows{} observations of \FlowMatchingDraws{} points each.}
  \label{alg:fm-train}
  \begin{algorithmic}
    \STATE {\bfseries Input:} network weights $\eta$
    \STATE draw $\fw$ and $\theta_{1:S}\sim\pi(\cdot\mid\fw)$ (\cref{app:priors})
    \STATE simulate the data $d$ and form $y = y(d,\fw)$ (\cref{app:forward-model,app:noise})
    \STATE $X_1\leftarrow\varphi(\theta_{1:S};\fw)$, sources in order of $f_0$
    \STATE encode $(y,\fw)$ once: frame $(\mu,\sigma)$ \hfill (\cref{eq:fm-frame})
    \STATE $Z_1\leftarrow\operatorname{sg}\bigl((X_1-\mu)/\sigma\bigr)$
    \FOR{each of the \FlowMatchingDraws{} points}
      \STATE $Z_0\sim\Normal(0,I)$;\ \ $s$ from its stratum
      \STATE $Z_s\leftarrow Z_0 + s(Z_1 - Z_0)$ \hfill (\cref{eq:fm-path})
      \STATE $(\hat Z_1,\log\tau)\leftarrow\mathrm{net}_\eta\bigl(Z_s, s; \operatorname{sg}(\mu,\sigma), y, \fw\bigr)$
    \ENDFOR
    \STATE take an optimiser step on \cref{eq:loss} in $\eta$ (\cref{app:training})
  \end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{algorithm}[t]
  \caption{Sampling the posterior of one window.}
  \label{alg:fm-sample}
  \begin{algorithmic}
    \STATE {\bfseries Input:} the input $y$ of a window $\fw$, number of draws $N$, steps $K_t=\FlowMatchingOdeSteps{}$
    \STATE encode $(y,\fw)$ once: frame $(\mu,\sigma)$;\ \ $\Delta s\leftarrow1/K_t$
    \STATE $\vnet(Z,s) = \bigl(\hat Z_{1,\eta}(Z, s; y, \fw) - Z\bigr)/(1-s)$ \hfill (\cref{eq:fm-velocity})
    \FOR{$n=1$ {\bfseries to} $N$ (in parallel)}
      \STATE $Z\sim\Normal(0,I)$
      \FOR{$j=0$ {\bfseries to} $K_t-1$}
        \STATE $s\leftarrow j\Delta s$;\ \ $Z'\leftarrow Z + \tfrac{\Delta s}{2}\,\vnet(Z, s)$
        \STATE $Z\leftarrow Z + \Delta s\,\vnet\bigl(Z', s+\tfrac{\Delta s}{2}\bigr)$ \hfill (\cref{eq:fm-ode})
      \ENDFOR
      \STATE {\bfseries output} $\varphi^{-1}(\mu + \sigma Z;\fw)$
    \ENDFOR
  \end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}
