\section{Priors and the Training Distribution}
\label{app:priors}
% =======================================================================================

The flow learns $p(X\mid y, \fw)$ for the $S=\PriorsSources{}$ sources of one frequency window,
so its prior is a prior over windows: where the window sits, and what its sources are given the
window. This appendix states both, the exact symmetries of the likelihood they leave, and the
stream of simulated windows the network trains on. \Cref{fig:priors} shows the prior;
\cref{tab:priors-bands} collects what differs between the two bands.

\subsection{The Window}
\label{app:priors-window}

A window is $4R$ consecutive frequency bins of width $1/\Tobs$, with $\Tobs = \qty{\PriorsTobsYears}{\year}$, starting at an even bin $k_0 = \PriorsStride{}j$; its centre is $\fw = (k_0 +
2R)/\Tobs$. A source belongs to the window when its whole $R$-bin response fits inside it
(\cref{app:forward-model}), which confines $f_0$ to the $\PriorsInteriorResponses{}R$-bin interior, cut to
the band $[f_{\min}, f_{\max}]$:
\begin{equation}
  f_0\Tobs \in \bigl[\max(k_0 + R/2,\ f_{\min}\Tobs),\ \min(k_0 + 4R - R/2,\ f_{\max}\Tobs)\bigr].
  \label{eq:priors-interior}
\end{equation}
The window index comes from a log-uniform centre snapped to the even grid,
\begin{equation}
  f_c = f_{\min}\,(f_{\max}/f_{\min})^{u},\quad u\sim\Unif(0,1),\qquad
  j = \operatorname{clip}\!\Bigl(\operatorname{round}\frac{f_c\Tobs - 2R}{\PriorsStride{}},\ j_{\rm lo},\ j_{\rm hi}\Bigr),
  \label{eq:priors-window}
\end{equation}
where $j_{\rm lo}$ and $j_{\rm hi}$ are the first and last windows whose interior reaches the
band, and within the window each $f_0$ is log-uniform over \cref{eq:priors-interior}. The $f_0$
marginal is then log-uniform over the band except within a few $R$ of its edges, where the
clipping piles the outermost centres onto the last windows and the cut shortens their interiors
(\cref{fig:priors}d). The departure exceeds a percent only within the distance listed in \cref{tab:priors-bands},
a couple of windows at each end of a band of tens of thousands of them or more.

The two bands share every prior except the band itself. $R$ is not a free choice: it is the
smallest power of two that holds the response of the heaviest source at the top of the band
(\cref{app:forward-model}). Log-uniform $f_0$ is a training choice, not a population model. The
Galactic population falls steeply with frequency \citep{maoz2018separation,korol2022observationally};
following it would starve the high-frequency end of the band, where sources are rare but loud
and well measured.

\subsection{The Sources of a Window}
\label{app:priors-sources}

Given the window, the $S$ sources are drawn independently from the per-source prior of
\cref{tab:priors-source} and sorted by \ac{snr}, loudest first. Only $f_0$ depends on the window
directly; the amplitude depends on it, and on the other parameters, through the \ac{snr}.

\paragraph{Chirp mass.}
We use the observationally driven double-white-dwarf population of
\citet{korol2022observationally}. The heavier component follows the single-white-dwarf mass
function of \citet{kepler2015new}, a three-component Gaussian mixture truncated to
$[m_{\min}, m_{\max}] = [\PriorsMassLow{}, \PriorsMassHigh{}]$\,\unit{\solarmass}, and the lighter one is uniform below
it, the flat mass-ratio distribution of close binaries \citep{moe2017mind}:
\begin{equation}
  m_1 \sim \sum_{j=1}^{3} w_j\,\Normal_{[m_{\min}, m_{\max}]}(\mu_j, \sigma_j^2),\qquad
  m_2\mid m_1 \sim \Unif(m_{\min}, m_1),\qquad
  \Mc = \frac{(m_1m_2)^{3/5}}{(m_1+m_2)^{1/5}},
  \label{eq:priors-mc}
\end{equation}
with $w = (\PriorsPrimaryWeights{})$, $\mu = (\PriorsPrimaryMeans{})$\,\unit{\solarmass} and
$\sigma = (\PriorsPrimaryStds{})$\,\unit{\solarmass}; truncation re-weights each component by the mass it
keeps. The induced chirp mass (\cref{fig:priors}a) is unimodal, with mode \qty{\PriorsMcMode}{\solarmass}
and median \qty{\PriorsMcMedian}{\solarmass}, between the equal-mass extremes $m\,2^{-1/5}$. Its density
has no closed form; the \ac{mcmc} reference (\cref{app:mcmc}) takes it as a table by quadrature over
$m_1$, which $\num{\PriorsMcCheckDraws}$ draws match with $\chi^2/\text{dof} = \PriorsMcCheckChi{}$ over
\PriorsMcCheckDof{} degrees of freedom. This population peaks at a higher chirp mass than the
population-synthesis catalogues of the \ac{lisa} Data Challenges
\citep{korol2017prospects,baghi2022lisa}, which peak lower
\citep[Fig.~3]{korol2022observationally}.

\paragraph{Sky.}
The sky prior is a mixture in galactic longitude $l$ and $\sin b$, the coordinates in which
$\dd l\,\dd\!\sin b$ is the solid angle:
\begin{equation}
  \pi(l, \sin b) = w_{\rm b}\,\Normal(l; 0, \sigma_l^2)\,\Normal(\sin b; 0, \sigma_b^2)
  + \sum_{d\in\{\rm thick, thin\}} w_d\,\mathcal{L}_{\pi}(l; \lambda_{l,d})\,\mathcal{L}_{1}(\sin b; \lambda_{b,d})
  + \frac{w_{\rm f}}{4\pi},
  \label{eq:priors-sky}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{L}_{c}(x; \lambda) \propto e^{-|x|/\lambda}$ is a Laplace density truncated to
$|x|\le c$. The bulge (widths $\PriorsBulgeScales{}$) holds \qty{\PriorsBulgeWeight}{\percent} of the weight,
the thick and thin disks (scales $\PriorsThickScales{}$ and $\PriorsThinScales{}$) hold
\qty{\PriorsThickWeight}{\percent} and \qty{\PriorsThinWeight}{\percent}, and the isotropic floor the remaining
\qty{\PriorsFloorWeight}{\percent}. The mixture was fitted to the sky positions of a catalogue of
$\num{\PriorsCatalogue}$ double white dwarfs. It traces the plane rather than the centre:
\qty{\PriorsPlaneShare}{\percent} of the sources lie within \ang{\PriorsPlaneDeg} of the galactic plane
(\cref{fig:priors}b). The forward model reads equatorial $(\alpha,\delta)$, where the galactic
centre sits at (\ang{\PriorsCentreRa}, \ang{\PriorsCentreDec}).

\paragraph{Amplitude.}
Each source's \ac{snr}, alone in the window, is log-uniform between \PriorsSnrLow{} and
\num{\PriorsSnrHigh}, the \ac{snr} range of the search of \citet{strub2022bayesian}, and the amplitude is set
to produce it. The \ac{snr} is linear in $\Amp$, so one simulation at unit amplitude gives
$\rho_1(\theta)$ (\cref{eq:inner}) and
\begin{equation}
  \Amp = \snr/\rho_1(\theta),\qquad \ln\snr\sim\Unif(\ln\snr_{\rm lo}, \ln\snr_{\rm hi}).
  \label{eq:priors-amp}
\end{equation}
Given the other seven parameters, $\ln\Amp$ is uniform on an interval that moves with them, so
the joint density is the product of the other seven priors and a constant in $\ln\Amp$, cut to
the \ac{snr} range; this is how the \ac{mcmc} reference imposes it (\cref{app:mcmc}). The interval follows
the noise curve in $f_0$ and, within it, mostly the inclination (\cref{fig:priors}e): a face-on
source needs a smaller amplitude than an edge-on one for the same \ac{snr}. Over the prior, $\Amp$
spans more than four decades in each band (\cref{tab:priors-bands}). Drawing the amplitude from
$(\Mc, f_0, D)$ with a Galactic distance model would make the prior astrophysically consistent,
at the price of a much less controlled \ac{snr} distribution.

\paragraph{Sorting.}
The sources are sorted by \ac{snr}, loudest first, so the prior over the ordered window is
\begin{equation}
  \pi_w(\theta_{1:S}) = S!\,\prod_{s=1}^{S}\pi_w(\theta_s)\quad\text{on}\quad
  \snr_1 > \dots > \snr_S,
  \label{eq:priors-window-prior}
\end{equation}
and zero elsewhere. With $u_s = \ln(\snr_s/\snr_{\rm lo})/\ln(\snr_{\rm hi}/\snr_{\rm lo})$, slot $k$
holds the $k$-th largest of $S$ uniforms, $u_k\sim\mathrm{Beta}(S-k+1, k)$, with median \acp{snr}
\PriorsSlotMedians{} (\cref{fig:priors}c). A window is rarely quiet: its total \ac{snr} has median
\PriorsWindowSnrMedian{} and 1st and 99th percentiles \PriorsWindowSnrLow{} and \num{\PriorsWindowSnrHigh}.

\paragraph{A fixed number of sources.}
Every window holds exactly $S$ sources, each above \ac{snr} \PriorsSnrLow{}. A real window holds a
variable number, most of them below any detection threshold, and a global fit infers that number
with a trans-dimensional sampler \citep{littenberg2020global}. Our flow answers the narrower
question ``given that the window holds $S$ resolvable sources, where are they?'', and fainter
sources are not simulated.

\subsection{Exact Symmetries}
\label{app:priors-symmetries}

The likelihood (\cref{eq:loglik}) has two groups of exact symmetries, which a posterior sampler
must either reproduce or remove.

\paragraph{Polarisation and phase.}
A quarter turn of $\psi$ flips the sign of both polarisation tensors, and a half turn of $\phi_0$
flips the sign of both polarisations (\cref{app:forward-model}). Together they leave the signal
unchanged:
\begin{equation}
  (\psi, \phi_0) \;\mapsto\; \bigl(\psi + k\tfrac{\pi}{2},\ \phi_0 + k\pi\bigr)
  \pmod{2\pi},\qquad k = 0,1,2,3,
  \label{eq:psi-phi-symmetry}
\end{equation}
so every source's posterior has four exact copies in $(\psi,\phi_0)\in[0,2\pi)^2$. The helicity
amplitudes of the flow coordinates depend on these angles only through $\phi_0\pm2\psi$
(\cref{eq:chart}), which all four copies share: the chart folds them into one point.

\paragraph{Exchangeable sources.}
The sources are i.i.d.\ before sorting and enter the likelihood through their sum, so the
likelihood is invariant under the $S! = \PriorsPermutations{}$ relabellings: the label-switching
problem of mixture models \citep{stephens2000dealing,jasra2005markov}, which \ac{mcmc} analyses of
gravitational-wave sources meet as well \citep{buscicchio2019label}. With
\cref{eq:psi-phi-symmetry}, every mode of an unordered posterior has
$\PriorsPermutations{}\times4^{\PriorsSources{}} = \num{\PriorsCopies}$ equivalent copies. The \ac{snr} ordering of
\cref{eq:priors-window-prior} removes the permutations (\cref{app:chart-slots}). The data do not
fix the \ac{snr} exactly, so two sources of similar \ac{snr} can trade slots between posterior draws; the
evaluation pairs drawn and injected sources before scoring (\cref{app:evaluation}).

\paragraph{Approximate degeneracies.}
Amplitude trades against inclination, a distant face-on source against a nearer inclined one;
for a nearly face-on source one helicity dominates and the phase of the other is barely
constrained (\cref{app:chart-helicity}). At low frequency the chirp drift $\fdot\Tobs^2$ is a
small fraction of a bin (\cref{fig:priors}f, \cref{tab:priors-bands}) and the chirp mass stays
close to its prior. A Fisher forecast at the \PriorsNarrowFisherInjections{} evaluation injections
of each band makes this quantitative: in the narrow band the median Fisher precision of $\Mc$
exceeds the prior's by at most \PriorsNarrowMcGain{} decades in any slot, against
\PriorsFullMcGainLow{} to \PriorsFullMcGainHigh{} decades across the slots of the full band, whose
loud high-frequency sources resolve the drift.

\subsection{The Training Distribution}
\label{app:priors-training}

There is no fixed dataset: every training example is a freshly simulated window,
\begin{enumerate}
  \item a centre $\fw$ by \cref{eq:priors-window} and sources $\theta_{1:S}\sim\pi_w$ by
  \cref{eq:priors-window-prior};
  \item noisy data in the window's $4R$ bins and its whitened conditioning input $y$
  (\cref{app:noise});
  \item the target $X_1 = \varphi(\theta_{1:S};\fw)$ in flow coordinates, $S\times\PriorsCoords{}$
  (\cref{eq:chart});
  \item \PriorsDraws{} base points $X_0 = \varphi(\theta'_{1:S};\fw)$, each from an independent
  $\theta'_{1:S}\sim\pi_w$ in the same window and each with its own flow time $t$, which give the
  interpolants and target velocities of \cref{eq:loss} (\cref{app:fm}).
\end{enumerate}
The base distribution is the prior itself, so a base point is simulated exactly as the target
is, amplitude scaling included; one example costs \PriorsPasses{} waveform evaluations. A batch of
\num{\PriorsBatch} $(X_0, t)$ pairs is $\PriorsObservationsPerStep{}$ windows times \PriorsDraws{} base
points, drawn inside the compiled training step. A run therefore sees $\num{\PriorsFullObservations}$ to $\num{\PriorsNarrowObservations}$ independent
windows (\cref{tab:priors-bands}): overfitting is not a concern, and every comparison between runs
is one of optimisation, not of generalisation. The simulation is cheap next to the network, 
\qty{\PriorsNarrowDrawShare}{\percent} of a step in the narrow band and \qty{\PriorsFullDrawShare}{\percent} in the full band, where each response
spans \num{\PriorsFullR} bins instead of \num{\PriorsNarrowR} (\cref{app:training}).
