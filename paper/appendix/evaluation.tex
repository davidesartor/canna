\section{Evaluation Protocol and Metrics}
\label{app:evaluation}

This appendix defines how the flows of \cref{app:results} are judged, in support of \cref{sec:validation}. The
\emph{reference pass} compares the flow with joint \ac{mcmc} chains (\cref{app:mcmc}) on fixed windows and uses the
truth to pair draws with sources, so it is descriptive. The \emph{validation pass} never sees the truth or a chain: it
ranks the truth among the draws on windows simulated from the prior, and is the only test of calibration
(\cref{tab:evaluation-passes}). We show on
posteriors with known errors that its tests detect what they should (\cref{app:evaluation-sensitivity}), then give the
sampler settings and out-of-distribution checks.

\subsection{Reference Pass}
\label{app:evaluation-reference}

\paragraph{Eval windows.}
\label{app:evaluation-injections}
The eval windows sit at \EvaluationInjections{} centre frequencies $\fw$, one log-uniform draw in each of
\EvaluationInjections{} equal bins of $\log f_0$, empty windows included. Each holds one prior draw of the run's $S$
sources (\cref{app:priors}), ordered by $f_0$, with window \acp{snr}
\rev{from 1.2 to 98 in the narrow band and from 0 to 380 in the full band, rising with $S$ (median 8.6 to 20 and
6.8 to 12)}. Windows and noise are fixed by the seed and band, so every checkpoint, run and chain sees the same data; as a
conditional sample they are never used for calibration.

\paragraph{Relabelling draws to the truth.}
\label{app:evaluation-matching}
The flow's draws list their sources in order of $f_0$ (\cref{app:chart-slots}), which breaks label switching
\citep{stephens2000dealing,jasra2005markov,buscicchio2019label} only where the frequencies are resolved. For display
and for the reference pass each draw $\theta_{1:S}$ is relabelled to the truth $\theta^\star_{1:S}$ by
\begin{gather}
  \hat\sigma = \operatorname*{arg\,min}_{\sigma\in\mathfrak S_S}
  \sum_{s=1}^{S}\bigl\|\mathbf m(\theta_{\sigma(s)}) - \mathbf m(\theta^\star_s)\bigr\|^2,
  \label{eq:evaluation-matching}\\
  \mathbf m(\theta) = \Bigl(\frac{f_0}{f_+ - f_-},\ \log_{10}\Amp,\ \hat n(\alpha,\delta)\Bigr),
  \nonumber
\end{gather}
found over the \rev{$S!$ permutations of the sources ($\EvaluationPermutations$ for $S=4$)}, with $(f_-, f_+)$ the window's range of $f_0$
(\cref{eq:forward-model-window-interior}) and $\hat n$ the unit sky vector. The orientation angles are left out, since
their symmetries (\cref{eq:psi-phi-symmetry}) make the cost multimodal. Because the truth is used, a posterior too wide
to separate two sources is sorted back onto them and looks better than it is.

\paragraph{Scorecard.}
\label{app:evaluation-scorecard}
Each source of the scorecard windows (\EvaluationWindowSources{} sources) gets the quantities of
\cref{tab:evaluation-scorecard} after relabelling, with $f_0$ in bins of $1/\Tobs$, grouped by true \ac{snr} in bands
$<\EvaluationBandOne$, \EvaluationBandOne--\EvaluationBandTwo, \EvaluationBandTwo--\EvaluationBandThree{} and
$\ge\EvaluationBandThree$, since the ideal width (below) runs from \EvaluationIdealLo{} to \num{\EvaluationIdealHi}
bins over the visible sources.

Coverage and offset are not a calibration test, since they use the truth, and coverage is optimistic: a flow that returns the prior still puts the truth in its central \qty{68}{\percent}
interval for a share \EvaluationMockPriorCover{} of the sources in the narrow band \rev{(\cref{tab:evaluation-mocks})} and \EvaluationMockPriorCoverFull{}
in the full band \rev{(the same mock, run on the full band)}, and only its width shows the failure.

\paragraph{The ideal width.}
For a sinusoid of \ac{snr} $\snr$ observed for $\Tobs$ in stationary noise, with the time origin at the centre, the
Fisher matrix in angular frequency and phase is diagonal, $\Gamma_{\omega\omega} = \snr^2\Tobs^2/12$ and
$\Gamma_{\phi\phi} = \snr^2$, and the frequency width is
\begin{equation}
  w^\star = \frac{\sqrt{12}}{2\pi\,\snr\,\Tobs}\,\Tobs = \frac{\sqrt3}{\pi\,\snr}\bins.
  \label{eq:evaluation-ideal-width}
\end{equation}
This is a scale, not a bound: other correlations widen the real width. Since $f_0$ is the frequency at the start of the observation (\cref{app:forward-model}), a measured chirp
widens it by a factor of \EvaluationStartFactor{} over the centred one, so a full-band ratio above one is not by itself a
failure. Ratios against the chains are the comparison that counts.

\paragraph{Widths and shapes against the chains.}
\label{app:evaluation-mcmc}
Chain and flow draws are compared with $(\psi,\phi_0)$ folded onto the copy nearest the truth, and orientation through
the phases $\phi_0 \pm 2\psi$, since $\phi_0$ alone inherits the spread of $\psi$. Widths are $1.4826\,\MAD$ per parameter, flow over chain, against
the joint chains of every eval window. The chains are an evaluation reference only.

% added 2026-11-18 (user): the Jensen-Shannon divergence of eq. 30 of delmond2026neural; results in app:results-js
The shapes of the marginals, offsets and tails included, are compared with the Jensen--Shannon divergence
\citep{lin1991divergence}, as \citet{delmond2026neural} do. For the densities $p$ and $q$ of one coordinate, with the
Kullback--Leibler divergence \citep{kullback1951information}
\begin{equation}
  D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p\,\|\,q) = \int p(x)\,\log_2\frac{p(x)}{q(x)}\,\dd x ,
  \label{eq:evaluation-kl}
\end{equation}
it is
\begin{equation}
  D_{\mathrm{JS}}(p\,\|\,q) = \tfrac12\,D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p\,\|\,m) + \tfrac12\,D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q\,\|\,m),
  \qquad m = \tfrac12\,(p+q).
  \label{eq:evaluation-js}
\end{equation}
It is symmetric, finite when one density vanishes where the other does not, invariant under one-to-one maps of the
coordinate, and with base-2 logarithms lies in $[0,1]$. We estimate it per source and coordinate from the flow's and the
chain's \ResultsJsDraws{} draws each, relabelled and folded as above, on $B=\ResultsJsBins$ bins whose edges are
quantiles of the pooled draws:
\begin{equation}
  \hat D_{\mathrm{JS}} = \frac12\sum_{b=1}^{B}\hat p_b\log_2\frac{\hat p_b}{\hat m_b}
  + \frac12\sum_{b=1}^{B}\hat q_b\log_2\frac{\hat q_b}{\hat m_b},
  \qquad \hat m_b = \tfrac12\,(\hat p_b+\hat q_b),
  \label{eq:evaluation-js-hat}
\end{equation}
with $\hat p_b$ and $\hat q_b$ the shares of the flow's and the chain's draws in bin $b$. For two samples of one continuous distribution its median is
\ResultsJsFloorMedian{} and its 95th percentile \ResultsJsFloorHigh{}, the floor of the comparison
(\num{2000} simulated pairs). Between two Gaussians it is \ResultsJsGaussWidthTwo{} for a width ratio of 2 and
\ResultsJsGaussOffsetOne{} for an offset of one width.
\Cref{app:results-js} gives the results.

\subsection{Validation Pass}
\label{app:evaluation-validation}

The validation pass draws \EvaluationValidation{} windows, each with its own $\fw$, sources and noise from the prior,
and $M = \EvaluationValidationDraws$ flow draws on each. If the flow is the exact posterior, the rank of any scalar test
quantity $g$ of the truth among the draws, the number of draws with $g(\theta_m) < g(\theta^\star)$ (ties broken at
random), is uniform on $\{0,\dots,M\}$ \citep{talts2018validating}. A failure is a non-uniform histogram: ranks mostly
high mean a bias, ranks piled in the middle a posterior too wide, ranks piled at the ends one too narrow.

\paragraph{Slots and strata.}
The same $g$ is applied to the truth and to every draw without using the truth. Slots are therefore defined by each
parameter set's own \ac{snr}: the truth and each draw are sorted independently by the \acp{snr} of their own sources, a
fixed function of the parameters, so the ranks stay uniform under an exact posterior, unlike those after
\cref{eq:evaluation-matching}. Strata follow the same rule: a source is binned in the bands of
\cref{app:evaluation-scorecard} by $\hat\snr$, the median own \ac{snr} of the draws in its slot, and window quantities by
the draws' median total \ac{snr}.

\paragraph{Tests.}
\Cref{tab:evaluation-tests} lists them. The coordinate and \ac{tarp} ranks test the parameters alone, which the prior passes; data-dependent
quantities catch it \citep{modrak2023simulation}. The likelihood is only a test quantity on draws already made.

\paragraph{Uniformity test.}
Ranks are tested with the graphical \ac{ecdf} test of \citet{sailynoja2022graphical}. With $N$ ranks on
$k = \EvaluationRankValues$ values, the statistic $\gamma$ is twice the smallest binomial tail probability of the rank
\ac{ecdf} at $z_i = i/k$, $i = 1,\dots,k-1$. Its $p$-value comes from \EvaluationGammaSims{} simulated sets of $N$
uniform ranks, which also give a simultaneous \qty{\EvaluationBandLevel}{\percent} band for the \ac{ecdf} minus $z$.
Each cell reports the $p$-value, the mean rank over $M$ minus one half (positive when the truth sits above the draws),
and the share of ranks in the middle half (above one half: too wide).

\subsection{Sensitivity of the Tests}
\label{app:evaluation-sensitivity}

Passing means something only if the tests fail on wrong posteriors, which we check on a toy and on mocks.

\paragraph{Size and power on an exact toy.}
\label{app:evaluation-power}
Each slot of the toy is a linear-Gaussian model in its eight coordinates, drawn exactly, with all test quantities
computed on its data. A test's rate (\cref{tab:evaluation-power}) is the share of chunks of \EvaluationPowerChunk{} of
\EvaluationPowerInjections{} injections (\EvaluationPowerDraws{} draws each) in which it rejects at
\qty{\EvaluationPowerAlpha}{\percent}. The rank tests hold their size. A phase or sky taken from the prior, or
coordinates shuffled across draws, keep every one-dimensional rank uniform and pass the coordinate ranks, as does most of a
\qty{\EvaluationPowerMixShare}{\percent} prior admixture; the overlap and the log-likelihood catch all four.
\Ac{tarp}\rev{, not shown in \cref{tab:evaluation-power}, rejects width errors at rates of} \EvaluationPowerTarpWidth{} but
\rev{the other errors at} most \EvaluationPowerTarpBlind{}.

Three readings shape how the results are read.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Marginal calibration proves nothing alone.} The prior mock passes \ac{tarp} and the coordinate ranks (its
    smallest $p$ of eight, \EvaluationMockPriorCoord, is what chance gives); the overlap and log-likelihood ranks reject
    it, and coverage reads it as conservative.
  \item \emph{The Gaussian mock is not a null.} Where sources are faint the linearisation fails: only $f_0$ and $\fdot$
    pass (or nearly pass), and the sky, the $z_\pm$ components and radii, \ac{tarp}, the overlap and the log-likelihood
    reject it. A flow's $p$-value or rank offset is therefore compared with the Gaussian mock's on the same test and
    windows, not with \qty{\EvaluationPowerAlpha}{\percent}.
    % updated 2026-11-19 (user): the TODO(check) on reproducibility turned into this sentence.
    A second realisation of the same mock on the same windows, on other hardware, gave $p=0.14$ for $f_0$ and $0.42$ for
    $\fdot$ where this one gives $0.04$ and $0.26$, while the prior mock reproduces to the digit: the Gaussian draws differ
    between machines (an eigendecomposition of an ill-conditioned covariance), so $p$-values near the threshold move by that
    much and these readings do not change.
  \item \emph{Coverage hides a width floor.} The loud-floor mock covers \EvaluationMockFloorCoverQuiet{} of sources below
    \ac{snr} \EvaluationBandTwo{} and \EvaluationMockFloorCoverLoud{} above, so it reads conservative; only the width
    ratio sees it (\EvaluationMockFloorIdeal{} times the ideal, against \EvaluationMockFisherIdeal{} for the Gaussian mock).
    On loud full-band sources the Gaussian mock is itself \EvaluationMockFisherIdealFull{} times wider than the ideal width.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Checkpoint and Sampler Settings}
\label{app:evaluation-weights}

\rev{Every pass evaluates the run's checkpoint with the lowest eval-catalogue loss (\cref{app:training-loop}),
which guards against a run that degrades late; it is chosen on the eval windows, so its results on them are
optimistic. The runs of \cref{app:results} were all evaluated at that checkpoint (\cref{tab:results-runs})}, except the full band with $S=4$, whose run diverged: its Student-t continuation is evaluated at
its last checkpoint, since its logged loss does not compare across the switch (\cref{app:results-runs}).

The flow predicts the endpoint $Z_1$ in the frame (\cref{app:fm}), and the sampler takes \EvaluationOdeSteps{} midpoint
steps toward the predicted mean from standard normal base draws in the frame (\cref{app:fm-frame}), in the precision the
run was trained in.

\subsection{Sampler Steps and Robustness}
\label{app:evaluation-sampler}

\paragraph{Step count.}
A coarse grid can collapse narrow posteriors late in the path (\cref{fig:fm-sensitivity}b), and a collapse only narrows.
On the source-count runs (narrow band, $S=4$, and full band, $S=8$; 128 draws per window) the median $f_0$ width of the
sources at $\snr\ge15$, over its width at \ResultsSamplerNarrowMax{} uniform midpoint steps, is
\ResultsSamplerNarrowEight, \ResultsSamplerNarrowSixteen, \ResultsSamplerNarrowThirtyTwo{} and
\ResultsSamplerNarrowSixtyFour{} at 8, 16, 32 and 64 steps on the narrow band, and \ResultsSamplerFullEight{} already at 8
steps on the full band. \EvaluationOdeSteps{} steps are within \qty{2}{\percent} of converged. The converged width does not
depend on the grid: the power-2 and log grids give \ResultsSamplerGridLo--\ResultsSamplerGridHi{} times the uniform
grid's from \ResultsSamplerGridFrom{} steps up, on both bands. Euler converges from below
(\ResultsSamplerEulerNarrowThirtyTwo{} and \ResultsSamplerEulerFullThirtyTwo{} of the converged width at
\EvaluationOdeSteps{} steps), so every width here is read with the midpoint rule.

\paragraph{Out of distribution.}
The flow is trained on one noise \ac{psd}, a fixed prior and exactly $S$ sources per window. Each case changes one of these
on the eval windows, keeping window, truth and noise: the \ac{psd} halved and doubled; the loudest source's $f_0$, $\fdot$
or amplitude past the prior's bulk; the quietest source's amplitude below it; one source removed or added; an empty window.
On the narrow band with $S=4$ (\ResultsRobustInjections{} windows, \ResultsRobustDraws{} draws) the flow is robust to its
prior's edges and fragile to its noise model. With the loudest source past the prior, or the noise halved, the share of
sources at $\snr\ge7$ whose truth lies within one flow width of its median is \ResultsRobustOtherWithinLo{} to
\qty{\ResultsRobustOtherWithinHi}{\percent}, against \qty{\ResultsRobustBaseLoudWithin}{\percent} in distribution\rev{ for the same sources}. With the
noise doubled the flow, which whitens by its own \ac{psd}, reads noise as signal: its slots claim a median \ac{snr} of
\ResultsRobustNoiseSlotSnr{} instead of \ResultsRobustBaseSlotSnr, the faint sources' $f_0$ widths shrink to \ResultsRobustNoiseFaintWidth{} of their in-distribution widths while their offsets
double, and only \qty{\ResultsRobustNoiseFaintWithin}{\percent} cover the truth within one width
(\qty{\ResultsRobustBaseFaintWithin}{\percent} \rev{of the faint sources} in distribution): confident and wrong. The full band with $S=8$ shows the same on its loud sources: with the noise doubled
\qty{\ResultsRobustFullNoiseLoudWithin}{\percent} cover the truth within one width
(\qty{\ResultsRobustFullBaseLoudWithin}{\percent} in distribution), and the other cases keep
\ResultsRobustFullOtherWithinLo{} to \qty{\ResultsRobustFullOtherWithinHi}{\percent}.
