We have shown that conditional flow matching returns joint posteriors of overlapping \acp{gb}
under the full instrument response, across the \ac{lisa} band, in a fraction of a second per
window. The chart, the frequency order and the frame turn a posterior multimodal by symmetry into one a
flow on straight paths can learn, and the one-way network makes each draw cost only the source
stream. Validation needs tests in data space and known-answer mocks: without them, flows that
ignore the data pass.

The flow is checked on data from its own prior; out-of-distribution windows are
probed in \cref{app:evaluation-sampler}: the flow tolerates sources past its prior but turns confident and
wrong when the noise is louder than its own \ac{psd}.

\paragraph{Proposed follow-ups.}
\label{sec:followups}
Our simulator is deliberately simpler than real \ac{lisa} data. The same simulator generates the
training data, the test injections and the likelihood of the reference \ac{mcmc} (\cref{app:mcmc}),
so its simplifications limit realism, not the consistency of the comparison. Lifting them, roughly
in the order a global fit would need them, is the work that follows:
\begin{itemize}
  \item windows of adaptive length, a realistic number of sources per window, varying from window
  to window with the density of the Galaxy, and sources that straddle a window's edge or leak in
  from a neighbouring window: every window now has a fixed length and holds exactly $S$ binaries,
  mostly below the noise, and nothing else. A tiling whose window length follows the Doppler spread
  of the responses, which grows with $f_0$, and keeps the number of sources per window small should
  simplify the analysis, at high frequencies in particular, where the Galaxy is sparse and the
  sources are loud. Using the posteriors inside a global fit, which infers the number of sources
  and updates overlapping segments in turn \citep{littenberg2020global}, needs all three;
  \item the confusion foreground of the unresolved binaries
  \citep{timpano2006characterizing,karnesis2021characterization} and the other source classes;
  \item gaps in the data \citep{baghi2019gravitational,dey2021effect}, and noise that is estimated
  with the sources rather than known, non-stationary, and with glitches (\cref{app:noise});
  \item realistic orbits in place of the equal-arm ones, with unequal, time-varying arms, the
  emitter's position taken at emission rather than at reception, and \ac{tdi} generation 2 in place of
  generation 1.5 (\cref{app:forward-model-tdi});
  \item the cubic phase of $\ddot f = \tfrac{11}{3}\fdot^2/f_0$, which JaxGB drops: the frequency now
  evolves linearly, which is negligible in the narrow band and matters most at the top of the full band;
  \item responses longer than $R$ bins, since the widest sources of the full band now lose their
  outermost bins above the noise (\cref{app:forward-model-heterodyne}).
\end{itemize}

We will add realistic orbits and gaps first. \emph{We expect the network to be very robust to both,
and this is one of the main advantages of a simulation-based approach over \ac{mcmc}.} The network
needs only a simulator, never a likelihood: orbits, gaps and glitches enter by simulating them in the
training data, and the network learns their effect on the posterior from examples. A sampler instead
needs that effect written into its likelihood. Gaps break the stationarity that makes the
frequency-domain likelihood diagonal (\cref{eq:loglik}), so an exact likelihood correlates
frequency bins and is costly to evaluate, and any effect the likelihood leaves out or approximates
(an orbit model, a gap's taper, an unmodelled glitch) biases the chains' posteriors
\citep{baghi2019gravitational,dey2021effect}. As long as the training simulations contain these effects, the
cost of the network's draws does not change, and neither does the way its posteriors are checked
(\cref{app:evaluation}).
