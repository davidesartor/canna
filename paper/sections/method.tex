\subsection{The Bayesian Inversion Problem}
\label{sec:inversion}\label{sec:problem}
% Moved 2026-11-18 (user) from the end of sec:gw, without its Factorisation, Windows and Symmetries
% paragraphs (now sec:gw-slicing); written after Delmond et al. (arXiv:2606.29039), Sec. III-A.

Inferring the sources of a window means estimating their posterior, \cref{eq:loglik}, in which
the prior of \cref{eq:priors-window-prior} encodes what the Galactic population says before the
data, and the likelihood measures how well the signal of candidate sources matches the data. The
evidence $p(d_w) = \int p(d_w\mid\Theta_w)\,\pi_w(\Theta_w)\,\dd\Theta_w$ is intractable,
so the posterior is usually explored by stochastic sampling, with \ac{mcmc}
\citep{littenberg2020global,strub2022bayesian} or nested sampling \citep{skilling2006nested}. These
methods are accurate, but they evaluate the likelihood many times for every window, and a global
fit repeats them over every window and every new stretch of data. We instead train a conditional
generative model of the posterior once, over all windows of a band, so that a new window costs only
draws from it (\cref{sec:method-fm}).

\subsection{Flow Matching}
\label{sec:method-fm}

The flow draws $X\in\R^{8S}$, the $S$ sources of a window in the flow coordinates of
\cref{sec:method-chart}, eight per source, held in $S$ slots in order of $f_0$; $X_1$ is the
window's true sources in these coordinates. The network first reads a Gaussian frame off the data,
a mean $\mu$ and a standard deviation $\sigma$ per coordinate of each slot, around the prior's
moments (\cref{eq:fm-frame}). It is trained on the Gaussian likelihood of $X_1$, so it learns the
posterior's marginal mean and width, with a weight that keeps loud sources, whose frames are decades
narrower than the prior, from dominating the batch (\cref{eq:fm-frame-loss}). The flow then works in
that frame, $Z = (X-\mu)/\sigma$: paths are straight, $Z_s = Z_0 + s(Z_1-Z_0)$ with time
$s\in[0,1]$, from a standard normal $Z_0$ to the true sources $Z_1 = (X_1-\mu)/\sigma$
(\cref{eq:fm-path}), and the flow cannot move the frame. The network predicts
the mean $\hat Z_1$ and log standard deviation $\log\tau$ of a Gaussian over the endpoint, per
coordinate, and is trained on the endpoint's negative log-likelihood (\cref{eq:loss}) at times
uniform along the path; its velocity is $(\hat Z_1 - Z_s)/(1-s)$ (\cref{app:fm-loss}). A loud
source's posterior is a small fraction of a bin wide in $f_0$. A Gaussian model
(\cref{prop:fm-sensitivity}, \cref{fig:fm-sensitivity}) shows that the velocity loss of standard
flow matching barely rewards narrowing such a posterior, while the likelihood pays the same for
every e-fold of excess width, relative to each source's own width. The same model shows that a
uniform sampler grid run on the prior's scale collapses the narrowest posteriors, and that in a
frame of the posterior's width it does not; draws use \FlowMatchingOdeSteps{} midpoint steps of the
velocity and are mapped back with $X = \mu+\sigma Z$ (\cref{app:fm-sampling}).

\subsection{Flow Coordinates}
\label{sec:method-chart}\label{sec:gw-chart}

% moved 2026-11-18 (user) from sec:gw-slicing.
The likelihood is invariant under $(\psi,\phi_0)\to(\psi+\pi/2,\phi_0+\pi)$ and its shifts, four
copies of each source for $(\psi,\phi_0)\in[0,2\pi)^2$ (\cref{eq:psi-phi-symmetry}), and under the
$S!$ permutations of the sources. A flow on the raw parameters must place mass on every copy and
every labelling, and evaluating it needs the copies matched
\citep{stephens2000dealing,jasra2005markov,buscicchio2019label}. Our coordinates remove both.
The flow transports points of $\R^{8S}$, one chart image per source (\cref{eq:chart}). Frequency
is scaled to the window, $\log\fdot$ standardised given $f_0$, and the sky taken in galactic
coordinates scaled by the prior's widths. Amplitude, inclination, polarisation and phase enter
as the two helicity amplitudes
$\zeta_\pm\propto\Amp\,(1\mp\cos\iota)^2\,e^{\ii(\phi_0\pm2\psi)}/4$, in units of the noise, with
their radius warped by $\operatorname{asinh}$. The signal is linear in $\zeta_\pm$
(\cref{eq:forward-model-helicity}), so the four copies of a source in $(\psi,\phi_0)$ map to one
point, and at high \ac{snr} the likelihood in $\zeta_\pm$ is close to Gaussian
(\cref{app:chart-helicity}). The chart is flat, so the flow's paths are straight lines
(\cref{app:chart-flat}). The sources are slots in order of $f_0$, so each mode of the posterior
has one copy instead of $S!$, one per labelling (\cref{app:chart-slots}).

\subsection{Network}
\label{sec:method-network}

The network is a two-stream transformer \citep{peebles2023scalable,esser2024scaling} with a data
stream and a source stream (\cref{fig:network-architecture}). The data stream reads the window's
tokens (\cref{sec:gw-tokens}). One frame register per slot runs with the
data stream and gives that slot's frame (\cref{app:network-frame}). The source stream holds one
token per slot, embedded from its coordinates in the frame and in the chart and from the frame
itself, together with attention sinks \citep{xiao2024efficient,darcet2024vision}. Attention is
one-way: the data stream and the registers attend only to each other and are conditioned on the
window alone, never on the time or the sources, so their keys and values, and the frame, are
computed once per window and reused by every draw and every sampler step; the source stream
attends to everything and is conditioned on the time. Rotary embeddings in frequency
\citep{su2024roformer} place each source at its $f_0$ and each data token at its centre bin.
\Cref{app:network} gives the embeddings and \cref{tab:network-sizes} the sizes; all results use
size B, \num{\NetworkBTotalFull} parameters.

\subsection{Training Set}
\label{sec:gw-training}
% Moved 2026-11-19 (user) from the end of sec:gw (was 2.5). New 2026-11-18, after Delmond et al. (arXiv:2606.29039), Sec. III-C; summarises app:priors-window and

Training needs windows paired with their sources, $(\Theta_w, y)$ drawn from
$\pi_w(\Theta_w)\,p(y\mid\Theta_w,\fw)$. \Citet{delmond2026neural} precompute a bank of
responses over frequency, drift and sky and draw the remaining parameters on the fly. We simulate
every window afresh instead, on the device and inside the compiled training step, so there is no
fixed dataset and no window is seen twice (\cref{app:priors-training}). One example takes four steps:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item a window centre $\fw$ (\cref{eq:priors-window}), placed mostly where the catalogue's sources
    can be seen, with weight the catalogue's $f_0$ marginal times the chance that a source there
    reaches $\snr\ge\PriorsVisibleSnr$, and in \qty{\PriorsUniformWindowsPercent}{\percent} of the
    windows log-uniformly over the band, so that training also sees the empty windows most of the
    band holds;
  \item $S_w = S$ sources $\Theta_w\sim\pi_w$ (\cref{eq:priors-window-prior}), with $f_0$ in the
    interior of \cref{eq:forward-model-window-interior};
  \item each source's $R$-bin response, computed with JaxGB \citep{jaxgb} over
    $\Tobs=\qty{\ForwardModelTobsYears}{\year}$ (\cref{app:forward-model-heterodyne}) and added at its
    offset in the window, and noise drawn by \cref{eq:noise-data};
  \item the whitened data $y$, and the sources in the flow coordinates of \cref{sec:method-chart},
    in order of $f_0$.
\end{enumerate}
The weights of the first step choose which windows training sees, not what their sources are, so
the posterior given a window is unchanged. Unlike \citet{delmond2026neural}, who keep sources with an
\ac{snr} between 10 and 100, we apply no cut: most of a window's sources lie below the noise, and
for them the flow must return the prior. The parameters are not standardised by the prior's
moments; the flow coordinates and the frame of \cref{sec:method} take that role. The simulation
takes \qty{\PriorsNarrowDrawShare}{\percent} of a training step in the narrow band and
\qty{\PriorsFullDrawShare}{\percent} in the full band (\cref{tab:priors-bands}).
\Cref{fig:forward-model-window} shows one window of each band.

\paragraph{Bands.}
We train two configurations (\cref{tab:forward-model-bands}): the narrow band, windows of
\PriorsNarrowW{} bins with \qtyrange{\PriorsNarrowLowMhz}{\PriorsNarrowHighMhz}{\milli\hertz}, where sources
crowd the most; and the full band, windows of \PriorsFullW{} bins up to
\qty{\PriorsFullHighMhz}{\milli\hertz}. At the top of the full band a source's Doppler, chirp and
antenna sidebands span \ForwardModelFullSpan{} bins at the mean $\fdot$, against
$R=\ForwardModelFullR{}$, so the few sources there lose their outermost bins above the noise. The narrow band's $R=\ForwardModelNarrowR{}$ keeps all
the power above the noise of \qty{99.9}{\percent} of its sources, which are
\qty{\ForwardModelNarrowSpanShare}{\percent} of the full band's sources above the noise
(\cref{app:forward-model-heterodyne}).

\paragraph{Tokens.}
\label{sec:gw-tokens}
The data stream of the network (\cref{sec:method-network}) reads the window as
a \ac{stft}, \NetworkPatch{} bins per token, each token holding its band's time
samples in the three channels (\cref{eq:network-stft}): \NetworkTokensNarrow{} tokens in the
narrow band, \NetworkTokensFull{} in the full band.

\subsection{Training}
\label{sec:method-training}

Every batch is simulated on the device: \TrainingObservations{} windows, each with
\TrainingDraws{} draws of time and base point sharing its cached data stream, so no observation is
seen twice (\cref{app:training-batch}). The optimiser is Muon \citep{jordan2024muon,liu2025muon}
for the hidden weight matrices and AdamW for the rest, with a linear warmup, a constant learning
rate and a cooldown to zero \citep{hagele2024scaling} (\cref{app:training}). Each run trains for a
fixed wall-time budget, \ResultsNarrowHours\,h on the narrow band and \ResultsFullHours\,h on the full
band on one A100 (\cref{tab:results-runs}), and is evaluated at the checkpoint with the lowest
eval-catalogue loss (\cref{app:evaluation-weights}).
