Inferring the sources of a window means estimating their posterior, \cref{eq:loglik}, in which
the prior of \cref{eq:priors-window-prior} encodes what the Galactic population says before the
data. Sampling it with \ac{mcmc} \citep{littenberg2020global,strub2022bayesian} evaluates the
likelihood many times for every window, and a global fit repeats that over every window and every
new stretch of data (\cref{sec:background}). We instead train a conditional generative model of the
posterior once, over all windows of a band, so that a new window costs only draws from it.

\subsection{Flow Coordinates}
\label{sec:method-chart}\label{sec:gw-chart}

The likelihood is invariant under $(\psi,\phi_0)\to(\psi+\pi/2,\phi_0+\pi)$ and its shifts, four
copies of each source for $(\psi,\phi_0)\in[0,2\pi)^2$ (\cref{eq:psi-phi-symmetry}), and under the
$S!$ permutations of the sources. A flow on the raw parameters must place mass on every copy and
every labelling, and evaluating it needs the copies matched
\citep{stephens2000dealing,jasra2005markov,buscicchio2019label}. Our coordinates remove both.
The flow draws $X\in\R^{8S}$, one chart image of eight coordinates per source (\cref{eq:chart}).
Frequency is scaled to the window, $\log\fdot$ standardised given $f_0$, and the sky taken in
galactic coordinates scaled by the prior's widths. Amplitude, inclination, polarisation and phase
enter as the two helicity amplitudes
$\zeta_\pm\propto\Amp\,(1\mp\cos\iota)^2\,e^{\ii(\phi_0\pm2\psi)}/4$, in units of the noise, with
their radius warped by $\operatorname{asinh}$. The signal is linear in $\zeta_\pm$
(\cref{eq:forward-model-helicity}), so the four copies of a source in $(\psi,\phi_0)$ map to one
point, and at high \ac{snr} the likelihood in $\zeta_\pm$ is close to Gaussian
(\cref{app:chart-helicity}). The chart is flat, so the flow's paths are straight lines
(\cref{app:chart-flat}). The sources sit in $S$ slots in order of $f_0$, so each mode of the
posterior has one copy instead of $S!$, one per labelling (\cref{app:chart-slots}).

\subsection{Architecture}
\label{sec:method-network}\label{sec:gw-tokens}

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/schematic.pdf}
  \caption{The network and sampler: four streams, each with its own weights. The data and register
  streams read the window $y$ once and attend jointly; the registers give the frame
  $\mu,\log\sigma$ of each slot, and the keys and values of both are cached. The source and sink
  streams read the current draw $Z_s$ at time $s$, attend jointly and to the cache, and the source
  stream predicts the endpoint $\hat Z_1,\log\tau$, from which each sampler step moves $Z_s$; the
  last draw is mapped back by the frame. \Cref{fig:network-architecture} shows the blocks in full.}
  \label{fig:schematic}
\end{figure}

The network is a two-stream transformer \citep{peebles2023scalable,esser2024scaling}
(\cref{fig:schematic}). The \emph{data stream} reads the window as a \ac{stft},
\NetworkPatch{} bins per token, each token holding its band's time samples in the three channels
(\cref{eq:network-stft}): \NetworkTokensNarrow{} tokens in the narrow band, \NetworkTokensFull{} in
the full band. One frame register per slot runs with it, and a frame head reads from each register
a Gaussian frame for its slot, a mean $\mu$ and a standard deviation $\sigma$ per coordinate,
around the prior's moments (\cref{eq:fm-frame}, \cref{app:network-frame}). The \emph{source
stream} holds one token per slot, embedded from its coordinates in the frame and in the chart and
from the frame itself, together with attention sinks \citep{xiao2024efficient,darcet2024vision}; a
head predicts, per coordinate, the mean $\hat Z_1$ and log standard deviation $\log\tau$ of a
Gaussian over the flow's endpoint.

Attention is one-way. The data stream and the registers attend only to each other and are
conditioned on the window alone, never on the time or the sources, so their keys and values, and
the frame, are computed once per window and reused by every draw and every sampler step. The source
stream attends to everything and is conditioned on the time. Rotary embeddings in frequency
\citep{su2024roformer} place each source at its $f_0$ and each data token at its centre bin.
\Cref{app:network} gives the embeddings and \cref{tab:network-sizes} the sizes; all results use
size B, \num{\NetworkBTotalFull} parameters.

\subsection{Flow Matching in the Frame}
\label{sec:method-fm}

Let $X_1$ be the window's true sources in the flow coordinates. The frame is trained on the
Gaussian likelihood of $X_1$, so it learns the posterior's marginal mean and width, with a weight
that keeps loud sources, whose frames are decades narrower than the prior, from dominating the
batch (\cref{eq:fm-frame-loss}). The flow then works in that frame, $Z = (X-\mu)/\sigma$: paths are
straight, $Z_s = Z_0 + s(Z_1-Z_0)$ with time $s\in[0,1]$, from a standard normal $Z_0$ to the true
sources $Z_1 = (X_1-\mu)/\sigma$ (\cref{eq:fm-path}), and the flow cannot move the frame. The
endpoint head is trained on the endpoint's negative log-likelihood (\cref{eq:loss}) at times
uniform along the path; its velocity is $(\hat Z_1 - Z_s)/(1-s)$ (\cref{app:fm-loss}).

A loud source's posterior is a small fraction of a bin wide in $f_0$. A Gaussian model
(\cref{prop:fm-sensitivity}, \cref{fig:fm-sensitivity}) shows that the velocity loss of standard
flow matching barely rewards narrowing such a posterior, while the likelihood pays the same for
every e-fold of excess width, relative to each source's own width. The same model shows that a
uniform sampler grid run on the prior's scale collapses the narrowest posteriors, and that in a
frame of the posterior's width it does not. Draws use \FlowMatchingOdeSteps{} midpoint steps of the
velocity and are mapped back with $X = \mu+\sigma Z$ (\cref{app:fm-sampling}).

\subsection{Training}
\label{sec:method-training}\label{sec:gw-training}

Training needs windows paired with their sources, $(\Theta_w, y)$ drawn from
$\pi_w(\Theta_w)\,p(y\mid\Theta_w,\fw)$. \Citet{delmond2026neural} precompute a bank of
responses over frequency, drift and sky and draw the remaining parameters on the fly. We simulate
every window afresh instead, on the device and inside the compiled training step, so there is no
fixed dataset and no window is seen twice (\cref{app:priors-training}). One example takes four steps:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item a window centre $\fw$ (\cref{eq:priors-window}), placed mostly where the catalogue's sources
    can be seen, and in \qty{\PriorsUniformWindowsPercent}{\percent} of the windows log-uniformly
    over the band, so that training also sees the empty windows most of the band holds
    (\cref{sec:narrow-band-prior});
  \item $S_w = S$ sources $\Theta_w\sim\pi_w$ (\cref{eq:priors-window-prior}), with $f_0$ in the
    interior of \cref{eq:forward-model-window-interior};
  \item each source's $R$-bin response, computed with JaxGB \citep{jaxgb} over
    $\Tobs=\qty{\ForwardModelTobsYears}{\year}$ (\cref{app:forward-model-heterodyne}) and added at its
    offset in the window, and noise drawn by \cref{eq:noise-data};
  \item the whitened data $y$, and the sources in the flow coordinates, in order of $f_0$.
\end{enumerate}
Unlike \citet{delmond2026neural}, who keep sources with an \ac{snr} between 10 and 100, we apply
no cut: most of a window's sources lie below the noise, and for them the flow must return the
prior. The parameters are not standardised by the prior's moments; the flow coordinates and the
frame take that role. The simulation takes \qty{\PriorsNarrowDrawShare}{\percent} of a training
step in the narrow band and \qty{\PriorsFullDrawShare}{\percent} in the full band
(\cref{tab:priors-bands}); \cref{fig:forward-model-window} shows one narrow-band window.

A batch holds \TrainingObservations{} windows, each with \TrainingDraws{} draws of time and base
point sharing its cached data stream (\cref{app:training-batch}). The optimiser is Muon
\citep{jordan2024muon,liu2025muon} for the hidden weight matrices and AdamW for the rest, with a
linear warmup, a constant learning rate and a cooldown to zero \citep{hagele2024scaling}
(\cref{app:training}). Each run trains for a fixed wall-time budget, \ResultsNarrowHours\,h on the
narrow band and \ResultsFullHours\,h on the full band on one A100 (\cref{tab:results-runs}), and is
evaluated at the checkpoint with the lowest eval-catalogue loss (\cref{app:evaluation-weights}).
