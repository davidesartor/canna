% =======================================================================================
\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}
% =======================================================================================

The space-based \ac{lisa}, due for launch in 2035, will open the millihertz band of gravitational
waves \citep{amaro2017lisa,colpi2024lisa}. Its most numerous sources are the compact binaries of
our own Galaxy: millions of quasi-monochromatic \acp{gb} between
\qtyrange{\RelatedWorkFzeroLow}{\RelatedWorkFzeroHigh}{\milli\hertz}, of which thousands will be
resolved \citep{nelemans2001gravitational,korol2017prospects,karnesis2021characterization}. Below a
few millihertz their signals overlap: the instrument's orbit and the source's own chirp spread each
signal over several frequency bins, neighbours share bins, and their parameters must be inferred
jointly (\cref{fig:related-crowding}).

The reference method is the global fit, a trans-dimensional \ac{mcmc} over every resolvable
source \citep{littenberg2020global,littenberg2023prototype,katz2024efficient,strub2024global,deng2025modular}.
It is the standard for what the data support, but a joint chain over one of our frequency
windows alone takes \qtyrange{\McmcCostJointLo}{\McmcCostJointHi}{\hour}, and the whole fit runs
again on every new stretch of data. \Ac{sbi} learns the posterior once and then returns
it in a fraction of a second. For \acp{gb} it has so far treated one source, two ordered sources
in a narrow band, or a factorised set of toy signals
\citep{korsakova2024neural,houba2025slotflow,delmond2026neural} (\cref{tab:related-closest}).

We bring \acs{sbi} to the global fit's unit of work: the joint posterior of the $S$
sources of a frequency window, $8S$ parameters under the full instrument response, for windows
anywhere in the band. A conditional flow-matching model
\citep{lipman2023flow,liu2023flow,albergo2023building,dax2023flow} draws it with no likelihood
evaluation.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Joint posteriors of overlapping \acp{gb} from the flow alone}, with $S$ from two to
    sixteen sources per window under broad catalogue priors, for windows anywhere in the band
    from \qtyrange{\PriorsFullLowMhz}{\PriorsFullHighMhz}{\milli\hertz}
    (\cref{sec:gw-slicing,sec:narrow-band}). Runs with increasing $S$ measure how the joint
    posterior scales.
  \item \emph{Coordinates without the posterior's symmetries.} A flat chart of eight coordinates
    per source folds each source's equivalent copies into one mode, and slots ordered in frequency
    remove the labelling modes (\cref{sec:method-chart}).
  \item \emph{A one-way multi-stream transformer} that encodes the data once per window, so every
    draw and every sampler step reuses it (\cref{sec:method-network}).
  \item \emph{Subbands of fixed shapes.} Dividing the band where the width of the sources'
    responses doubles gives five subproblems, each trained on its own device (\cref{sec:narrow-band}).
  \item \emph{Validation on known answers.} A toy with an exact posterior shows that ranks of
    data-space quantities catch phase, sky and dependence errors that parameter ranks miss, and
    mock posteriors are run through the whole evaluation (\cref{sec:validation}).
\end{itemize}
