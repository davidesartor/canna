Gravitational waves have opened a new window on the Universe: ground-based interferometers observe
the mergers of compact binaries \citep{abbott2016observation}, and pulsar timing arrays have found
evidence for a nanohertz background \citep{agazie2023nanograv,antoniadis2023second}. The space-based
\ac{lisa}, due for launch in 2035, will open the millihertz band, home to massive black-hole
binaries, extreme mass-ratio inspirals and the compact binaries of our own Galaxy
\citep{amaro2017lisa,colpi2024lisa}. It will observe millions of \acp{gb} in the Milky Way, quasi-monochromatic sources
between \qtyrange{\RelatedWorkFzeroLow}{\RelatedWorkFzeroHigh}{\milli\hertz}, and resolve thousands of
them \citep{nelemans2001gravitational,korol2017prospects,karnesis2021characterization}. Below a few millihertz
their signals overlap: a source spreads over several frequency bins through the instrument's
orbit and its own chirp, and neighbours share bins, so their parameters must be inferred jointly
(\cref{fig:related-crowding}). The reference method is a global fit, a trans-dimensional \ac{mcmc}
over every resolvable source \citep{littenberg2020global,littenberg2023prototype,katz2024efficient,strub2024global,deng2025modular}.
It is the standard for what the data support, but even a joint chain over one of our windows takes
\qtyrange{\McmcCostJointLo}{\McmcCostJointHi}{\hour}, and it has to run again on every new stretch
of data. Amortised estimators learn the posterior once and
then return it in a fraction of a second, but for \acp{gb} they have so far treated one source,
two ordered sources in a narrow band, or a factorised set of toy signals
\citep{korsakova2024neural,houba2025slotflow,delmond2026neural} (\cref{tab:related-closest}).

We bring the amortised route to the global fit's unit of work: the joint posterior of the
$S$ sources in a frequency window, $8S$ parameters under
the full instrument response, for windows anywhere in the band, drawn by conditional flow
matching \citep{lipman2023flow,liu2023flow,albergo2023building,dax2023flow} with no likelihood
evaluation. The difficulties are three. The number of parameters is very large: eight per source,
for thousands of resolvable sources, inferred jointly wherever their signals overlap. The exact
number of sources is unknown: a window holds sources above and below the noise, and which of them
the data resolve is part of the answer. And reference posteriors are lacking: a joint chain exists
only for a few windows, and without one the usual calibration checks pass flows that are plainly
wrong.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Joint posteriors for thousands of \acp{gb} at once, under broad catalogue priors rather than
    narrow boxes around each source: one network, run over every window of a band in parallel, returns
    each window's joint posterior, and their product is the band's (\cref{sec:gw-slicing}). It is a
    first proof of concept of an amortised approach that scales to the full global fit.
  \item Broad frequency bands: one network, conditioned on the window's centre, serves the whole band
    from \qtyrange{\PriorsFullLowMhz}{\PriorsFullHighMhz}{\milli\hertz}, where the responses widen with
    frequency (\cref{sec:gw-windows,sec:narrow-band}).
  \item A way to handle an unknown number of sources: a fixed number of slots per window, most of them
    below the noise, for which the flow returns the prior when the data do not resolve them; runs with
    2 to 16 sources per window measure how the joint posterior scales (\cref{sec:gw-rates}).
  \item A flow-matching recipe: a chart in which each source's four equivalent copies become one mode,
    slots in order of frequency, each with a Gaussian frame read off the data, so the flow transports
    order-one widths for loud and quiet sources alike, and a one-way transformer that encodes the data
    once per window (\cref{sec:method}).
  \item Bayesian validation checks tested on known answers: on a toy with an exact posterior, ranks of
    data-space quantities catch phase, sky and dependence errors that parameter ranks miss, and mock
    posteriors are run through the whole evaluation (\cref{sec:validation}).
\end{itemize}
